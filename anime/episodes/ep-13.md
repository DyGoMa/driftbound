## SCENE 1 — EXT. THE GULLWAKE, DECK — INSIDE THE GAP — CONTINUOUS (≈ 1:10)

Black. A young man's voice, fast, trying to sound in charge of it.

KAITO (V.O.): Previously. Okay. So. A woman the size of a door harpooned our ship, which you can't DO, and then her and Sela had a swordfight, and they were flirting, the whole time, with swords, and nobody else thought that was weird. Then she put a cutter on Sela's mark and started COUNTING, so I grabbed it, and now my arm doesn't work, which is fine. It's fine. Then one island stopped and the other one didn't, so the Gap's shutting, and the tow's on the bottom, and Bram said we've got about an egg, and Juno didn't have a number, which has never happened, so that was great. Then Sela got up on the rail, and I asked her what it costs, and she said— (a beat) She said she'd let me know. If she remembered. Then she jumped. (a beat) That's where we are. That's... yeah.

Fade up on the rail. Empty. One wet handprint on the wood, drying.

The deck in shadow; the rock walls lean so close that the sky is a crooked thread. Grit ticks down. Kaito (21, white streak plastered flat, red jacket tied at his waist) stands where she stepped off, the brass spyglass in his left hand, his right arm hanging dead, a fresh forked scar red past the elbow. Below him, in black water, a line of teal lights goes down: Vesh's lures, smaller, smaller.

Juno (23, copper braid, bomber jacket, gloves) beside him. Bram (31, huge, bearded, shirtless under his dungarees) holds an oilskin bundle to his chest with both hands. Skarn flat, ears back. Grist, awake for once, horn jammed under the torn deck.

Nobody speaks.

JUNO: (quiet) Still see them?
KAITO: (spyglass up, one-handed, not steady) Yeah.
JUNO: Now?
KAITO: ...Yeah.

SFX: rock on rock, a long slow grind, somewhere above.

KAITO: Now I don't.

He lowers the glass.

KAITO: How long can she—
BRAM: Long as Vesh can.
KAITO: How long's that?
BRAM: Longer than you.

Then:

BRAM: Stew's on.

## SCENE 2 — EXT. DECK — CONTINUOUS (≈ 1:45)

The walls grind; the thread of sky is thinner. Kaito tries to tie off a line with his right hand. The rope slides through his fingers. He tries again. Juno takes it off him and ties it.

KAITO: I can do it.
JUNO: You can watch me do it. That's a job.
KAITO: It's not a—
JUNO: Sweetheart. Your hand's a glove with nothing in it. Go and sit on it.

He sits. Tells the hand to close. It doesn't.

KAITO: Bram. How long does it stay like this?
BRAM: Last time was two days.
KAITO: This is further up than last time.
BRAM: Then longer.
KAITO: That's not an answer.
BRAM: It's what I've got. Galley Rule Thirty-Three. Nobody asks the cook for odds.
KAITO: That's Juno's job.
BRAM: She's not getting one either.

Bram sets the oilskin bundle in a bucket, lid on, wedges it under the galley step, pot lid on top. Checks it twice.

KAITO: She just handed it to you. Like you'd hold her coat.
BRAM: She's given it me before.
KAITO: How many times?

Bram looks at the bucket. Picks up his ladle.

BRAM: Stew wants stirring.
KAITO: It's STEW. It doesn't WANT anything.
BRAM: (going below) Everything wants something.

Kaito looks at Juno.

KAITO: He does that. I ask about her and he cooks.
JUNO: Everybody's got a tell. His is soup.
KAITO: What's mine?
JUNO: You shout at furniture.
KAITO: That's not a tell, that's—
JUNO: It's a tell, sweetheart. It tells me where the furniture is.

Juno knots a scarf from the mast ring to her own ankle. Tugs it. Takes the chalk off its string.

JUNO: Right. Book's open. Odds the captain comes up.
KAITO: Don't.
JUNO: I'm not asking you to take it. I'm telling you I've got one. Back there I didn't have a number and you saw my face. I'm not doing that face twice in one day. (she writes) Evens.
KAITO: Evens? She's—
JUNO: Evens is the best price I have ever given anything in my life. Say thank you.
KAITO: (small) ...Thank you.

SFX: a crack like a tree going over. A boulder drops past the stern; the sea jumps.

Skarn whines, once. Kaito's good hand finds the wolf's neck.

KAITO: When it comes back. The arm. Does it come all the way back?
JUNO: Did it last time?
KAITO: Mostly.
JUNO: Then mostly's your number. (beat) Go on. You've got another one in you.
KAITO: Hekka said it. In the fight. "You go deep, you pay in what you know." That's what she meant. "If I remember."
JUNO: (not looking up) Ask her.
KAITO: You know.
JUNO: I know what I've been told, and I know Bram's got a face about it, and that's enough for me to leave it where it is. (beat) Ask her after. She'll tell you, sweetheart. She writes everything down. It'll be in the book.
KAITO: And if it's not in the book?
JUNO: Then it didn't happen. That's how books work.
KAITO: That's horrible.
JUNO: It's a book, sweetheart. It doesn't care.

The shadow has swallowed the deck; the sky is a line. Kaito goes to the rail and looks down into black water. The spyglass is the only bright thing left.

KAITO: (to the water, low) Come on. Come on, come on.

## SCENE 3 — EXT. UNDER THE GAP — THE DIVE (≈ 2:45)

[No dialogue. Low strings, one held note, water. The animation is the point.]

Green going to black. Vesh descends in a long spiral, lures lit down her back. Sela (28, ponytail streaming) rides at the base of the skull, both hands flat on the scales, eyes open. Her whirlpool mark glows steady teal. A ring of air leaves her mouth and she lets it go.

They pass a drowned island: a town still on its hill, a bell tower leaning, all of it slanted on the sea floor. On one wall, under weed, a painted chart. Fish in the doorways. The lures light it and leave it dark.

Deeper. An older one: a harbour wall, iron rings, stone steps going down past the light. Blood threads from Sela's nose; she wipes it with her thumb and doesn't look.

Deeper. The lures are the only light in the world.

Then:

SFX: a very low hum, more felt than heard, coming up from below. Three small wavy lines of it.

Sela's head lifts. Eyes wide, then half-closed: the way she listens at the wheel. Her lips move, a note, three notes, the way you join a song a bar late. She almost has it.

Almost.

Vesh stops. Hangs. The lures throw their light down.

Below, the light finds something. A curve. It runs left past the light and right past the light and down past the light. Ridged, like a hull, like a hillside, like nothing anyone has a word for. Not rock: very slowly, it rises by the width of a hand, and settles.

Breath.

[Hold. Vesh and Sela a spark against it. Then her face, lit teal. Not fear. Nearer to recognising someone across a room without knowing where from.]

She lays her forehead on Vesh's scales. Shuts her eyes.

Her mark goes white.

SMASH CUT, fast, bright, silent: flashes, each half a second.

A pier in hard sun. A woman of seventy with hands like oars and a grey plait, laughing with all her teeth.

A girl of nineteen in the water below the pier, furious, spitting, the old woman's shadow over her.

A kitchen. A pot. The old woman's hand over a younger hand on a knife. Pepper, far too much.

The pier at dusk. The two of them, feet in the water, saying nothing.

Each flash shorter than the last. Each one, when it ends, does not fade. It goes out, like a lure.

Black.

Then every lure on Vesh's back lights at once, and Vesh turns upward, and the whole sea turns with her.

[EYECATCH: Black water. A small round Pal with a single lantern lure on a stalk swims very seriously beside one of Vesh's lures, matching its glow exactly. Vesh's eye, the size of a door, regards it.]

## SCENE 4 — EXT. DECK — CONTINUOUS (≈ 1:15)

The walls meet overhead with a sound like a church door shutting. Full dark. One lantern.

JUNO: That's it. That's shut. Oh, that is shut—
BRAM: Hold on to something.
KAITO: What's the sea DOING—

Under the hull the black water lights teal, from far down, coming fast: one lure, ten, a hundred. The water swells, a hill of sea rising under the ship.

SFX: a roar from below, climbing.

The Gullwake lifts, flat, on top of a dome of ocean that climbs the rock on both sides. Grist slides; Bram catches him by the horn. Both rails scrape stone, and the ship rises toward the one crack where the walls haven't quite met, a slot of grey.

BRAM: GRIST! DOWN!
KAITO: (left hand on the wheel, all his weight) WHICH WAY—
BRAM: (iron to the shoulders, holding the split deck shut) DOESN'T MATTER! SHE'S STEERING! YOU'RE JUST HOLDING IT!
JUNO: (lashed to the mast, screaming, delighted) EVENS! EVENS, YOU GLORIOUS—

Through the slot. Open water. The dome falls away in a long roar; the Gullwake is set down beyond the Gap like a toy on a table.

Behind them, the Gap shuts.

SFX: a boom that goes on for a long time.

Dripping. Kaito on his back, looking up.

KAITO: ...There's sky.
JUNO: (from under a fallen sail) Somebody owes me money. Where is she? Where's Vesh?
BRAM: (standing, slow) Not here.
KAITO: Did she just— did she lift the whole—
BRAM: She said she'd bring the sea up with her.
KAITO: I thought that was a FIGURE OF SPEECH.
BRAM: She doesn't do those.
JUNO: (fighting the sail) I'm not sorry I tied myself to the mast. I want that on the record.

At the rail: grey water. Then, a quarter mile off at the Gap's mouth, Hekka's cutter at anchor. Waiting, as promised.

KAITO: She's right there. If Vesh comes up anywhere near—
BRAM: (quiet) Watch.

## SCENE 5 — EXT. SEVER CUTTER, STERN — CONTINUOUS (≈ 1:20)

Hekka Dorn (33, 193 cm, wavy black hair swept back, grey plate, one glove off, that palm burned red) at the stern rail, the bear's chain in her good fist. The bear sits beside her, blank-eyed. A trooper (34, man, visor up, nervous) holds two tin mugs, filled.

TROOPER: Gap's shut, Captain. Nothing comes out of that.
HEKKA: I said I'd be at the end. I'm at the end.
TROOPER: You said pour two, ma'am. Why pour, if nothing comes out?
HEKKA: Manners.
TROOPER: Should I pour them back?
HEKKA: No.

She looks past him. Beyond the Gap, small in the mist: a battered ship, afloat. Her mouth moves at the corner.

HEKKA: She got the ship out.
TROOPER: Ma'am, how is that—
HEKKA: Under it. She went under it. (almost admiring) Mad cow.

Beneath the cutter, the water lights teal.

Hekka looks down. It gets bigger.

HEKKA: Huh.

SFX: KRRRAAAKK.

Vesh comes up through the cutter's hull. Planks go up like a thrown hand of cards. The stern drops, the trooper and both mugs go one way, and Hekka goes over the rail into the sea. The bear's chain snaps taut.

Vesh's head rises out of the wreck, lures blazing, frills spread. Standing between the frills, soaked and entirely upright: Sela Vey.

The bear braces and backs up the sloping deck; the chain hauls in. Hekka comes out of the sea on the end of it, coughing, and gets a boot on the planks.

She sits on the tilted deck. Looks at the hole where her ship had a middle. Looks up at Sela.

Not angry. Nothing like angry.

HEKKA: Captain Vey.
SELA: (wringing her ponytail out in both hands) Captain Dorn.
HEKKA: You're meant to come out the other end.
SELA: I came out the end you were at. I thought it was polite.
HEKKA: It was. That's the trouble with you.

A tin mug floats past her boot. She fishes it out: a mouthful left. She lifts it.

HEKKA: Said I'd buy you a drink if you lived.
SELA: I'll collect.
HEKKA: Noted.
SELA: You're very calm for a woman with half a boat.
HEKKA: I've got the half with the bear on it.

She drinks. Then holds her bare burned hand out to the bear.

HEKKA: Come on. Heel.

The bear doesn't heel; it doesn't know the word. It lets her take the chain. Sela sees the burned hand on the links and says nothing.

TROOPER: (hauling himself onto a hatch) Captain. It— it pulled you out.
HEKKA: It pulled the chain. The chain was on me. (flat) Don't write that down as anything.
TROOPER: No, ma'am.

Sela's eyes drop, one second, to the dull ring scar on Hekka's side, then back to her face.

SELA: Your lance?
HEKKA: Bottom. With your tow.
SELA: Your ship's sinking.
HEKKA: Noticed.
SELA: We've a stew on.
HEKKA: (a real pause) ...No. (she stands) Next time I'll bring the lance back, and we'll see about seven.
SELA: Looking forward to it.
HEKKA: I know you are. Go on. Before I remember what I'm for.

Vesh sinks away, Sela riding her. The cutter's halves settle. Troopers in the water.

TROOPER: Do we pursue, Captain?
HEKKA: In what?
TROOPER: ...Noted, ma'am.
HEKKA: Don't.

## SCENE 6 — EXT. THE GULLWAKE, DECK — MINUTES LATER (≈ 1:50)

Vesh's head at the rail. Sela steps down onto the deck, dripping, and the first thing she does is laugh. Not a tease: the laugh of someone back from a very long way. Hands on her knees.

The crew stare.

SELA: (through it) Oh, your faces. You should see your— Juno. What were the odds.
JUNO: (hoarse) Evens.
SELA: Evens! Pup, write that down, she gave me evens—
KAITO: (not moving) You said you'd— you were down there for—
SELA: Was I? (she straightens, wipes her eyes with the back of her wrist) Felt like a minute. How long?
BRAM: Long enough the egg's hard.
SELA: Then I hope you made two.
BRAM: Made four.
JUNO: Eight to one he ate two.
BRAM: Three.

She laughs again. Kaito doesn't. He crosses the deck and holds out the spyglass, left-handed.

KAITO: Right way up. The whole time.
SELA: (takes it; softer) Good lad.
KAITO: You said don't look through the wrong end. I didn't look through either end. I just held it.
SELA: That's what it was for.

She sees his right arm. Lifts it by the wrist. It hangs.

SELA: Hekka?
KAITO: Me. Grabbing her stick.
SELA: Mm. That I remember. (she sets the arm back down, careful) Never do that again.
KAITO: You said that already.
SELA: Then it bears repeating.
KAITO: What's down there?
SELA: Dark. Old towns. Steps. And— (she stops; tilts her head, as if for a sound that isn't there any more) ...And I'll tell you when I've written it down.

Juno, chalk in hand, pretending her eyes aren't wet.

JUNO: Captain, you're dripping on my book.
SELA: Your book was in the way of my sea.
JUNO: Everything's in the way of your sea. (writing) New line. The captain and the Sever captain. Twenty to one.
SELA: Short.
JUNO: You rammed her boat and she offered you a drink.
SELA: ...Fifteen, then.
JUNO: And Kaito and the sea's still five to two.
KAITO: I didn't GO IN this time!
JUNO: You went through it. Counts.

Kaito's ears go red. Skarn, by the mast, shuts his eyes as if in pain.

Bram comes up with a bowl of stew in each hand. Steam.

BRAM: Rule Thirty-Four. Captain eats first after a dive.
SELA: That's been a rule for years.
BRAM: Been a rule. Never been written.

He gives her the first bowl. She breathes it in with her eyes closed, and the smile goes soft and private.

SELA: Oh, that's— (eyes open) Bram. Who taught me to make that stew?

Bram stops.

BRAM: You taught me, Captain.
SELA: I know I taught you. Who taught me?
BRAM: ...You never said.
SELA: (easy, to Juno) Did I ever tell you?
JUNO: Sweetheart, I've been here a fortnight.
SELA: Pup?
KAITO: (very quiet) No.
SELA: Well. Somebody did. I don't put that much pepper in anything by nature.

A beat. Sela shrugs, tiny and real: a woman who has reached for a cup that isn't where she left it, and decided it doesn't matter.

SELA: It'll be in the book. Everything's in the book. (she eats; eyes close again) God, that's good.
BRAM: You say that every time.

He bends to the bucket. Pot lid. Bucket lid. Oilskin. The logbook, dry. He holds it out in both hands, exactly the way he took it.

She tucks the bowl into one arm and takes the book, and for a second she just holds it, the laugh still on her face. She has no idea.

SELA: Right. Somebody tell me what I missed. Slowly. I'll write it down.
JUNO: Start with "Juno was right."
SELA: I'll start with the sea. You're after Grist.
JUNO: Grist slept through it!
SELA: Then it's a short entry.

Along the hull, Vesh's lures dim one at a time, the way a house goes dark for the night.

MUSIC: nothing.

## SCENE 7 — INT. CHART ROOM — NIGHT (≈ 1:20)

Lamp. Sela at the chart table in a dry shirt, hair down, the logbook open. The day's entry in her neat hand:

INSERT: "Dived under the Gap. Came up through Dorn's cutter; she owes me a drink. The pup held my glass right way up the whole time. Stew was good. Ask Bram who taught me the stew."

She turns back a page for room. Another, idly. Stops. Reads.

Kaito in the doorway, right arm in a flour-sack sling. He came to ask something. He sees her face and doesn't.

Her face: nothing. Not teasing, not tired. A woman reading. Her lips move over the words the way they did the night of the eel pie: learning a part.

INSERT: the page. Her own handwriting, steady, a little younger. "Mag Tolliver. Sorrow Point. Seventy, hands like oars. Threw me in forty-one times before I felt the two seas. Best year of my life. I won't need this one written down. Writing it anyway."

SELA: (mild, to the page) Forty-one times. (a small laugh) She sounds like a lot of fun. I'd have hated her.

Kaito's hand tightens on the doorframe.

KAITO: ...Who does?
SELA: (looks up; the lamp comes up in her face) Pup. You've got ink on your— no, you haven't. Habit. (she taps the page) Mag Tolliver. Says here she taught me to read the water. Says it took a year.
KAITO: You told me about her.
SELA: Did I?
KAITO: Yesterday. On the rail. You said— (he stops)
SELA: Then I told you right. It's a good page. (she closes the book on one finger, pleasant) What did you want?

Kaito looks at her a long moment. Her eyebrows lift: patient, kind.

KAITO: Bram says the arm's two days. Maybe more.
SELA: Then you've two days of being right-handed with your left. Good practice. (she glances down the page) Says here Mag tied my good hand behind me for a month. Sensible woman.
KAITO: (a beat) Which way's the water going?

She tilts her head. Listens, the way she does. Smiles.

SELA: East on top. West underneath. (fond) Who taught you that?

He doesn't answer. The lamp. The sea. Her thumb rests on the closed book's torn edge without her knowing it.
