## SCENE 1 — EXT. THE GULLWAKE, DECK — NIGHT (≈ 1:20)

Black. A voice like warm oil with a crack in it.

SILAS (V.O.): Previously, darling. Lanternreach: nobody uses names, and everybody owes a man called Maro, which tells you how much good not using names does. A crew of very damp strangers took a ledger out of a bathhouse. I was there. I was magnificent. Ask any of them; they'll deny it. The ledger lists every smuggler who ever sold a partner to the Sever. Page one, in a very good hand: my name. At which point the boy tied me to the mast. With my own chain. Which is a conversation for another evening. (a beat) Oh, and two nights ago I let eleven monsters out of a Sever pen, for nobody, and the boy saw me do it. Honestly, I don't know what he's angry about.

Fade up. Lanternreach climbs its hill in stairs and lanterns; a thousand small yellow points come down over the black harbour. On the Gullwake's deck, Silas Moreau (24, white shirt open to the stomach, red sash, long dark-teal hair tied low at the neck) sits against the mast with his own chain-blade wound round him three times. He looks comfortable.

Kaito (21) stands over him holding the ledger open in both hands like a hymn book. Juno (23) sits on a barrel with chalk. Bram (31) fills the galley door, stirring. Sela (28) leans at the rail, logbook open, writing.

KAITO: Page one. "Moreau, S. Agent. Nine deliveries, paid." Nine.
SILAS: Ten. He never counted the duke's wife.
KAITO: You sold PARTNERS. To THEM.
SILAS: I sold crates, darling. I was very careful never to look in them.
KAITO: That's worse!
SILAS: Yes. I know. That's why I said it like that.

Skarn lies along the rail with his ears flat, watching Silas. On the yardarm above, Hush, the owl-lynx, sits perfectly still and looks down at Skarn with round flat eyes. Neither blinks.

JUNO: (chalking) Odds the whole story's a lie: three to one. Odds the lie's the part about not looking: evens.
SILAS: Sweetheart, you'd clean up either way.

Kaito drops into a crouch, face to face.

KAITO: Why's your name on page one?
SILAS: Alphabetical, I expect. (Kaito's glare) It isn't alphabetical. He puts his favourites first.

Sela closes the logbook on one finger and looks over.

SELA: Tell it. All of it.
SILAS: Which version, Captain?
KAITO: The TRUE one.
SILAS: (a small sigh) Oh. That one's shorter.

## SCENE 2 — INT. GALLEY, WITH FLASHBACK — NIGHT (≈ 2:30)

Bram's galley, lamplit and low. Silas is now chained to a chair, a bowl of stew steaming in front of him and no free hands. Bram stands over him with the ladle.

BRAM: Nobody eats tied up in my galley.
KAITO: He's not eating. He's a prisoner.
BRAM: Then he's not in my galley.

He unwinds the chain. Kaito opens his mouth; Sela, in the doorway, lifts one finger; he shuts it. Silas rolls his shoulders, flexes his wrists, and picks up the spoon as if nothing has happened. The shirt is doing none of its job.

JUNO: Button one. Any one. I'm not fussy.
SILAS: The buttons are decorative, sweetheart. So am I. (to Bram) You cut a man loose like you've done it before.
BRAM: Nets. Mostly fish.
SILAS: Mostly.

Bram goes faintly pink above the beard and turns back to his pot. Silas eats. The laugh goes out of his face by degrees, the way a lamp goes down.

SILAS: I was twenty. I had a boat with a hole in it and a mouth without one.

CUT TO:

FLASHBACK. The same lamplight, colder. A counting house: tall desks, ledgers, lanterns on chains. Silas (20, the same face, shirt buttoned to the collar, which is the strangest thing in the shot) stands before a desk. Behind it, small and soft-voiced in a brown coat: Ledger Maro (52, 172 cm, round spectacles, a pencil behind one ear; he looks like a man who sells seed).

MARO (V.O.): You'll carry a crate from the Hook Pier to the grey cutter at the north wall. You will not open it.
SILAS (V.O.): He paid in advance. Nobody pays in advance. I thought that was kindness.

Flashback: Silas carries a crate down a lamp-lit stair at night. The crate shifts. Something inside breathes. He stops. He does not open it. He keeps walking.

SILAS (V.O.): The second one scratched. The fourth one cried. By the ninth I'd learned to hum. Something cheerful. You would be amazed what a man can carry if he hums.

Flashback: Maro at his desk, writing one line. Silas, a little older, shirt open now, counting coins without looking at him. In the lamplight the coins look very small.

SILAS (V.O.): He never once said the word. Nobody in Lanternreach does. It's a port of lamps, darling. You'd think somebody would see.

BACK TO: the galley. Silas's spoon has stopped.

JUNO: Nine crates. What did you tell yourself?
SILAS: That I was a courier. Couriers don't ask. It's in the word.
KAITO: It's not in the word.
SILAS: It's in the way I say the word.
KAITO: And Hush? You sell him too?
SILAS: Hush found me. Not the other way round. He has terrible judgement.

In the galley skylight, Hush's flat face appears upside down, listening. Skarn's face appears beside it, the right way up, unimpressed.

SELA: And two nights ago.
SILAS: Two nights ago I'd stolen your copper to pay him a slice of what I owe, and he had me walk it to the north wall myself. Eleven cages on the dock. (beat) One of them looked at me.

Silence. Bram's ladle doesn't move.

SILAS: So I opened them. All eleven. I'd like to say I thought about it.
JUNO: (quiet, no odds) Why don't you?
SILAS: Because then I'd have to say what I thought.

He eats again. Brisk, elegant, done.

SILAS: Now I owe a very patient man eleven partners at Sever price. He'll come for it, he'll be polite, and I would rather not be on this boat when he does. Thank you for the stew. It's sultry.
BRAM: New galley rule.
SILAS: Already?
BRAM: Nobody calls the stew that.
SILAS: I'll find another word.
BRAM: Don't.

Kaito, arms folded, has not sat down.

KAITO: I don't believe a word of it.
SILAS: Which word?
KAITO: "Looked."

Silas looks at him for the first time without a smile. Then the smile comes back, and it fits a little worse than before.

SILAS: No. I didn't think you would.

## SCENE 3 — EXT. THE GULLWAKE AND THE QUAY — DAWN (≈ 1:40)

Grey morning. The lamps of Lanternreach still burn under a pale sky; nobody here puts them out. One figure comes down the quay under a black umbrella it isn't raining for. Ledger Maro. Alone. A small leather case.

Kaito at the rail, Skarn's hackles up beside him. Juno behind a barrel, gloved hand already warm. Bram at the gangplank, where Grist sleeps across it like a dropped portcullis.

MARO: Good morning. I won't come aboard. I never do.

Silas stands on the quay below the rail, hands in his sash, hair wet from a bucket. He doesn't bow. Maro does, very slightly.

MARO: Mr. Moreau.
SILAS: Names, Maro. In Lanternreach? People will talk.
MARO: Nobody here uses names because I have them all. (opening the case) You're looking well. Four thousand one hundred and eighty, by my count. Eleven at the north-wall price. I've waived the copper. It was good copper.

He takes out a single sheet of paper and holds it out. Silas takes it and reads. The crew watch from the rail. Sela, at the stern, has her spyglass on Maro's mouth.

JUNO: (hissed) What's he saying?
SELA: Numbers.
JUNO: What kind?
SELA: The kind with people in.
BRAM: Can you read him?
SELA: His lips barely move. Men like that don't need them to.

On the quay, Maro's soft voice. We hear this part.

MARO: Return my book. Bring the five accounts aboard that vessel to the Oil Store by second bell. Partners included; I'm not interested in the people. Your line comes off page one. Their copper goes back on their deck. Everyone leaves Lanternreach with what they came for.
SILAS: Except them.
MARO: They came for a cargo. I'll give them one.

Maro waits. Patient. A man who has never once needed to raise his voice.

Silas folds the paper. He looks at the Gullwake: Bram on the gangplank, Kaito glaring, Juno's hand, Sela's spyglass, Hush on the yardarm. He looks for a long time, for him.

SILAS: Done. One condition.
MARO: I don't take conditions. I take payment.
SILAS: Keep every lamp lit. In the Store. I like to see who I'm dealing with.
MARO: (a small nod, almost fond) I always do.

He turns and walks back up the quay under the umbrella. He has not looked at the ship once. Silas stands with the folded paper. Then he climbs the gangplank, steps over Grist, and when he reaches the deck he is smiling like the first night they met him.

SILAS: Good news, darlings. He wants his book back more than he wants me dead, which I'll admit stings. The book for your copper. Tonight. Oil Store, second bell. All of us.
KAITO: All of us.
SILAS: He wants to look at what he isn't buying. He's a collector. It's a sickness.
KAITO: Screw that. It's a trap.
SILAS: Of course it's a trap, darling. It's a warehouse in Lanternreach. The question is whose.

Sela snaps the spyglass shut. She looks at Silas for a long moment, exactly as long as he looked at the ship.

SELA: We go.
KAITO: Sela— he just stood down there and AGREED to something. We all watched him agree.
SELA: I did. I also watched him look back at this ship first. Took him a while.
KAITO: So?
SELA: So I'm curious what he was looking at.
KAITO: It's a TRAP.
SELA: Pup. The copper's in that room. We don't leave this port without it, and I'd sooner leave with it than swim out after it. (to Silas) Partners come. Vesh stays in the harbour. If we're not back by third bell, she eats the town.
SILAS: (lightly) Starting with?
SELA: Whoever lied to me last.

She writes. Silas watches her write, and for once doesn't ask what.

## SCENE 4 — EXT. THE GULLWAKE, DECK — DUSK (≈ 1:15)

The town's lamps coming up one by one in the blue. The crew gear up, quietly. Juno at the mast with chalk, under the harpoon hole from Gaptooth. She writes: SILAS SELLS US — 2 TO 1.

Silas comes past, takes the chalk out of her glove, rubs out the 2, writes EVENS.

SILAS: I know me.
JUNO: That's not how odds work, sweetheart. You don't set your own.
SILAS: Darling, I have never once in my life done anything else.

Beneath KAITO AND WHO?, he adds SILAS — 3 TO 1. Kaito, lacing his boot, sees it.

KAITO: TAKE THAT OFF.
SILAS: Three's generous. You blushed at the stew.
KAITO: Stop flirting with my crew!
SILAS: Your crew?
SELA: (passing, not stopping) Pup. You're the deckhand.
SILAS: Oh. A deckhand.

Kaito stands up fast, straight into the boom.

SFX: DONK
KAITO: (holding his forehead) I knew that was there.

Skarn puts his head down on his paws. Disgust. Hush, above, watches Skarn do it and then does it too, exactly.

Bram comes out of the galley with a cloth-wrapped pie and puts it into Silas's hands.

BRAM: Eat before. People who eat before don't do stupid things.
SILAS: Is that true?
BRAM: No. But they're not hungry while they do them.

Silas looks at the pie. Something crosses his face and is gone.

SILAS: Bram, darling. If I'm ever hanged, I want you doing the catering.
BRAM: (misty, gruff) Don't be hanged.

Sela at the stern, logbook open; Vesh's lures glow faint under the black water beside the hull. Silas stops near her. Not too near.

SILAS: You haven't asked me why.
SELA: Why what?
SILAS: The eleven.
SELA: You said one of them looked at you.
SILAS: And you believe that?
SELA: (writing) I believe you said it.

She closes the book. Then, almost kind:

SELA: Ask me after the Store.

Grist is asleep across the gangplank again. One by one, the whole crew climbs over him. Kaito last, with Skarn, who clears him with insulting ease.

SELA (V.O.): (writing) Taking the crew into a room owned by a man who prices people, on the word of a man who sells them. Pup says this is stupid. Pup is right. Going anyway. It's where the copper is, and I want to see his face when he does it.

## SCENE 5 — INT. THE OIL STORE — NIGHT (≈ 3:00)

A long high warehouse at the top of the lamp-stairs, far from the water. Hundreds of lanterns hang on chains at every height, so the air itself seems lit; between them, barrels stacked three high, stencilled LAMP OIL. At the far end, a clerk's desk on a raised floor. Behind it, Ledger Maro, pencil behind his ear. The Gullwake's copper sits beside the desk in neat crates. A gallery runs round the walls. Empty.

The crew come in through the great doors. Silas first, hands in his sash. Kaito and Skarn. Juno, Ashka on her shoulder. Bram, with Grist clanking behind him and filling the doorway. Sela last.

MARO: Thank you for being punctual. Nobody is, here. Lamps make people think it's always evening.

Silas walks the length of the room and lays the ledger on the desk. Maro opens it to page one. Takes the pencil from behind his ear. Draws one neat line through MOREAU, S.

MARO: Closed.

SFX: the great doors swing shut behind the crew. A bar drops.

On the gallery, all the way round, collectors step out from behind the barrels: men and women in brown coats (adults, thirties and forties), each with a hooked white blade and a coil of cable. Twenty of them. Nobody shouts. Nobody runs. Lanternreach doesn't.

Juno's hand comes up, glove already smoking.

MARO: Please don't. There are four hundred barrels in this room. I'd lose the copper.

Juno's hand stops. The smoke thins. Ashka, on her shoulder, puts her head under a wing.

MARO: And the young man's lightning will go to the nearest metal. Which is the lamps. Which are over the oil. I did think about this.
SELA: You've done this before.
MARO: Every second bell for thirty years, Captain. I am very good at inventory.

Kaito's gauntlet sparks anyway. Skarn's fin crackles. A cable whips down from the gallery and takes Skarn round the muzzle, another round the forelegs; three collectors haul and the wolf goes down snarling. Kaito lunges, and a hooked blade is at his neck from behind, very gently.

Grist charges, and the floor in front of him is not floor: a cable net lifts out of the sawdust and takes six iron legs at once. The beetle goes over like a dropped stove.

SFX: KRRANNNG

Bram sets his feet. Grey iron climbs his arms. Four cutters come to his chest, white and humming. He stops. Not for the cutters. For Grist's eye, looking up at him through the net.

SELA: Silas.
SILAS: Captain.

He hasn't turned round. He's watching Maro write out a receipt.

JUNO: You bastard. I had you at evens.
SILAS: You should have taken the other side, sweetheart. I did tell you I know me.
KAITO: You're SELLING us.
SILAS: Yes.
JUNO: Odds you choke on it.
SILAS: Oh, sweetheart. Long.

He turns now. Smiling, open-shirted, utterly himself. Nothing in his face for anyone to hold onto.

SILAS: I told you what I was the first night. I said it in a very good voice and you all heard a joke. That's the whole trick of this town, darling. Nobody uses names, and nobody ever looks in the crate.

Maro, pricing as he writes. Soft. Polite.

MARO: Storm wolf, four hundred. The raptor, ninety; she's small. Iron beetle, six hundred for the plating alone. The serpent in the harbour I'll bill the north wall for a barge. (looking up over his spectacles) Captain Vey, I don't price captains. The Director does that himself.
SELA: Does he.
MARO: He's read your name. In another man's book.

Sela says nothing. Her face says nothing. Her thumb rests on the logbook at her hip.

BRAM: Silas.

One word. Silas doesn't look at him. He is the only person in the room Silas doesn't look at.

Maro tears off the receipt and holds it out. Silas takes it, reads it, folds it, and tucks it inside the shirt where a button should be.

MARO: Paid in full, Mr. Moreau. Your account is closed.
SILAS: Darling, I've never been closed in my life.

He walks back down the length of the room. Past Bram's iron arms. Past Juno. Past Skarn on the floor, who tries to bite him through the cable. Past Kaito, who strains against the blade at his throat.

KAITO: SILAS!
SILAS: (not turning, almost gentle) Don't shout. Not in here. The lamps don't like it.

A collector unbars a small side door for him. Lamplight in the alley beyond. He steps into it.

KAITO: I KNEW it! I KNEW IT! The first night, I KNEW—

The door shuts on the rest.

## SCENE 6 — EXT. THE LAMP STAIRS — NIGHT (≈ 1:00)

A narrow stair down the hill, a lantern every six steps, the harbour black below with one faint teal line in it where Vesh waits. Silas walks down alone. Behind and above him, muffled through the Oil Store's wall:

KAITO: (O.S., faint) —KNEW IT—

Silas stops on the stair. He takes the receipt out of his shirt and reads it under a lamp. PAID IN FULL, in a very good hand. He puts it back.

No music.

He lays his hand flat against his throat, fingers over the three feather lines on his neck. They are glowing, faintly, violet.

A shape lands on the roofline above him without any sound at all. Hush. Round flat eyes. Waiting.

SILAS: (very quiet, to the roof) Last words, darling. Make a note of them.

He breathes in. Long. The feathers on his neck flare.

He breathes out, slow, and keeps breathing out, longer than a breath should go.

SFX: nothing. Not even the harbour.

The lantern beside him dims. Then the one above it. Then the next, up the stair toward the Oil Store, one by one, like a hand closing.

Black.
