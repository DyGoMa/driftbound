## SCENE 1 — EXT. LANTERNREACH, A DEAD-END ALLEY — NIGHT (≈ 1:15)

Black screen. A voice like silk dragged over gravel: warm, amused, a faint rasp on the long words.

SILAS (V.O.): Previously, on a programme I have not been in. A boy with a wolf wants to draw a map. A captain with a serpent forgets her supper. A gambler who won't take her gloves off, a cook who won't put a shirt on. Somebody enormous in armour stabbed their boat, the captain went for a swim and came back lighter, and there was, I'm told, dancing. I wasn't there. I'm told a great many things, darling, and I believe about half, which is more than this town does.

Fade up. Lanternreach: a port climbing a black cliff in tiers, every tier hung with thousands of lamps. No signs. Over each door, a chalked number.

SFX: lamps creaking on chains; water; somewhere a bell, counting

A dead end behind a lamp-chandler's. Silas Moreau (24, man, 183 cm, pale, violet eyes, a beauty mark under the left one, long dark-teal hair tied low; white shirt open to the stomach, red sash, a coiled chain-blade at his hip) leans on the wall as though he picked it. Three knives. Three people.

A docker (man, forties, arms like hawsers). A fence (man, sixties, tiny spectacles). A knife-woman (woman, thirties, cropped hair, a lamp-lighter's leather coat), the only steady hand of the three.

DOCKER: Eighteen silver.
FENCE: Forty, and the brass lamp.
KNIFE-WOMAN: (nothing)
SILAS: Three creditors in one alley. I'm honoured. Usually I have to walk to each of you.
SILAS: (to the docker) Eighteen. For that I'd stab me too. Here's something worth more: the harbourmaster's second pilot skims every Tuesday berth and writes it down as "rope." You've paid rope for a year, darling. Go and have a conversation about rope.

The docker lowers the knife, counting back Tuesdays. Then he's gone, cracking his knuckles.

SILAS: (to the fence) Forty and a lamp. The lamp's in your window. You bought it from me, I bought it from you; we're a small economy. Here. (a folded paper into his hand) Berth seven. Salvage ship, docking tonight. Crates on the quay till the harbourmaster's done with the captain. Gaptooth brass.
FENCE: (lips moving over it) ...And this clears us?
SILAS: This makes you forget my face. Go and practise.

The fence shuffles off, delighted. The knife-woman hasn't moved. Silas's smile changes: smaller, truer.

SILAS: Hello.
KNIFE-WOMAN: You said you'd write.
SILAS: I did write. Nine pages. I didn't send them, which I grant you is a technical point.
KNIFE-WOMAN: You left before the lamps came on.
SILAS: I had the Ledger to think of—

The knife goes into the wall beside his ear, all the way in. He doesn't flinch. He looks at it with something like admiration.

SILAS: You've been practising.
KNIFE-WOMAN: Nine pages.
SILAS: Nine. (beat) Ten, if you'll stand there a moment.

She pulls the knife out, sheathes it and walks away without a word. For one second his face is unguarded. Then the smile's back.

SILAS: (to nobody, strolling off) Everyone in this town wants to stab me. It's a kind of fame.

On a chain-walk above, a shape with owl wings and a cat's body steps off into the dark without a sound.

## SCENE 2 — EXT. BERTH SEVEN — NIGHT (≈ 1:15)

The Gullwake slides in under the cliff of lamps. Everyone at the rail, looking up.

JUNO: (low whistle) Sweetheart. Somebody's paying a lamp-oil bill.
BRAM: Smells like a thousand kitchens.
KAITO: Where are the signs? It's all numbers.
SELA: (writing as the hull kisses the quay) Lanternreach. Nobody here uses a name. You're what you owe.

A collector waits on the quay (man, forties, long coat, an account book on a strap, a lamp on a pole over his shoulder). Polite. Already writing.

COLLECTOR: Welcome to Lanternreach. What do you owe?
KAITO: Nothing.
COLLECTOR: (writes) Then you'll want to start. Harbour dues go to the Ledger. The harbourmaster's up at Forty-One; he'll need the captain. The Ledger will want to know who's aboard.
SELA: The Ledger can want. What's the number for "none of your business"?
COLLECTOR: (not a flicker) A large one, Captain. Most people can't afford it.

He hangs a chalked tag on the bow rail: 7. Goes.

Four crates swing down to the stones: green-black Gaptooth brass, a ship's bell, a binnacle with the sloop's name sanded off. Kaito and Bram stack them under a lamp. Grist settles beside them like a parked wagon and begins to snore.

KAITO: That's the mast, the sail, the planks Hekka's lot cut, and food for a month. That's everything.
SELA: That's four crates off a ship that lost. Don't give it a speech, pup.
JUNO: Odds it's still here when we get back?
BRAM: It's got Grist.
JUNO: Grist is asleep.
BRAM: Next to it. That's a lock.

Sela nods Bram and Kaito up the stairs with her.

SELA: Juno. Stay with the brass.
JUNO: (drifting toward a lit doorway) Absolutely. Right here. Not moving.

## SCENE 3 — EXT. BERTH SEVEN — LATER (≈ 0:50)

Sela, Kaito and Bram coming back down the stairs, a chalk 7 on Sela's palm. Kaito, ahead, stops.

The crates are gone. Grist is asleep in exactly the same place, guarding a square of empty stone. Beside him a lamp has been left burning, and under it, chalked in a beautiful hand:

INSERT: "Thank you, darling. Mind the step. —"

No name. Just the dash.

KAITO: ...No.
BRAM: (misty, to Grist) You were right there.
SFX: a snore
KAITO: NO. Screw this. Screw this whole town. Where's JUNO?

From the lit doorway, a whoop and a crash of coins.

JUNO: (O.S.) BRAM, I'VE DOUBLED THE FOOD MONEY— (beat) Why's everyone standing like that?

A lamp-seller (woman, sixties, apron full of wicks) trims a wick nearby, not looking up.

LAMP-SELLER: Tall one. Hair like kelp. Six of the Ledger's men and a cart. He never lifted a thing, love. He pointed.
KAITO: Who is he?
LAMP-SELLER: We don't do who. Who's what you give the Ledger. (she lifts a lamp to Kaito's furious face and inspects him like a wick) He hadn't done his buttons, if that helps.
JUNO: (arriving, pocketing coins) Oh, that helps enormously.
SELA: (writing) Thief. Male, tall, unbuttoned, polite. Took everything. Left a lamp.
KAITO: Why are you WRITING—
SELA: So I'll know I liked him.

## SCENE 4 — INT. THE FORTY — NIGHT (≈ 1:30)

A tavern with a 40 chalked on the door. Lamps hang so low that people duck. Cards, dice, a fiddle.

The crew in the doorway. Juno reads the room in one sweep.

JUNO: Six tables, two exits, one bar. Three to one he's at the bar.
SELA: Why?
JUNO: Nobody that polite sits with his back to a door.

At the bar: Silas, a glass of something gold, listening to a very old man's story with total attention.

Kaito crosses the room like a thrown knife, Skarn behind him. Both hands on the bar.

KAITO: You.
SILAS: (turning; takes him in, slowly, delighted) Oh, hello. Gaptooth brass. You've still got the salt on you. (to the barman) Whatever he's having, and I'll have him.
KAITO: WHAT?
SILAS: A drink. I'll have him a drink. Your ears have gone red. Is that a signal? What does it mean?
KAITO: It means I'm about to put you through that mirror.
SILAS: You'd be the fourth tonight, and the best-looking, which I say to very few people in alleys.

Kaito swings. Silas isn't where the swing goes; he's on the next stool, glass unspilt.

Bram arrives at Kaito's shoulder, shirtless under the dungarees, towel on. Silas's whole posture changes, like a cat finding a sunbeam.

SILAS: And who feeds you.
BRAM: (blinking) Me.
SILAS: Of course. The forearms. Nobody gets those lifting money. (raising his glass) I have been robbed, sir, of the chance to be robbed by you.
BRAM: (to Sela, gentle, baffled) He's doing it to me now.
SELA: He's doing it to you on purpose. The pup he does for free.
KAITO: Nobody is DOING anything. Where's our brass?

Silas looks at Sela at last. Two professionals.

SILAS: Captain. Did Forty-One give you a number?
SELA: (shows her palm) Seven.
SILAS: Lucky. Everyone here is a number, Captain. Mine's rather large, and the gentleman who holds it collects on new ships as harbour dues. Your brass is paying my account. I'd apologise, but you'd know I didn't mean it, and I like you already.
SELA: You stole four crates to pay a tab.
SILAS: Four crates, and I left you a lamp. I'm not an animal.
JUNO: (elbow on the bar, reading him) Five to one he's lying about which part.
SILAS: (eyes to her, pleased) Oh, you're good. You're the one who bets. Bet me.
JUNO: Evens you've been stabbed this week.
SILAS: This hour. (tilts his head: a fresh nick on his ear) Nine pages' worth.

Juno laughs, the big laugh.

KAITO: SELA. We're having a DRINK with him.
SELA: (writing) No. You are. I'm having one with the lamp. (to Silas) Where's our brass, darling?
SILAS: (a small real flinch at the word, covered instantly) That one's mine. You don't get that one.

Outside, a bell. Three counts. Everyone in the bar looks at the door, then at Silas, then away. He drains his glass.

SILAS: That's for me. (standing) Captain, a pleasure. Big man, write to me. (to Kaito, close, soft) And you. Hands up when you swing. You leave your whole face open.

Kaito grabs for his collar and gets a fistful of shirt; Silas is already gone, chain whispering up into a lamp-hook, over the crowd, out the back.

KAITO: SKARN!

## SCENE 5 — EXT. LANTERNREACH, CHAIN-WALKS — NIGHT (≈ 1:15)

Chase. Chain-walks between tiers, lamps swinging, the whole cliff shivering with light. Silas runs the way he talks: never in a straight line. Kaito behind him. Skarn on the planks below. Bram coming the slow way with the lamp, because he would not leave it.

MUSIC: brass, running; a sly violin that keeps slipping in behind the beat

SILAS: (over his shoulder, not out of breath) Left is quicker!
KAITO: I'M NOT TAKING DIRECTIONS FROM—

He takes the left. It's quicker. He gains. Silas drops a tier on his chain, lands in a queue at a soup window, and is instantly in conversation with the woman at the front.

SILAS: Is that the eel? That's not the eel. (to the cook) Darling, when's the eel?

Kaito lands behind him. Silas steps aside. Kaito goes through the queue and into the soup window.

SFX: CLANG. Pots.
KAITO: (face in a pot lid) I'm fine. I'M FINE.

Juno on the tier above, chalking a lamp-post: KAITO AND WHO? — THE SOUP WINDOW — 9 TO 2.

JUNO: Head first again, sweetheart. Odds shorten.

Silas runs out of walkway: a ten-foot gap, black water below. He doesn't slow. A silent thing with owl wings and a cat's body comes down out of the dark and its wingbeat carries him across as if he weighed nothing. Hush. Kaito skids to the edge.

KAITO: He's BONDED.
SILAS: (across the gap, a hand on the owl-lynx's flat face, catching his breath; the rasp is in it now) Everybody's something. (points up the cliff) Room over the chandler's. Eighty-Eight. If you must. Knock first. I'm shy.

Gone into a lamp-lit stairwell.

KAITO: Why would he TELL me that?
JUNO: (arriving) Because he wants you there. Worry about that more than the brass.

## SCENE 6 — INT. ROOM EIGHTY-EIGHT, ABOVE THE CHANDLER'S — NIGHT (≈ 2:00)

A door chalked 88. Kaito doesn't knock. He kicks it.

A small lamp-lit room. Silas sits cross-legged on an unmade bed, shirt off and folded beside him for once, sash still on, surrounded by letters in dozens of hands, a stack on the pillow tied with red ribbon.

SILAS: (not looking up) You didn't knock.
KAITO: You stole our—
SILAS: I'm doing my correspondence. Sit or stand, either's fine, you're very decorative.

Kaito, despite himself, reads a letter upside down by his boot.

KAITO: "...the way you hold a glass like it owes you..." What IS this?
SILAS: Fan mail. Everyone I've ever left has written to tell me so.
KAITO: You slept with half of Lanternreach?
SILAS: Don't be unkind. A third. The rest I just owe money.

Kaito's ears are red. He is furious about it.

KAITO: Why's your shirt off?
SILAS: Because I was in bed, darling, and a shirt isn't a limb. Why are you still looking at it? (beat; plainly, for once) You can have it back. The brass. Not the shirt.
KAITO: ...What?
SILAS: The Ledger's dock, in the shed by the pen barge. Nobody moves a thing before the dawn bell. If you can lift it, it's yours. You look like a lifter.
KAITO: You stole it to GIVE IT BACK?
SILAS: I stole it because I'm a thief. I'm giving it back because you're red to the ears and your giant looks at me like I kicked a bird. (shrug) Life's long. Make a note.
SELA: (O.S.) He's lying about the dawn bell.
SILAS: (brightening) Is that the captain? Captain, come in, there's a letter here in your hand, I'd swear to it—

Sela in the doorway, Juno behind her, gloved hands glowing faint orange. Silas looks at them, then at Kaito, then at the window.

SILAS: Three. It's always three. Fine.

He strips the sheet off the bed in one pull, letters bursting up like startled birds, and goes out the window backwards with the sheet in his fist.

KAITO: SKARN—
SELA: Pup, don't—

He's out the window after him.

EXT. ROOFTOPS. The sheet is a cape, then a sail, then a rope as Silas swings a chimney and lands running, shedding letters. Kaito sprints behind through a snowfall of them. One hits his face.

KAITO: (reading, running) "...you ruined me for Tuesdays..." WHO WRITES THIS?
SILAS: (ahead) The harbourmaster! Very lonely man! Mind the—

Chimney. Full sprint.

SFX: DONG
KAITO: (flat on his back in the letters) ...chimney.

Silas's face appears over a ridge-line, upside down, honestly checking.

SILAS: Still with us? Good. (pulling his shirt on, not buttoning it) Hands up when you swing. I meant that.

Gone. Kaito lies in the letters. Skarn, finally up here, looks down at him. Absolute disgust.

KAITO: Don't.

## SCENE 8 — EXT. THE GULLWAKE, DECK — NIGHT (≈ 0:50)

The crew on deck, battered. Kaito with a pot-lid dent on his forehead. Juno reading love letters aloud for her own pleasure. Sela writing. Bram ladling.

JUNO: (reading) "Your absence is a lamp I can't put out." Oh, that's good. Nine to two he wrote them to himself.
SELA: (writing) He didn't. The hands are all different.
BRAM: (a bowl into Kaito's hands) Eat.
KAITO: We've no food. He TOOK the food.
BRAM: I keep a pot back. Galley rule.
KAITO: Which one?
BRAM: New one. Anyone who hits a chimney eats first. (beat) He should come for dinner.
KAITO: WHAT?
BRAM: Anybody that thin. Can't help it.
KAITO: He ROBBED us, Bram. He robbed us and he— the SHIRT—
SELA: That's four times you've said "the shirt," pup. I've written them all down.

KAITO: I'm going to the Ledger's dock. If the brass is there, I'm taking it. If it isn't, I'm finding him.

KAITO: I don't START things—
SELA, JUNO AND BRAM: (together, flat) Yes you do.

## SCENE 9 — EXT. THE LEDGER'S DOCK, THE PENS — DEEP NIGHT (≈ 1:40)

A long dark quay below the lamps. A grey Sever pen barge under a tarp roof. Beside it a shed, door ajar: four crates of Gaptooth brass under a cloth. Kaito and Skarn come along the wall in the dark.

KAITO: (whisper) There. Told you. Now we just—

Movement. Silas, on the barge. Alone. Shirt open, chain loose in one hand, very still in front of the cages.

Kaito presses into the shadow. Skarn's ears flatten. What Silas sees: eleven cages. A ridge-backed thing with sad eyes; a long-legged bird with a bandaged wing; something small and shelled with its face in a corner; a furred creature still wearing a harness, somebody's. Each cage tagged with a chalk number.

No trooper on deck. A light in the cabin: voices, dice. Nobody watching. Nobody paying.

Silas looks for a long time. His face, lit from below by the water, isn't the man from the bar. There is nothing on it at all.

SILAS: (barely) ...Nine. Ten. Eleven.

Kaito's hand on his gauntlet, the fork flickering. He waits for the deal: a buyer, a boat, a bag.

The chain-blade moves. One lock, cut without a sound: a breath of wind through the blade, and the padlock drops into Silas's palm before it can ring on the deck. He sets it down. Opens the door. Steps back.

The ridge-backed thing doesn't move. Silas waits. Doesn't coax. Doesn't speak. Then it comes out low, past his legs, over the side, into the black water with hardly a sound.

SFX: a soft splash; a dice-cup rattling in the cabin; laughter

Second lock. Third. The bird can't fly; he lifts it onto the quay and it hobbles off along the wall, past Kaito's boots, without seeing him. Kaito does not breathe. Fourth. Fifth. The shelled thing: he has to reach right into the cage, and it bites him, and he lets it.

SILAS: (whisper, to it) Fair.

Sixth. Seventh. Eighth and ninth with one swing. Tenth, the furred one: he kneels and takes the harness off first, buckle by buckle, and lays it on the deck. Then the door.

Eleventh. The last padlock drops into his palm. He stands in a barge of open doors, eleven locks in a neat row at his feet, like coins.

Nobody comes. Nobody pays him. He never once looked round.

He sits on the edge of the barge, legs over the side, and looks at the water where they went. His hand goes to his throat. The rasp in his breathing is louder now.

SILAS: (very quiet) Well. Nobody's buying tonight.

In the shadow of the shed, Kaito lowers the gauntlet. The fork goes dark. He looks at the man he came here to hit, and he hasn't got a face for it yet.

Skarn looks at Silas, then at Kaito. For once, no disgust. Just watching.
