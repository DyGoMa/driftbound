## SCENE 1 — EXT. OPEN SEA, EAST OF GAPTOOTH — MORNING (≈ 1:20)

Black screen. A woman's voice, dry and unhurried. The small sound of a page turning.

SELA (V.O.): Previously. According to me. (page) "Gaptooth Reach. Hekka Dorn, one R, Sever captain. Good reach. Owes me a drink." Fine. "The deckhand hit her with lightning." I've written "brave," crossed it out, written "stupid," and underneath, smaller, "both." (page) "Went under with Vesh to lift the sea. Came up through her boat." I've underlined "her boat" twice, so I think I enjoyed that. (a beat) "Laughed." It says I laughed. I'll take my word for it.

Fade up. Flat bright sea, the Reach a smudge astern. The Gullwake under tow, patched: the split deck bridged with planks, Grist asleep across the join, a spliced tow of rope and chain running out to Vesh. Chain cuts show on her scales.

Sela (28, cropped blue navigator's jacket, silver-blue ponytail, tin mug in hand, logbook under her arm) stands at the bow. Bram (31, huge, shirtless under his dungarees, towel on one shoulder) has the wheel.

SELA: Bram. Half a point to starboard. There's a tooth that followed us out.
BRAM: Half a point.

Kaito (21, hair flat on one side from sleep, right arm in a sling of torn sailcloth, chart board tucked under it, pen in his left hand) comes up the ladder and stands beside her, working up to something.

KAITO: You're reading it again. The water.
SELA: It's rude not to. It's talking.
KAITO: Yesterday. Under there. (stuck) I just mean. That was. (gives up) Mag would've been proud of you.

Sela sips.

SELA: Who?
KAITO: (grinning) Come on.
SELA: Is this a Juno thing? Have I got a silver on it?
KAITO: Mag Tolliver. Sorrow Point. Seventy, hands like oars. Threw you in forty-one times.

She looks at him with polite interest, the way you look at someone telling you about a dream they had.

SELA: Sounds like a dreadful teacher.

Kaito's grin goes. Behind him, at the wheel, Bram has gone very still, both big hands flat on the spokes.

SELA: What's the face, pup?
KAITO: Nothing. (turning away) Nothing.

## SCENE 2 — EXT. THE GULLWAKE, AFT DECK — MORNING (≈ 1:45)

Kaito stalks aft. Juno (23, copper braid, bomber jacket open over her white tank, Ashka hunched on her shoulder) is at the mast with chalk, rewriting the betting pool around the harpoon hole from the Reach. KAITO AND WHO? is back. So is THE SEA — 5 TO 2.

KAITO: She doesn't know who Mag is.
JUNO: Who's Mag?
KAITO: The woman who taught her everything! The best year of her life! She stood right there, Bram, you heard her, "that one I don't need the book for"—
BRAM: (quiet, at the wheel) I heard.
KAITO: So where is it?
BRAM: Bottom of the Gap. With the tow.

Kaito stares at him. Bram wipes down a wheel spoke with the towel, not looking up.

BRAM: She asked me last night who taught her the stew.
KAITO: And?
BRAM: Nobody did. She taught me.

Juno's chalk has stopped on the mast.

JUNO: She asked me what the bet was. Third time. I'd told her twice, sweetheart, and I'm loud.
KAITO: So it's not just the one year.
JUNO: (no joke in it) I don't know what it is. I've got no book on it.

Kaito hits the rail with his good hand.

KAITO: She KNEW. She knew the price and she went in laughing—
BRAM: It's a bill. You pay it. You don't pick the coins.
KAITO: Screw that. She's up there sipping tea like she lost a glove.
JUNO: Six to one you go in there and make it worse.
KAITO: Screw that too.
JUNO: (to Ashka, chalking) That's a yes.

He goes. Skarn, flat out by the galley door, watches him pass and shuts his eyes again. Not his problem.

## SCENE 3 — INT. CHART ROOM — MORNING (≈ 3:00)

Sela at the chart table, writing. She doesn't look up when the door bangs.

SELA: Door's on hinges, pup. Quite good ones.
KAITO: You said you didn't need the book for it.
SELA: (writing) For what?
KAITO: THAT! That's the whole point! You stood there and said best year of your life, and it's GONE, and you don't even know what you're missing—
SELA: That's rather the nature of it.
KAITO: Don't be clever.
SELA: (looking up at last) I'm not being clever. I'm being accurate. There's a difference, and you'll learn it.

She puts the pen down. Perfectly calm. He is shaking.

KAITO: How can you just sit there?
SELA: Where would you like me to sit?
KAITO: Be ANGRY! It took a whole year off you and you're—
SELA: Pup. (a beat) You've seen what happens when I raise my voice at the sea. I came up through a boat.

He has nothing. She gets up, crosses to a brass-bound sea chest against the bulkhead, and throws the lid back. Logbooks. Forty of them, worn and water-stained, spines up, each one dated in the same neat hand.

SELA: All mine. In my writing. About a woman I've never met.

She lifts one out and holds it the way you'd hold a glass of wine.

SELA: I take her on trust. She's been reliable.
KAITO: (quiet) That's insane.
SELA: That's Tuesday.
KAITO: Tuesday?
SELA: Every day's Tuesday in there. It's restful.

She opens one and shows him: beside every entry, in the margin, a small tick or a small circle.

SELA: Every morning I read yesterday. What I remember gets a tick. What I don't gets a circle, and I read it twice more so it at least sounds like mine.
KAITO: How many circles?
SELA: I don't count. Juno would.

He turns pages with his one hand. Ticks. Ticks. Then a page of circles. Then another. He stops.

KAITO: Mag's in here?
SELA: (back at the table) Sorrow Point, by the dates. Four pages.
KAITO: Four PAGES? For a year?
SELA: I wrote it when I was twenty and thought I'd keep it.

Silence. Sela stirs the ink. Kaito looks at the book in his hand like it might bite.

SELA: Want the funny one? There's a funny one.
KAITO: No.
SELA: Brown book, page sixty-something. "Bram told a joke about a lobster. Laughed till I cried." Three weeks later: "Bram told a joke about a lobster. Laughed till I cried. Bram looked odd after."

Kaito finds it. Reads it. Looks up.

KAITO: ...What's the lobster joke?
SELA: No idea. I'm told it's filthy. I've laughed at it twice and he won't tell it a third time.

A laugh gets out of Kaito, awful, like a cough. He sits down hard on the edge of the trunk.

KAITO: That's the saddest thing I ever heard.
SELA: It's a very good joke, apparently.

She comes and sits beside him on the trunk, shoulder to shoulder. The teasing goes out of her voice.

SELA: Here's what I know, and I know it fresh every day. I pick the ship. I pick the crew. I don't pick the coin. If I'd stopped paying every time I didn't like the price, I'd have a lovely clear memory of standing on a dock.
KAITO: (small) Forty-one times. She threw you in forty-one times and you loved it.
SELA: Then write that down.

She slides her open logbook across the chart table to him, today's page half full, and lays the pen on it.

SELA: You're the only one on this ship who went into that Gap with me and came out with all of it. So you do mine. Just the bits I owe.
KAITO: In your book?
SELA: Under the line. So I know it isn't me.

He looks at the pen. At his right hand, dead in its sling. He takes the pen in his left and writes. It takes a long time. Sela watches, head on one side.

INSERT: under her neat line, in letters like a fence after a storm: MAG TOLIVER. SORROW POINT. 41 TIMES. YOU LOVED IT. — K

SELA: Two Ls in Tolliver, I'd guess.
KAITO: You GUESS?
SELA: And you write like Grist walks.
KAITO: It's the wrong HAND.

She takes the pen back and puts a tick in the margin beside his line. Before she's read it twice.

SELA: There. That one's mine now. Secondhand. Still fits.

## SCENE 4 — EXT. THE GULLWAKE, MAIN DECK — DUSK (≈ 1:15)

Orange light. Juno at the mast, chalking a brand-new line in big letters: CAPTAIN KEEPS TONIGHT — 3 TO 1 ON. Kaito reads it, appalled.

KAITO: You're betting on her HEAD?
JUNO: I'm betting FOR it, sweetheart. First time in my life I've backed the house. Feels filthy.
KAITO: What's "tonight"?
JUNO: A memory worth keeping. She's been paying in old ones. So we give her a new one so good she'd fight the sea for it.

From behind a barrel she lifts a dusty black bottle, wax-sealed, no label.

JUNO: Forty years old. Cinder Palace cellar. Lazare Quint doesn't know he's lost it yet.

Bram passes with a steaming pot, shirtless, towel over his shoulder.

BRAM: Rule Thirty-Three. Nobody drinks on an empty stomach on my deck.
JUNO: (watching him go, to Kaito) Thirty-three rules and not one of them's "shirt."
BRAM: (O.S.) I'm cooking.
JUNO: Yeah, you are.

Kaito's ears go red on her behalf. Ashka hides her head under a wing.

SFX: a long, low hhhOOOOM from the water

The light on the deck changes. Off the tow, Vesh has come alongside and is curling herself around the ship, the whole length of her, until the Gullwake sits inside a ring of dark blue scale. One by one, from head to tail, her lures come on: teal and gold, a wall of lanterns.

Sela comes up from below, logbook under her arm, and stops on the top step. Juno puts a tin cup into her hand before she can speak.

JUNO: Captain. You're off duty. The serpent's got the watch.
SELA: (looking at the ring of lures) She hasn't done that since—

She stops. Thinks. Doesn't know since when. Opens the logbook to check. Juno closes it with one gloved finger.

JUNO: Nope.
SELA: Juno.
JUNO: Drink.
SELA: (drinking) Mutiny.
JUNO: Hospitality.

## SCENE 5 — EXT. THE GULLWAKE, MAIN DECK — NIGHT (≈ 2:45)

Later. Lanterns on the mast, Vesh's lures all round, the sea beyond black. The black bottle is a third down. Bram sits on an upturned bucket with a bowl in his lap, singing low, the kind of voice you'd feel through a wall.

BRAM: (singing) Lay the keel where the water's kind, / nail her fast and leave the shore behind. / What the sea takes, she takes for good, / so build her twice, lad. Build her of wood.

Sela on a coil of rope, cup in hand, swaying very slightly to it. Her logbook is open on her knee and her pen is moving.

JUNO: Oi. No.

Juno takes the pen out of her hand mid-word and tucks it into her own braid.

SELA: That's not how it works.
JUNO: Tonight it is. You remember it the hard way like the rest of us. Drink.
SELA: You're very bossy for a deckhand.
JUNO: I'm a sharpshooter.
SELA: With what?
JUNO: (beat) Drink.

Sela drinks. Closes the book. Her hand stays flat on the cover a moment, then lets it go.

JUNO: Right. Everybody up. Captain gets a dance.
KAITO: No.
JUNO: Didn't ask you, sweetheart, you've got one arm. You're the audience.

She kicks off into it: the Ashmarket furnace shuffle. Elbows. A lot of elbows. Ashka leaves her shoulder in protest and lands on the wheel with her back turned. Bram keeps singing, nodding along. Grist snores. It is genuinely terrible.

KAITO: What IS that?
JUNO: I learned it in Ashmarket. Men used to pay me to stop.
KAITO: How much?
JUNO: You couldn't afford it.
BRAM: (between verses) She's not wrong. It was a lot.
JUNO: You PAID?
BRAM: I paid you to keep going.

Sela is laughing. Properly, head back. Juno grabs Kaito's good hand and hauls him up anyway, spins him once, lets go. He goes straight into the mast.

SFX: BONK
KAITO: AGH! Juno!
JUNO: (not stopping) THE MAST — 2 TO 1 ON!

Skarn, under the rail, lifts his head to look at his human, and lowers it with the slow disgust of a wolf who has seen enough.

Later still. The bottle lower. Bram hums, eyes wet, happy. Sela sits with her back against Vesh's scales, a lure glowing over her head like a reading lamp. Kaito slides down beside her, rubbing his forehead.

SELA: (tipsy, fond) You hit that mast like it owed you money.
KAITO: It does. It knows what it did.

A quiet beat. The hum of Vesh through the scales at their backs.

SELA: Pup. If I lose this one too—
KAITO: Don't.
SELA: Listen. If I lose it, it still happened. That's the trick I've worked out. The night happens whether I keep it or not. You lot keep it. That's what a crew's for.
KAITO: That's not a trick. That's just losing.
SELA: Everything's a trick, pup. The good ones you let yourself fall for.
KAITO: I'll write it down.
SELA: You'll write it like Grist walks.
KAITO: It's the wrong HAND.
SELA: (patting his knee) I know, pup. I remember.

She raises her voice across the deck without raising it much.

SELA: Bram. Tell me the lobster one.
BRAM: No.
SELA: Bram.
BRAM: Third time's unlucky.
JUNO: I'll tell it.
BRAM: You don't know it.
JUNO: I know a lobster.

Juno plants herself in front of them, cup raised, breath in. Bram puts his face in his towel.

JUNO: So there's this lobster, right, and he walks into a bathhouse—

CUT TO: Skarn's face. Pure disgust.

CUT TO: Grist. Snoring. A bubble at the horn.

CUT TO: Ashka on the wheel, feathers slowly rising.

O.S.: the whole deck erupting, Bram's laugh like a landslide, Juno's like a bark, and Sela's over all of it, high and helpless, until she's coughing.

SELA: (O.S., through it) Oh, that's AWFUL. Oh no. Oh, Bram.
BRAM: (O.S.) Told you.

## SCENE 6 — EXT. THE GULLWAKE, MAIN DECK — DAWN (≈ 1:15)

Grey light. Vesh still curled round the ship, lures dimming. Bram asleep sitting up, bucket on his knee. Juno flat on her back with Ashka on her stomach. Kaito in a heap by the mast, a bruise on his forehead, the sling over his face. Grist has not moved all night.

Sela wakes against Vesh's scales under a blanket somebody put on her. Winces. Finds her logbook. Opens it to last night's page.

INSERT: her own neat hand, cut off mid-word where the pen was taken: "Vesh has rung the ship. Juno says I'm off duty and she is holding my pe"

Below it, in fence-after-a-storm letters: SHE WAS HAPPY. — K

Sela looks at that a long moment. Then her shoulders start to shake. It's not crying. It's the lobster. She puts a hand over her mouth and laughs until Vesh's lure flickers above her head.

She retrieves the pen from Juno's braid without waking her, and writes.

SELA (V.O.): (writing) Juno danced. Badly. Men once paid her to stop; I'd have paid double and asked for an encore. Bram sang. Kaito hit the mast twice, once on purpose. The lobster is in a bathhouse, and I know the rest.

She draws the tick first. Then writes.

SELA (V.O.): (writing) Checked. This one's mine.

Juno, on her back, eyes still shut, speaks from under Ashka.

JUNO: Well?
SELA: The lobster's in a bathhouse.
JUNO: (a slow grin, eyes closed) ...Pay me.
SELA: You bet on it, you don't get paid.
JUNO: Three to one on, sweetheart. I'd have paid out at a loss and smiled. (opens one eye) Don't tell anyone.
SELA: And you danced for me.
JUNO: I danced AT you. Different thing.
SELA: (writing) Noted.
JUNO: Don't write it DOWN.

## SCENE 7 — INT. CHART ROOM — LATE MORNING (≈ 1:15)

Kaito, one-handed, is putting logbooks back into the trunk in date order, reading spines, tongue between his teeth. The oldest one, cloth cover gone grey, slips from under his arm and falls open on the deck.

He picks it up. Stops.

INSERT: the page is a stub: a page torn out close to the spine, the edge brown and soft with age. On the facing page, in Sela's hand but younger, rounder: "Ask him about the song. He will say you made it up. You did not."

Kaito reads it twice. Sela comes in behind him with her mug and a course to plot.

KAITO: Sela. Who's "him"?

She glances at the page. No pause at all.

SELA: Old tear.
KAITO: It says ask him about the song.
SELA: If I knew him, pup, I'd have asked.

She takes the book out of his hand, not unkindly, and sets it back in the trunk, spine up, in its place.

SELA: That one's older than the ticks. Nothing in it I can check.
KAITO: Doesn't it bother you?
SELA: Everything in that trunk bothers me. That's why I've got a system.

She closes the lid. Sits. Opens today's book to a fresh page and writes a heading.

SELA: Lanternreach. Three days east. We need a tow that isn't rope and a deck that isn't a beetle.
KAITO: What's Lanternreach?
SELA: Lamps on every rope and nobody uses a name. You'll hate it.
KAITO: Why?
SELA: Everyone there flirts. Professionally.
KAITO: Three days of that?
SELA: Three days of you walking into lamp posts. I've budgeted for it.

Kaito's ears, red, in the doorway. Skarn, behind him, already looks tired.

## SCENE 8 — EXT./INT. LANTERNREACH — NIGHT (≈ 0:50)

A port built out of light: lamps on every post, rope and bowsprit, hundreds of them, and not one sign with a name on it. Fog sits on the water and glows.

A room over a chandler's. Cards, smoke, three losers and one winner. The winner: Silas Moreau (24, man, 183 cm, pale skin, violet eyes, a beauty mark under the left eye, long dark-teal hair tied low, white shirt unbuttoned to the stomach, red sash), stacking coins with two fingers, bored by how easy it is. On the beam above him, something with an owl's face and a cat's body sits perfectly still: Hush.

A dock runner (woman, 34, oilskin, hood up) leans in the door and holds out a letter without a word. Silas takes it without looking at her. Wax seal: a straight line cutting a circle.

He breaks it. Reads. Among the lines, one word is clear: Gullwake. His face, pleasant, doesn't change at all.

SILAS: (a faint rasp) Well. That's inconvenient, darling.
RUNNER: Which of us is darling?
SILAS: (tucking the letter into his sash, already dealing) Everyone, eventually. It saves time.

Above him Hush opens one round eye.

SMASH CUT TO BLACK.
