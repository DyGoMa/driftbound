## COLD OPEN

## SCENE 1 — INT. THE SLAG BAR, ASHMARKET — NIGHT, CONTINUOUS FROM EPISODE 6 (≈ 1:00)

Black. Juno's voice, bright, over the sound of coins being counted.

JUNO (V.O.): Recap, sweetheart. Odds I tell it straight: don't ask. Pinhold got nailed to the sea, this crew got out, and a boy with a wolf and no sense bet me he could last ten seconds. He lost his boots. Then his jacket. Then... well. The bar got its money's worth. I kept his goggles as a deposit, because I am a businesswoman. And then the grey coats walked in and bought every debt I've got. So. Odds I'm fine: don't ask that either.

Fade up. The Slag Bar, lantern-lit, soot in the air, fifty gamblers gone quiet. WARDEN KRELL (41, 195 cm, heavy trooper officer, a shield on his back like a door) stands mid-room with a paper in one fist. Behind him, six troopers in a shield line.

Kaito (21) at the bar in no shirt, no jacket, no boots. Skarn lies across the doorway. Sela (28) hasn't put her drink down. Bram (31) stands behind Juno, enormous, silent.

Juno (23) sits on the card table, Ashka on her shoulder, Kaito's goggles around her neck, a grin doing a lot of work.

KRELL: Calloway, Juno. Marker for four thousand one hundred, held by the Slag, the Furnace Row book, and two men who are now dead. Held now by the Sever.
JUNO: You bought my tab. That's sweet. Nobody's ever bought me anything.
KRELL: Payment due on Ember Night. In coin, in full.
JUNO: And if I'm short?
KRELL: (looks at Ashka) Then the bird is coin.

Ashka's feathers lift. Sparks drop onto the table.

KAITO: Hey. Hey! You don't get to—
SELA: (not loud) Pup.
KAITO: She's not even—! He's talking about stripping her bird in a bar!
KRELL: I'm talking about an account, boy. Put a shirt on.

Krell turns and the shield line turns with him, a grey wall walking out. Skarn doesn't move. The last trooper has to step over his tail. Skarn lets him. Barely.

Silence. The bar starts breathing again.

JUNO: (to the room, bright) Well. Who had "the Sever" in the pool?

Nobody laughs. Her gloved hand is flat on the table so it won't shake. Ashka puts her beak against Juno's ear.

## PART A

## SCENE 2 — INT. GULLWAKE GALLEY — MORNING (≈ 2:00)

Grey light. The Gullwake moored under Ashmarket's black crag; through the porthole, lanterns burning in daylight because the soot never lets the sun through. Grist asleep across the galley floor, a truck-sized beetle in a room built for people. Everyone steps over him.

Bram at the stove, shirtless under the dungarees, towel on his shoulder. Sela at the table, logbook open, writing. Kaito in a borrowed shirt of Bram's that could hold three of him.

Juno in the doorway, not quite in, Ashka on her shoulder.

BRAM: Sit.
JUNO: I'm not crew, big man.
BRAM: Galley Rule six. Nobody stands in my door looking hungry. Sit.

She sits. A bowl lands in front of her. She eats like someone who has always eaten fast.

KAITO: My goggles.
JUNO: Your deposit.
KAITO: You lost the bet with Krell, not me. Deposit's void.
JUNO: Nothing's void, sweetheart. I've got till Ember Night. Three days. (mouth full) Good stew.
BRAM: (misty) It's the bone.
SELA: (writing) "Day two in Ashmarket. We have no parts, no money, and a guest."
JUNO: Put "charming guest."
SELA: I write what happened.

Kaito drops onto the bench opposite Juno.

KAITO: Four thousand. You can't have four thousand.
JUNO: I can't have four hundred. Eight to one I can't have forty by Thursday.
KAITO: So he takes Ashka.
JUNO: (grin, fixed) So he tries.
KAITO: You know what that is? I watched them do it at Pinhold. A plough-beast. They count to ten, and the man who—
BRAM: Kaito.
KAITO: (standing) Screw it. We're not letting a Sever officer run a tab on a living thing.
SELA: We?
KAITO: I'm crew. I signed. Deckhand. It's on your roster in my handwriting.
SELA: It is. (writes) "The deckhand has opinions."

Sela closes the logbook and looks at Juno properly.

SELA: Why does a woman who cheats Sever officers at cards owe four thousand in a town this size?
JUNO: Because I'm very good at cards and very bad at stopping.
SELA: That's the joke answer.
JUNO: It's the only kind I do, Captain.

A beat. Sela lets it sit.

SELA: I'm not asking you to pay us. I'm asking what we're walking into.
JUNO: (standing, bowl in hand) Then don't ask me. Ask Nim. He does the maths for this whole city, and he's the only man on the crag who tells the truth, because nobody's ever paid him to lie.

She takes the bowl to the sink and washes it. Bram's eyes go wet.

BRAM: She washed up.
KAITO: Bram.
BRAM: Nobody washes up.

Skarn, filling the companionway stairs, watches Juno pass. She scratches behind his ear without asking. He lets her. His face says he did not agree to this.

## SCENE 3 — EXT./INT. NIM HADDA'S BOOK, FURNACE ROW — DAY (≈ 2:40)

Ashmarket by day: stairs cut into a volcanic crag, lanterns on chains, soot on everything, vents breathing grey steam up through street grates. Gamblers already at tables under awnings. Every table has the same little brass lamp.

A stall squeezed between two furnace-houses, with a chalkboard of odds the height of a man. NIM HADDA (30, one arm, the empty sleeve pinned neatly, soft voice, pencil behind his ear) writes odds with his good hand while Juno leans on his counter like she grew there.

NIM: Juno. You brought me strangers.
JUNO: I brought you customers, Nim.
NIM: Same thing in this town. (to Sela) Captain Vey. The hundred-foot serpent in the harbour, I take it that's yours.
SELA: Vesh. She's not for betting on.
NIM: Everything's for betting on. That's the trouble.

He chalks a new line without looking: GULLWAKE CREW LEAVES ALIVE — 3/1.

KAITO: Three to one?
NIM: It was five before you walked up the stairs with the wolf. People like the wolf.

Skarn looks pleased. It's disgusting.

SELA: Juno says you'll tell us how this city works.
NIM: (gentle) It doesn't. It's rigged, Captain, from the lamps down. Every table in Ashmarket is a Cinder Palace table. The Palace owns the cards, the dice, the dealers, and the lamp you count your money under. You can win. People do. You just can't win more than the house decided on Monday.
KAITO: Then how does she owe four thousand?
NIM: Because she won.
JUNO: (shrug) I kept winning. The house doesn't like that. So it let me keep winning, on credit, against players who were the house, until the night it asked for it back.
NIM: The House always wins. It just sometimes lets you hold the money first.
BRAM: (quiet) Like feeding a pig.
NIM: (nods) Like feeding a pig.

A pause. Juno is studying the chalkboard very hard.

SELA: And now the Palace has sold the marker to the Sever.
NIM: Warden Krell bought the whole Row's bad paper yesterday. Cash. Nobody asked where a soldier gets cash. I asked. (taps his sleeve) I've already lost an arm asking things, so I'm careful how I ask.
KAITO: So we take four thousand off the house.
NIM: You can't. See above.
KAITO: Screw that. There's got to be one table in this whole city that isn't—
NIM: One.

He turns the chalkboard around. On the back, in gold chalk: EMBER NIGHT — THE HIGH TABLE — CINDER PALACE.

NIM: Ember Night. The crag vents, every lantern in the city goes out for the breath, then they relight them. One night a year the Palace runs an honest table, because the richest people in the Drift sit at it and they'd notice. Outside money. Buy-in's five hundred a seat. Pot clears four thousand easy, paid on the spot. In coin. In full.
JUNO: And it's the one night nobody's ever let me near.
NIM: Because you're Juno Calloway and your face is on a board in every pit on the Row.
SELA: (writing) "A table we can't afford, in a palace that knows her face, on the night the debt comes due." (looks up) I like it.
KAITO: You like it?
SELA: It's the only door, pup. I've always liked a door.

Bram counts on his fingers. Stops.

BRAM: We don't have five hundred.
NIM: No. But the city does.

He thuds a tin box onto the chalkboard.

NIM: Since you walked up my stairs, every soul on Furnace Row has been laying money that the Gullwake strangers get thrown off the crag by Ember Night. I hold the book. That's a float. I stake your buy-in from it. If you lose, everybody who bet against you was right, I pay out, and I'm no worse off.
SELA: And if we win?
NIM: Then an entire city of people who bet on you dying owes me, and I retire. (small smile) I'm not staking you, Captain. They are. They just think they're betting against you.
JUNO: (quiet, for him) Nim.
NIM: You'd have done it for me, Ju. You did, once. (to the crew) One problem. The High Table is formal dress, and faces are checked at the door. Hers is known. The boy's is on a Sever bill from Pinhold. (nods at Bram) And you're memorable.
BRAM: Thank you.
NIM: Wasn't a compliment. You'll need to be other people for a night.
KAITO: Disguises.
NIM: Clothes, mostly. Formal ones. Have you got any?

Everyone looks at Kaito, still in Bram's enormous shirt.

JUNO: Ten to one he hasn't got a shirt of his own.

[EYECATCH: Grist asleep on the galley floor with a teacup balanced on his horn, steam rising from it.]

## PART B

## SCENE 4 — INT. GULLWAKE GALLEY — EVENING (≈ 3:00)

The galley is a dressing room now. A salvage trunk open on the floor, stencilled PLEASURE BARGE AMARANTH — LOST — SALVAGE: GULLWAKE, silk spilling out of it. Grist asleep exactly where everyone needs to stand, so Bram has put the teapot and four cups on his shell: he is a coffee table now.

A sail hung across the far end as a changing screen. Skarn by the stove with the face of a dog at a wedding.

Kaito on the bench in a black waistcoat a size and a half too small, the collar trying to kill him.

KAITO: I can't breathe.
JUNO (O.S.): That's formal, sweetheart. Nobody comfortable has any money.
KAITO: Where'd you even get a trunk of ball gowns?
BRAM: (from the stove) Pleasure barge went down off Cinderhead. Nobody aboard. (beat) We checked.
KAITO: You're a salvage crew. You salvaged dresses.
BRAM: Sela said they'd be useful.
KAITO: When?
BRAM: Now.

Behind the sail: movement, a laugh, a cork.

JUNO (O.S.): Captain, you have been holding out.
SELA (O.S.): I've been waiting for a reason.

Bram comes round the stove in a waiter's vest. Technically. White, made for a slim man, it gave up at the third button. His arms don't fit the holes. The towel is still on his shoulder.

KAITO: ...Bram.
BRAM: The vest was made for a smaller man.
KAITO: Most things were.
BRAM: (misty) It's got a little pocket for a pencil.

He has put a pencil in it. He is delighted.

Sela steps out from behind the sail.

Eye level, plain, the way a friend looks at a friend: a midnight-blue gown, backless, slit to the thigh, silver-blue hair down for once, gold hoops, the bond spiral just visible below her collarbone. A glass in one hand and the logbook in the other, because of course.

Kaito stands to say something, turns, and walks into the galley door frame. The whole frame.

SFX: wood, skull

SELA: Pup. Door.
KAITO: (holding his face) I KNOW where the door is.
SELA: You knew where it was. (writes) "He looked. Then the door."

Juno comes out laughing the big laugh, and it stops the room. A red gown that catches every lantern, sparking like her own bird; the braid pinned up; long red gloves to the elbow. Kaito's goggles still around her neck, with the gown.

JUNO: Well? Odds he says something nice: twenty to one.
KAITO: You're wearing my goggles with a ball gown.
JUNO: They're a statement.
KAITO: They're MINE.
JUNO: Twenty to one, told you. (to Sela) Pay up, Captain.
SELA: I didn't take the bet. I never bet against the ears.

Kaito's ears: yes, red. Skarn puts a paw over his own eyes.

Sela looks at Juno's long gloves. Not pointedly. Just looks.

SELA: Gloves with a gown.
JUNO: Opera gloves. It's a look. Everyone at the High Table will have them.
SELA: Will they?
JUNO: (lightly) The rich ones.

Sela writes something small. Juno sees. Neither says anything.

Bram, meanwhile, reaches for the teapot; Grist shifts in his sleep; the teapot slides slowly down an iron shell.

BRAM: (gentle) Grist. Grist. No.

SFX: teapot, floor

BRAM: ...Galley Rule seven. Grist is not furniture.
JUNO: He's very good furniture.
BRAM: He's not furniture when he's AWAKE.

Grist opens one eye, decides none of this is his problem, and closes it.

Kaito tugs the waistcoat. A button fires across the room and hits Skarn.

KAITO: I'm going to die in this.
JUNO: Then die handsome. Stand still.

She crosses and does up what's left of the waistcoat herself, like fixing a friend's collar before a wedding, except she takes her time and he looks at the ceiling. Comedy, not heat: her face knows exactly what it's doing; his doesn't know what day it is.

JUNO: (low) You're going to be my husband tonight.
KAITO: WHAT.
JUNO: Cover story. Rich couple from Gaptooth, new money, bad manners. You can do bad manners.
KAITO: Why am I the husband?
JUNO: Because Bram's the waiter, the Captain's the Captain, and you've got the face of a man who married above himself. Evens you pull it off.
KAITO: Evens is bad.
JUNO: Evens is the best you've ever had, sweetheart.

She pats his chest and goes to find shoes. Kaito stands there.

KAITO: (to Skarn) Not a word.

Skarn's face says all of them.

Sela, at the table, dressed like a duchess, writing like a clerk.

SELA: (reading it back) "Ember Night. Four of us, one honest table, one city that wants us off a cliff. Bram found a pencil." (looks up) Everybody on deck. Vesh wants to see.

## SCENE 5 — EXT. GULLWAKE DECK — NIGHT, EMBER NIGHT (≈ 1:20)

Night harbour. Ashmarket climbs the crag above in a thousand lanterns. Vesh's lures glow under the black water along the hull, a slow blue line, and when Sela comes to the rail in the gown the great head rises, drips, and looks.

SELA: Yes. I know. Don't start.

Vesh starts: a long low rumble that is clearly a joke. Sela flicks water at her.

Juno alone at the stern, Ashka on the rail. Not grinning now; feeding the bird from a glove. Kaito comes up in the waistcoat, no jacket, because the jacket didn't fit either.

KAITO: Nim's cousin has a cart at the top of the stairs.
JUNO: I know. I heard.
KAITO: You're quiet.
JUNO: I'm doing sums.
KAITO: What are the odds?

She doesn't answer for a second. Everything else tonight she answered before he finished asking.

JUNO: On a straight table? Good. I'm good, Kaito. I'm the best card player on this rock, and the rock knows it. That's why it stopped letting me play.
KAITO: Then?
JUNO: Then it's the Cinder Palace, whose owner sold bad paper to Krell yesterday, and I don't believe in honest tables. Never seen one. (strokes Ashka) I just don't have a different door.
KAITO: You've got us.
JUNO: I've got a stranger in a tight waistcoat I stripped in a bar last night.
KAITO: You stripped me in a bet.
JUNO: In front of a bar.
KAITO: (louder) I know where I was!

Ashka chirps. It sounds like laughing.

KAITO: Look. If it goes bad in there, Krell doesn't get her. I don't care what the paper says. I've seen that done to a plough-beast. Not her.
JUNO: That's a bet you can't cover.
KAITO: Screw that. I've got a wolf.

Skarn barks once, below. Juno laughs, properly; a relief.

JUNO: Fine. Side bet. You get through tonight without walking into anything, you get your goggles back.
KAITO: Deal.
JUNO: Anything, sweetheart. Door, lamp, waiter, me.
KAITO: ...Deal.

He puts his hand out. She looks at it, then takes it with her gloved one, and a half-second before she does, the glove tightens on nothing, a small habit, like checking a lock. One firm shake. Lets go.

JUNO: Come on. Husband.
KAITO: Please stop.

Sela, at the rail, has written it down. Vesh, under the water, has seen it too.

## SCENE 6 — EXT. THE CINDER PALACE — EMBER NIGHT (≈ 1:30)

The top of the crag. The Cinder Palace: black stone and brass over the city's biggest vent, every window lit red from below. A long stair to double doors. Carriages, furs, money. A doorman the size of Bram checking faces against a book.

The crew climb. Bram carries a tray, the only way to make the vest make sense. Sela walks like she owns the staircase. Juno holds Kaito's arm; Kaito walks like a man avoiding every object in the world.

KAITO: (muttering) Lamp. Step. Lamp.
JUNO: You're doing fine.
KAITO: Fine is evens.
JUNO: Fine is the best you've ever had.

Then, under their feet, the crag breathes.

SFX: a long exhale through the whole mountain

Every lantern in Ashmarket goes out at once. The whole city, black. Below, a thousand voices say "oh" together. Above, for the first time all episode, stars.

Nobody on the stair moves. Sela, in the dark, looking down at the harbour, at a blue line under the water still lit.

SELA: (soft) There you are.

Then the relighting starts at the foot of the crag and climbs, lamp by lamp, a tide of fire, and when it reaches the Palace stair the doors behind them are open and a man stands in them.

OSSIAN BRAND (49, owner of the Cinder Palace: small, neat, soft-spoken, dark suit with a red lining, a lamp-lighter's taper still in his hand). The manner of a man who runs a very good hotel. The worst thing about him.

BRAND: Ember Night. I light the first one myself. Superstition. (to Sela) Good evening, madam. Welcome to the Cinder Palace.
SELA: Good evening. Party of four. The High Table.
BRAND: Of course. Names?
JUNO: (bright, new money) Mister and Missus Toller. From Gaptooth. And my sister, and our man.
BRAM: (tray) Good evening.
BRAND: (warm) How lovely.

He doesn't look at the doorman's book. He takes a folder from inside his jacket and fans four sheets across the tray in Bram's hands, like dealing a hand.

Four likenesses. A Sever bill from Pinhold: Kaito, the white streak inked in. A Furnace Row board sketch: Juno, grinning. Sela, drawn well, by someone who watched her a while. Bram, with a note in the margin: LARGE.

Nobody breathes. Kaito's hand goes to a weapon he isn't carrying. Juno's grip on his arm says don't.

BRAND: Captain Vey. Miss Calloway. Mister Torvald, the vest is very brave. (to Kaito, kindly) And you'll be the boy with the wolf. I do like the waistcoat.
KAITO: (strangled) It's not mine.
BRAND: Nothing in this city is, Mister Brask.

He closes the folder.

BRAND: I've seen your pictures. The Warden was good enough to send them. He's also at my High Table tonight, waiting for a game. (steps aside, taper raised) So please. Come in. The House has been expecting you.

Through the open doors: a long red room, the High Table under one brass lamp, and at its far end a man the size of a door, a shield behind his chair.

Juno looks at Kaito. Kaito looks at the doorway. He steps through and, for once, doesn't walk into anything.

JUNO: (under her breath) Goggles are still mine.
