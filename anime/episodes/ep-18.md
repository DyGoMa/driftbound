## SCENE 1 — EXT. LANTERNREACH, END OF THE LONG QUAY — NIGHT (≈ 1:20)

Black screen. A young man's voice, loud and already angry.

KAITO (V.O.): Previously. Lanternreach. Port of lamps. Nobody uses names, everybody owes a man called Maro, and the baths have a ledger in them, which, long story, don't. Page one of the ledger: SILAS MOREAU. Sold partners to the Sever for years. Then Maro turns up with his bill, and Silas, smiling, sells us to pay it. Us. All of us. And everybody on this ship kept saying, "Kaito, you're too hard on him," "Kaito, he's funny," "Kaito, give him a chance." I SAID IT. Day one, fifteen minutes in. I said that man would sell us for a hat. (a breath) It wasn't a hat. It was worse than a hat. I'd like that written down somewhere.

SELA (V.O.): (dry, far off) It's written down.

KAITO (V.O.): Good.

Fade up. Lanternreach at night, a port built of lamps, the harbour a low gold smear on black water. At the end of the long quay, alone, Silas Moreau (24, man, 183 cm, slim, long dark-teal hair tied low, white shirt open to the stomach, red sash, chain-blade at his hip). Hand flat against his own throat. Three short feather lines down the left side of his neck glow faint violet.

Beside him, from nowhere, without a sound: Hush, cat-bodied, owl-winged, flat owl face.

Silas breathes in once. Then out. And keeps breathing out.

SFX: nothing. Not wind; the absence of it, like ears underwater

The lines on his neck blaze. The breath leaving him is a thin draught, and down the quay lamp after lamp leans in it and goes out. It goes on far longer than a man has breath for. His knees give; a hand on Hush's shoulder.

The last lamp goes out. He straightens. Opens his mouth.

SILAS: (the shape of "Darling." No sound at all.)

He touches his throat with two fingers and smiles like a man finding a bill correct. He lifts a tally slate off the quay-master's hut, chalk on a string, and follows Hush into the dark.

## SCENE 2 — INT. MARO'S WAREHOUSE — NIGHT (≈ 2:30)

A long hall of crates and chains, forty lamps in two rows, all lit: bright as a church, the shadows hard black. At the far end, a desk under the biggest lamp, and a man writing.

Ledger Maro (52, man, 172 cm, soft-spoken, grey waistcoat and sleeve garters, round spectacles, ink on one finger). Three account books open. His pen never hurries.

Nearer the doors, an iron holding cage. Inside: Kaito (21, goggles up, gauntlet on, pacing), Sela (28, cropped blue jacket, silver-blue ponytail, cross-legged on the floor, writing in her logbook), Juno (23, bomber jacket, gloves on, lying on a crate, boots crossed), Bram (31, enormous, shirtless in his dungarees, sitting against the bars, towel over his shoulder).

Across the floor: Skarn chain-muzzled in an iron pen, back to everyone. Ashka in a tall birdcage, sparks dying on the stone. Grist under a cargo net weighted with three anchors, asleep.

SFX: zzzzRRNK... zzzzRRNK...

Beside Skarn's pen a big man (Tull, 44, shaved head, leather apron) holds a Sever bond-cutter a foot from the wolf's throat. Not glowing. Not yet.

MARO: (writing) Four. Three in iron. One in the harbour. (dips the pen) And one owed.

KAITO: Are you counting us?

MARO: I'm counting everything, Mr Brask. I find it calming. You might try it.

KAITO: I'll count your teeth in a minute.

MARO: You're welcome to. Thirty-one. A lady in Ashmarket has the other one. She'd earned it.

JUNO: (not opening her eyes) Evens he's bluffing about the teeth.

Kaito grips the bars; the fork on his hand flickers yellow. Tull's hook lights white, an inch closer to Skarn's neck. Skarn doesn't turn round.

MARO: Please don't. Mr Tull is a patient man but he's been standing a long time. And your captain's serpent is in my harbour under six harpoon guns, so I'd ask the captain not to either.

SELA: (writing, not looking up) I wasn't going to.

MARO: I know. You've been very civil. I confiscated the spyglass and left you the book. A woman should have her book.

SELA: You read it.

MARO: I did. (a small, honest nod) I'm sorry about the year.

A beat. Sela's pen stops. Then moves again.

KAITO: What are you even writing?

SELA: What happened. Who did it. What I thought of them.

KAITO: We're in a CAGE.

SELA: Then it's a short entry.

JUNO: Put me down as blameless.

SELA: I've put you down as asleep.

JUNO: (eyes still shut) What do we go for? At dawn. Roughly.

MARO: You personally, Miss Calloway? Less than the bird.

JUNO: (sitting up) Less than the—

MARO: The Sever pay by the core. Not by the charm.

JUNO: Sweetheart, I have been undervalued in far better rooms than this.

BRAM: What's the beetle worth?

MARO: To them? Very little. He sleeps.

BRAM: He's worth a lot to me.

MARO: I don't doubt it, Mr Torvald. I'm not selling him to you.

Kaito turns and points at the floor, at nothing, at everyone, the way he does.

KAITO: I said it. First day. First HOUR. I said that man would sell us.

JUNO: You said he'd sell us for a hat.

KAITO: And?

JUNO: It's not a hat, sweetheart. It's eleven. Credit where it's due, that's a real number.

MARO: (pleased, to his page) Eleven animals. One night. Out of a pen I'd already paid for. Eleven has been Mr Moreau's account ever since. Four of you, three in iron, one in the harbour: eight. Mr Moreau and his owl: ten.

BRAM: That's ten.

MARO: It is. He's one short. Mr Moreau was always one short. It's why he had an account.

KAITO: And what happens to us? After you've counted?

MARO: I don't ask. A ledger has two columns, Mr Brask. In, and out. What happens between them isn't a column.

KAITO: So he walks, and we go out your door at dawn, and that's the deal he made. Smiling.

MARO: The Sever pay at dawn. They like a count in daylight. (writing) He did smile. He has lovely manners for a thief.

Silence but for the pen.

SELA: He fed Grist.

KAITO: WHAT?

SELA: Fourth night out of Gaptooth. I saw it through the galley hatch. He gave Grist a sausage and told him he was beautiful.

BRAM: (misty, immediately) He never told me.

SELA: He wouldn't. He'd tell the beetle.

KAITO: Oh, he's nice to a BEETLE. Wonderful. He sold us to a bookkeeper—

MARO: Ledger.

KAITO: Nobody asked you!

BRAM: (quiet, hands still folded) He had thirds.

KAITO: Course he had thirds. He knew he wasn't paying.

BRAM: Asked me how I get the stew to go thick. Nobody asks that. (a beat) Nobody who eats like that is all bad.

KAITO: Bram. He's a smuggler. He lies for a living. He lied to you about STEW.

BRAM: You can't lie about thirds. Your plate's empty or it isn't.

JUNO: Five to one he'd sell us. I chalked it on the mast in Gaptooth, in front of everyone. Nobody took it. Nobody. I've got a whole night's work on there and no one to collect from.

Bram says nothing. His beard moves in something that might be a smile.

Sela closes the logbook on one finger and looks up at Kaito, quite gently.

SELA: I wrote down that I liked him, pup. First night. In ink. I'd like to know if I was wrong. I don't get many chances to check.

Kaito opens his mouth. Shuts it. Sits down hard beside Bram.

KAITO: ...You're all mad.

By the doors, the last lamp in the left-hand row goes out. Only Maro notices, and he doesn't look up.

## SCENE 3 — INT. MARO'S WAREHOUSE — CONTINUOUS (≈ 1:40)

The second lamp goes out. Then the third. No wind. No sound. A flame, then not a flame, and the black at the end of the hall takes one more step toward the desk.

A clerk (Ansel, 37, thin, a cudgel, ink-stained cuffs) peers down the hall.

ANSEL: Mr Maro. Lamps.

MARO: (writing) Yes.

ANSEL: Lamps don't go out on their own, Mr Maro.

MARO: Nothing does. That'll be him. Go and see. Take Hob.

Ansel and another man walk into the black with a lantern. Then it goes out in Ansel's hand with nothing touching it. Two shapes. One shape. None.

SFX: a cudgel rolling on stone. Nothing else

JUNO: (sitting up) Okay.

KAITO: (on his feet, bars in both hands) It's him. It's HIM. He's come to watch!

Lamp four. Lamp five.

KAITO: SILAS! I know it's you! Come to collect?! Dawn's in an hour, you can watch us go for FREE!

JUNO: Twenty to one he's here for his cut.

SELA: (still writing) Thirty to one he isn't.

JUNO: You don't do odds.

SELA: I do tonight.

A lamp near the cage swings once, for no reason, and shows him: Silas between two crates, chain-blade loose, shirt open, no smile on him at all. Looking straight at Kaito.

He opens his mouth to answer.

SILAS: (nothing)

His hand goes to his throat. He tries again, reaching for a word that has always been there.

SILAS: (nothing. The lamp swings back and he's gone.)

KAITO: He's just standing there. He's just STANDING there!

JUNO: ...Is he doing a bit?

SELA: (very quietly) No.

She has stood up. Logbook shut. Her face gone still.

SELA: He's spent it.

KAITO: Spent WHAT?

SELA: His voice, pup. That's what Hush costs him. (a beat) He's paid it to get in the door.

Tull's hook flares white. He steps toward the dark, blade up.

TULL: SHOW YOURSELF! Say something, you little—

The chain comes out of the black and wraps his wrist three times. He's pulled into the shadow as if it were water. The hook clatters on the stone, still glowing; something soft and winged passes over it, and it's gone too.

SFX: one clatter. Then the hush

Skarn, back still turned, lifts his head, sniffs once, and puts it down with enormous disdain.

Maro turns a page.

MARO: Mr Moreau. I'm told you can't say anything. I'll do the talking, then. I always did.

## SCENE 4 — INT. MARO'S WAREHOUSE — CONTINUOUS (≈ 2:30)

Half the hall is black. The lamps go faster. Maro's men (eight, thirties and forties, cudgels and two bond-cutters) back toward the light in a ring, shouting into the dark and getting nothing back.

LEDGER-MAN: Where IS he? SAY SOMETHING!

Another lamp goes. The man under it isn't there any more.

KAITO: (at the bars, pointing) LEFT! He's on your LEFT— no, HIS left— does he know his left?!

BRAM: (gentle) He knows.

JUNO: Behind the barrels! Behind the— oh. Oh, he's got it. Never mind.

JUNO: Eight men, one of him, and he can't shout for help. I'm not giving odds on this. I'm just watching.

A swinging lamp: Silas by the cage for one second, one finger to his lips. Gone.

JUNO: Did he just SHUSH us? We're the ones in a CAGE.

KAITO: SAY SOMETHING! Say one smug thing so I know it's really you, you—

SELA: Pup. He can't.

KAITO: (quieter) He came back with no voice.

SELA: He came back.

Kaito stops. Across the floor a hook glows on the stone; a hand picks it up and puts it out against a boot. A wrapped forearm. A sleeve that was white once.

Maro caps his inkwell.

MARO: Mr Tull isn't answering, so. Mr Ansel. No. (he counts on his fingers, then raises his voice, only slightly) Whoever's left. The doors, please.

Two men haul on a chain. The loading doors roll open and grey dawn comes across the floor like water.

MUSIC: one low note, held

Crates. Fallen men. Dead lamps. Silas in the open, twenty paces from the desk, chain-blade in hand, in daylight like a card turned face up. Hush beside him, wings half open.

MARO: Lamp-cutting is a trick, Mr Moreau. You showed me yourself, the night of the eleven. I've had a long time to think about doors.

Six men close on him, two hooks lit. He can't take six in daylight; his face knows it. He looks at Maro. Then past him, at the cage.

He breathes in.

SELA: (soft) Don't—

He breathes out. The lines on his neck go white-violet. A wind lays every flame flat and lifts the dust in a sheet, and the chain-blade goes out of his hand, not at Maro, not at the men, but the length of the hall, singing, and takes the cage's three hinge pins off like the heads off flowers.

SFX: tink. tink. tink. KRANNNG

The door falls flat. Silas is on one knee in the dust, hand at his throat, coughing with no sound. Hush's wings close round him.

Kaito is out before the door's stopped ringing.

MARO: (not raising his voice) Begin the count on the wolf, please.

The man nearest Skarn snatches up Tull's hook and sets it at the wolf's chest; his mouth opens on "One—" and a crack of yellow takes the hook out of his hand and him off his feet.

SFX: KRAK

Kaito, gauntlet smoking, lands on the pen and tears at the chain muzzle.

KAITO: Up. UP. Skarn, I swear, if you make me say please—

Skarn stands, shakes, and bites through the bars like bread. His yellow eyes find Silas; his ears flatten in total disgust. Then he walks at the men.

Juno bends Ashka's cage door with both gloved hands until it screams. Ashka explodes out in sparks onto her shoulder; the gloves are already smoking.

JUNO: Right. Who's betting?

Two men come at Bram with cudgels; the iron climbs his arms grey to the shoulder and the cudgels bounce off like dropped pans. He looks only at the net.

SELA: Bram. The net.

BRAM: I know. (to the net) Grist.

SFX: zzzzRRNK

BRAM: ...Vegetable.

One eye opens. Six iron legs. The anchors go over like skittles, the net goes up on his horn like a veil, and the men go away, fast, in several directions.

JUNO: (counting on her fingers as she throws) Three left. Two. Oh. One.

Fast cuts, no music: Skarn through a crate stack; Ashka's fire knocking a hook from a hand; Sela, unhurried, lifting her spyglass off Maro's side table; the last man out the doors at a run.

Quiet. Dust in the dawn. Six people, four animals. One desk. One lamp, still lit.

## SCENE 5 — INT. MARO'S WAREHOUSE, THE DESK — DAWN (≈ 1:30)

Maro has not moved. He closes the first account book. Then the second. He takes off his spectacles, folds them, sets them on the third.

Silas gets up. The chain comes back into his hand link by link and drags behind him to the desk: the first sound he has made all night. Iron on stone, slow, ringing.

SFX: shhhk... shhhk... shhhk

Kaito takes a step; Bram's hand lands on his shoulder, and he stays.

KAITO: (low) He's going to—

BRAM: Not ours.

Maro looks up at Silas as if he's come to settle a small account, late, and that's fine.

MARO: You've cost me eleven, and a floor of good men, and you've done it twice. (a beat) I'll tell you what I told you the first time, because you weren't listening then either. You don't undo a sale by burning the receipt.

Silas stops at the desk. The blade turns slowly at the end of its chain.

MARO: Those eleven are on a chart in Meridian with your name beside them, in a hand much neater than mine. The Sever know what you were. I know what you were. (his eyes go past Silas, to the four by the cage, and come back) And now they know.

He folds his hands on the closed books.

MARO: (soft, kind, utterly without fear) So. Who will you tell?

Silas's face. Nothing in it. Nothing at all.

He raises the chain-blade.

The lamp.

## SCENE 6 — EXT. LANTERNREACH, THE QUAY — DAWN (≈ 2:00)

Grey light. The Gullwake at the quay, a slow swell beside her where Vesh turns over. Along the harbour the lamps go out as the sky comes up, as if the town were doing it on purpose.

The crew come out between the warehouses. Grist first, still in the net. Bram carrying Juno's crate of winnings, of course. Sela with three account books under her arm.

Silas last, Hush at his side. He walks like a man who has carried something heavy a long time and only now noticed.

Sela stops him with a look.

SELA: Is he—

Silas looks at her. His mouth moves. Nothing. He looks away, at the water.

JUNO: (watching him) Huh. That's the one question he can't talk his way out of, and he's still got out of it.

Nobody laughs. Sela writes, walking.

SELA (V.O.): (writing) Didn't ask twice. Some bills you don't read back.

Juno sits on the crate by the gangplank, pulls off one smoking glove with her teeth, looks at her blistered palm, and puts the glove back on before anyone's close enough to see.

JUNO: Five to one he'd sell us. Chalked. Public. Nobody took it. I'm out a night's work.

BRAM: I took it.

JUNO: You did not.

BRAM: Chalked it on the stove, in the galley. Small. "Bram: he won't."

JUNO: Sweetheart. He DID.

BRAM: And then he came and got us. (he thinks about it) Doesn't count as sold if you get it back. That's a loan.

JUNO: (a long look at him) ...Pay you tonight.

BRAM: (misty) And he had thirds.

KAITO: (passing) You two are betting on whether we got SOLD.

JUNO: Sweetheart, I'd bet on the tide if the tide took markers.

JUNO: (to Sela, passing) What'd you write?

SELA: Mind your business.

JUNO: Odds it's about him?

SELA: Evens.

Across the quay, Silas sits on a bollard, Hush at his feet, and takes the tally slate from his sash. He looks at it a while before he writes.

INSERT: the slate, in a slanting, elegant, slightly shaky hand. "Darling, I was never going to"

The chalk stops. He looks at the words. Then he wipes them out with the heel of his hand, slowly, until the slate is clean, sets it face down on the stones, and sits looking at nothing.

Kaito has watched from the gangplank, hands in his pockets. Sela comes to the rail above him.

KAITO: (not looking up) You're letting him on.

SELA: I'm not. You are.

KAITO: Why me?

SELA: You hated him first. You've got seniority.

KAITO: That's not how—

SELA: It's how it is on my boat. I wrote it down.

He walks over, not quickly. Picks up the slate. Turns it over.

Silas looks up. Doesn't try to speak. Waits for it.

Kaito holds the slate out to him.

KAITO: Your handwriting's shit.

Silas takes it.

KAITO: (already walking back) Get on the boat.

A beat. Silas looks at the Gullwake: Sela at the rail hiding a smile behind her book; Juno counting coins into Bram's palm one at a time; Grist lying across the gangplank with the net still on, so nobody can get aboard anyway.

SELA: (over the rail, not looking) Pup. Rope.

KAITO: I'm GETTING the rope.

SELA (V.O.): (writing) Silas Moreau. Sold us. Came back. Can't talk. (a beat) Best he's ever been.

Silas stands. Hush goes up the gangplank over Grist, no sound. At the top its flat face turns toward the far quay, and the knife-edged feathers along its back lift, one row, then another. Silas follows the look. Nothing there but the last lamps going out, and the grey.

KAITO: (coming back with the rope) What? What's he looking at?

SILAS: (nothing)

KAITO: ...Right. Course. Very helpful.

He throws the rope.
