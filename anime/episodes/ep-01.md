## COLD OPEN

## SCENE 1 — EXT. PINHOLD ROOFTOPS — LATE AFTERNOON (≈ 1:20)

Black. SFX: boots on slate, fast. A brass whistle. Then light.

A steep island town stacked up a cliff: slate roofs, washing lines, a grey harbour below. Terraces that should be green are the colour of ash.

KAITO BRASK, 21, runs the ridgeline of a roof with a canvas parcel under one arm. Black sleeveless zip top half unzipped, red flight jacket tied round his waist by the sleeves, brass goggles pushed up on his forehead, one white streak in shaggy black hair. Scars shaped like lightning run from the back of his right hand to his elbow.

Above him, SKARN, a storm wolf the size of a horse, rides the updraft, the sail-fin down his spine crackling. Slate-grey fur, yellow eyes, forked tail.

Behind: two men in blue-grey Empire customs coats, slipping on tiles.

KAITO: (not slowing, to the sky, to nobody) First map of the Drift that stays right. Mine. Say it back.
SKARN: (a grumble)
KAITO: You never say it back.

A gap between buildings. He jumps it without looking. A tile goes, clatters into an alley.

DOV LINNET, 24, customs runner, young, all elbows, lands the gap badly, knees first.

DOV: Stop! In the name of the, the—
KAITO: (over his shoulder) Finish the sentence, Dov, I'll wait!
DOV: The Harbour Authority!
KAITO: Screw the Harbour Authority!

In the street below, HARBOUR-CLERK TEODOR HASK, 44, round, red-faced, brass whistle on a cord, keeps pace with the grim determination of a man who has never caught anything.

HASK: (whistle, then) Brask! That parcel is impounded property!
KAITO: It's medicine!
HASK: It's unlicensed medicine!
KAITO: Lungs don't check the licence!
HASK: Everything checks the licence now! That's the whole point of a licence!
KAITO: Then write me one! You've got the pen!
HASK: I've got the whistle! (he blows it, pointlessly)

He runs a wall-top, arms out. Skarn's shadow crosses him. He grins up at it.

KAITO: Skarn. Washing line. Watch this.
SKARN: (a short, doubtful bark)
KAITO: It'll hold. It's rope. Rope holds.

He jumps for a washing line, meaning to swing across. It snaps. He drops into a yard of washing, comes up wearing a bedsheet, keeps running in the bedsheet.

KAITO: Didn't watch that. Watch the next one.
SKARN: (a huff that is clearly laughter)

Kaito takes the last roof at a dead sprint and goes off the end into open air, the harbour a long way down.

Skarn folds a wing and drops under him. Kaito lands on his back, parcel and bedsheet and all, and they glide out over the water.

Dov arrives at the roof edge and stops with his toes over nothing.

DOV: (winded, to himself) He's got a wolf. Why do I never remember he's got a wolf.
HASK: (from the street, far below) Did you get him?
DOV: (calling down) He flew off, sir.
HASK: (a long pause) Right. Well. Write that down.

Wide, from above: Pinhold entire, and on the far headland something new and wrong. A tower of black iron and white stone driven into the island's rock, braced by cables as thick as houses. Grey-coated men at its base, tiny from here.

KAITO: (quieter, looking at it) Yeah. Still there.

## PART A

## SCENE 2 — EXT. AMA KETTLE'S YARD, UPPER TERRACES — LATE AFTERNOON (≈ 2:00)

A yard of hard grey dirt. A stone well with a bucket. Hens. Skarn lands on the yard wall, cracks a flagstone, and lies down on it as if that was the plan.

Kaito drops into the yard, pulls the bedsheet off, folds it, badly.

AMA KETTLE, 70, small, brown, a face made of creases, comes out drying her hands. She breathes shallow, with effort.

AMA: You're wearing Pell Oduya's bedsheet.
KAITO: I'm returning Pell Oduya's bedsheet. Later. (he tears the parcel open, pulls out a brown glass bottle) Here. Two spoons morning, two at night. Not with brandy.
AMA: Does it say not with brandy?
KAITO: I say not with brandy.
AMA: You're not a doctor.
KAITO: I'm the one who fetched it. That outranks a doctor.
AMA: (sniffing the bottle) Smells like a boot.
KAITO: Good. Means it's working.
AMA: (taking it, holding it to her chest a second) Three weeks that sat in the customs house.
KAITO: Three weeks it sat on a shelf with a stamp on it saying it couldn't move till a man in Meridian said so. Same as the island. Same as everything.

She hands him a cloth bundle. He opens it. Six eggs. One cracked.

KAITO: Ama. Five and a half eggs.
AMA: Six.
KAITO: One's leaking.
AMA: That's what I've got.
KAITO: I ran across the roofs of this entire town.
AMA: You'd have done it for none.
KAITO: (beat) ...Yeah.

He pockets the eggs like they're worth something. She drops the well bucket. The rope runs out. SFX: a hollow knock, far down. No splash.

AMA: (cheerful, not looking round) That one's gone too. Have to go down to Pell's for water now. Mind, Pell's is going.

Kaito looks at the well. Says nothing.

AMA: Come in. You've a face on you.

CUT TO:

INT. AMA'S KITCHEN — CONTINUOUS

Low ceiling, one lamp. One whole wall is a chart: Pinhold and its neighbours, inked and re-inked, a lifetime of pencil corrections, each one dated. The corrections stop three weeks ago.

AMA: Look at that. Look. Fifty years I've moved Pinhold on that wall every morning, and three weeks it's not needed a touch. Right as a church. First time in my life a map's told me the truth.
KAITO: (looking at it) Yeah.
AMA: You don't sound pleased. You're the map boy.
KAITO: I'm pleased.
AMA: Your dad would have been pleased.
KAITO: (a nothing beat) He'd have said the pencil's wrong. (he taps the island on the chart) It's not right because anyone got good at drawing. It's right because it stopped.
AMA: And a good thing. Ships can find us. My cousin's boy can send a letter that arrives.
KAITO: Wells don't die on an island that's moving, Ama.
AMA: Wells die. Wells have always died.
KAITO: Not six in a month. Not all on the one side.
AMA: (a pause, then brisk) Two spoons morning and night. I heard you. Go on.
KAITO: Ama—
AMA: I'm seventy years old and the map's right. Let me have it.

He goes. At the door he looks back at the chart. So correct it looks like a headstone.

## SCENE 3 — EXT. PINHOLD HIGH STREET — DUSK (≈ 1:15)

Kaito walks downhill, Skarn padding the rooftops above him, leaping gap to gap. Every open door shows a chart on a wall. Every one of them still.

The street ends at the harbour square; past it, the PYLON WORKS: a fenced yard of iron crates, the tower above, cables tight in the wind. Not humming. Not yet.

On every crate, stencilled white: a straight line cutting a circle.

Grey-coated men at the fence. Long coats, white collars, a black bar across the chest. Not customs. Something else. One of them, a SEVER PYLON OFFICER, 47, turns his head as Kaito passes, follows Skarn with his eyes, says nothing.

Kaito meets the look and holds it until it's stupid to. Above, Skarn growls, low.

KAITO: (under his breath) I know. I don't like them either.

Hask is waiting at the corner, hands on his knees, purple.

HASK: (gasping) Brask.
KAITO: Clerk Hask. You ran. I'm proud of you.
HASK: You are. Banned. From the harbour.
KAITO: I live at the harbour.
HASK: You live in a lighthouse that isn't a lighthouse with a wolf that isn't legal.
KAITO: He's got papers.
HASK: He ate the papers.
KAITO: So he's got them.

Hask straightens, with effort. Lower now, actual worry, a glance at the grey coats.

HASK: They've asked me for a list. Every bonded animal on the island. Name, owner, where it sleeps.
KAITO: (beat) Who's they? Customs?
HASK: Customs is me, and I didn't ask. (a nod at the grey coats, not looking at them) Them.
KAITO: What do they want with a list?
HASK: I'm a clerk, Brask. I don't get told what. I get told when. (he puts the whistle away) And I'm a very slow writer. Deliver the rest of that parcel and get that wolf off the skyline, you idiot.
KAITO: Hask.
HASK: What.
KAITO: Thanks.
HASK: Don't. I'll still fine you.

He walks off. Kaito watches him go. Then looks at the parcel, at a tag tied on it in a neat hand: S. VEY — GULLWAKE — EAST MOORING.

## SCENE 4 — EXT. EAST MOORING — DUSK (≈ 2:15)

The far end of the harbour, the cheap berths. One ship: the GULLWAKE, battered, broad, patched in three colours of timber, no sail up, no tow-line out, sitting lower than she should. Under the surface at her bow, a line of soft lights, too long and too deep to belong to any harbour. Then gone.

Kaito walks the mooring. Skarn stays up on a warehouse roof, watching the water, ears flat.

A woman sits on the Gullwake's rail writing in a worn logbook. SELA VEY, 28. Tall, dark brown skin, sea-green eyes, long silver-blue hair in a high ponytail, gold hoops. A short deep-blue navigator's jacket with brass buttons over a black bandeau, bare midriff, a wrap skirt slit at the side, strapped boots, a brass spyglass on her hip. Just below her left collarbone a small spiral mark glows faint teal.

She does not look up.

SELA: You're the one who shouts.
KAITO: What?
SELA: On the roofs. "First map of the Drift that stays right. Mine." Half the island heard it. I wrote it down. (she taps the logbook) It's good. Needs work.
KAITO: It doesn't need— (he stops at the gangplank and holds up the parcel) S. Vey?
SELA: Sela. (she closes the book and looks at him properly, and takes her time about it) And you're the courier. You're younger than the shouting.
KAITO: I'm twenty-one.
SELA: I didn't ask. (a small smile) You're staring, courier.
KAITO: I'm not. I'm reading your... jacket.
SELA: It has buttons. Count them, if you need something to do with your eyes.
KAITO: (ears already red) Six.
SELA: Seven. One's under the collar. You stopped early.
KAITO: I need to get paid, is what I need.
SELA: And I need my parcel, so one of us has to move. (she pats the rail beside her) Come up, then. I don't bite. Not on a first delivery.

He steps for the plank, looking at her and not at the plank. A mooring post is between him and the ship.

SFX: a clean wooden THOCK.

Kaito flat on his back on the dock. The eggs, by a miracle, held clear above his chest in one hand.

SELA: (not moving, warm) The post was there when you arrived.
KAITO: (to the sky) Screw the post.
SELA: Are the eggs all right?
KAITO: Eggs are fine.
SELA: Good. I was worried about the eggs.

Cut to Skarn on the warehouse roof: the most disgusted face a wolf has ever made. He lies down with his back to the whole business.

Sela, writing in the logbook without looking down at it.

SELA: "Courier arrived. Walked into a post. The post is fine."

[EYECATCH: Skarn asleep on a chimney stack. A Pal, a round palm-sized limpet-thing with two big eyes, is trying to climb his forked tail and keeps sliding off.]

## PART B

## SCENE 5 — EXT. GULLWAKE DECK, EAST MOORING — NIGHT FALLING (≈ 2:00)

Lanterns lit. Kaito on a crate, a wet rag on his forehead. Sela slits the parcel and lifts out yellowed survey sheets: soundings, tiny neat figures, coastlines drawn over themselves forty times in forty positions.

KAITO: That's what customs had a three-week fit about? Old survey?
SELA: (reading, not looking up) The Empire doesn't like old maps. Old maps tell you where things were.
KAITO: Where things were is useless. Everything's moved.
SELA: Draw a line from where it was to where it is.
KAITO: (beat) ...You've got a direction.
SELA: (looks up) You've got a direction. Hm. So you don't only shout.
KAITO: I shout and I think. I can do both.
SELA: Not at once, I'd guess.
KAITO: (opens his mouth, shuts it) ...No.
SELA: Honest. I'll put that in the book too.
KAITO: What is that book?
SELA: Everything that happens to me. (she doesn't elaborate; she turns a page of the survey instead) Forty years of this island's wanderings, in a dead man's ink. Beautiful.
KAITO: You don't know he's dead.
SELA: Surveyors who stay alive keep the sheets. These were sold.

He comes to look over her shoulder and forgets to be embarrassed. A stamp in one corner: MERIDIAN SURVEY OFFICE, a chain-and-sextant mark, a surveyor's number.

Kaito goes still.

SELA: You know the mark.
KAITO: Everybody knows the mark.
SELA: (watching him) No. They don't.

He doesn't answer. She lets it go. She does not write that one down. She looks at the page, then at him, and closes the book.

SELA: What does a courier want with a map that stays right?
KAITO: Nothing. Everything. (he waves at the town, the harbour, the dark headland) Everybody on this island's got a chart on the wall. They move the island on it every morning. Fifty years of pencil. The maps always lied, so people quit minding. I mind.
SELA: And now they're right.
KAITO: Now they're right. (he looks at the headland) And the wells are dead and the birds have gone and everyone's saying what a relief.
SELA: (quiet) The Empire pins an island, the island holds still. That's the whole pitch. Safe harbour. Reliable post.
KAITO: You believe it?
SELA: I came here to buy old maps of an island that doesn't need them anymore. (she pays him: coins, counted, and one more) Draw your own conclusions. You seem to want to draw everything else.
KAITO: I don't need conclusions. I need a boat that goes where I point.
SELA: Everyone needs one of those. Nobody's got one.
KAITO: How does a ship with no sail up and no tow out get into a harbour?
SELA: Slowly. Why does a courier walk around with a storm wolf the Harbour Authority would love to have a word with?
KAITO: He walks around with me. I don't get a say.
SELA: No. (something almost gentle) You don't, do you. None of us do.

He looks at the extra coin.

KAITO: What's this one for?
SELA: The post.

He turns to go. Stops.

KAITO: How'd you know I was the courier? From the shouting?
SELA: From the wolf. Nobody else on this island is stupid enough to keep one in plain sight this month. (beat) I like stupid. It tells you who's still honest.

He goes down the plank. Carefully. He looks at the post as he passes it. The post is fine.

SELA: (calling after him, lightly) Courier. If you're going to shout it, the line is "a map that stays right." Not "the." You haven't drawn it yet.
KAITO: (not turning round) It's "the" when I draw it!
SELA: (to herself, writing) He's going to be insufferable.

Behind the ship, under the dark water, the line of lights passes again. Slow. Very long. She puts a hand flat on the rail, the way you'd quiet a dog: I know. Hush.

## SCENE 6 — EXT. THE ROOST, WEST RIDGE — NIGHT (≈ 1:45)

A dead signal tower on the ridge above town, lamp gone, top floor roofless: Skarn's roost, a nest of sailcloth and stolen blankets. On the one standing wall, Kaito's own chart of Pinhold, erased and redrawn a hundred times, pencil ghosts of every old position. The current ink is three weeks old and perfect.

A small fire. Kaito cracks five eggs into a pan. Then the sixth, cracked one, carefully.

KAITO: Two for you, three for me, half for whatever bird's going to steal it anyway.
SKARN: (a rumble)
KAITO: You had a goat Tuesday.
SKARN: (a longer rumble)
KAITO: Half a goat is a goat. Nobody eats half a goat and calls it a snack.

He pokes the eggs. Skarn watches the pan the way wolves watch pans.

KAITO: She said my line's wrong. "A map," not "the map." (beat) She's wrong. (beat) She's probably not wrong. She's got a face like she's never been wrong in her life. You see her face?
SKARN: (a huff)
KAITO: I wasn't staring. I was reading the buttons. Seven. I counted.

He eats from the pan with his knife. Looks at the chart. Gets up with a pencil, puts it to the island. Doesn't move it. Nothing to move.

KAITO: (to the chart) Go on. Move. (nothing) ...It's right.

He lowers the pencil.

KAITO: Why does that feel like shit?

Skarn lifts his head, ears up, towards the headland. A low sound that isn't a growl. More like a question.

INTERCUT — EXT. PYLON WORKS — NIGHT

Grey coats in rows, floodlamps up. At the pylon's foot, a house-sized ring of white stone with a black line cut across it. The Pylon Officer at a lever.

PYLON OFFICER: Anchor One. Hold is sound. On the Director's word.

He pulls.

The cables take the strain. The white ring lights from inside, slow as a lamp turned up.

SFX: a hum. Not heard. Felt. Below hearing: three slow pulses, long apart. (The Undersong. The same cue every time it returns.)

BACK AT THE ROOST

The water in the bucket by the fire rings in rings. The pan skitters. Skarn is on his feet, every hair on the sail-fin standing, lightning walking along it. Not attacking. Not hiding. Listening.

Kaito's right hand. The lightning-fork mark on the back of it glows yellow, unasked.

KAITO: (not frightened, exactly) ...What is that.

Below, in Pinhold, the hens all go quiet at once. No dog barks. A hundred charts on a hundred walls, right.

MUSIC: one held note, low strings.

Kaito, useless pencil in one hand, glowing hand at his side, looks at the headland.

KAITO: (to Skarn, very plainly) I don't think the island's being pinned. I think it's being held down.

Skarn looks at him. Then back at the tower. The hum sinks. Not gone. Lower.

KAITO: Something under there felt that. Something big. You felt it too, I saw you. (he looks at his hand; the glow fades) Everyone in this town's going to sleep fine tonight. Charts right. Post on time.

He puts the pencil down on the sill, carefully, like it might still be needed.

KAITO: First map of the Drift that stays right. Mine. (quieter) Say it back.

Skarn, for the first time ever, lowers his head and pushes it into Kaito's chest. Kaito stands there with both hands up and no idea what to do with them.

KAITO: (muffled) Okay. Okay. That's not saying it back.

[ENDING: "Logbook" — 60 s, held over the following scene]

## SCENE 7 — EXT. THE CLIFF ABOVE THE ROOST — NIGHT (≈ 0:40, inside the ending)

Higher than the roost. Bare rock, sea wind.

A shape against the stars. A hound the size of a cart, standing dead still, cables running down its flanks like veins, a white core glowing in its chest where a heart would show. Blank eyes.

On it, a rider. Long ragged black coat. A smooth white mask with one eye slit.

SILT, 29. He looks down at the broken tower, the small fire, the wolf's crackling fin, the boy in red.

He does not move for a long time. The hound does not breathe.

SILT: (flat, tired, almost kind) Found you.

Hold on the mask. Cut to black.
