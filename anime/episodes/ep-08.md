## COLD OPEN

## SCENE 1 — INT. CINDER PALACE — FOYER — NIGHT (≈ 1:15)

Black. Then the slow creak of a very expensive door.

BRAM (V.O.): Galley rules. Recap edition.

Soot-lantern light. The crew stands in the foyer of the Cinder Palace, dressed up and frozen mid-step. Hold on them while Bram talks.

BRAM (V.O.): One. We came to Ashmarket for parts. Two. Juno played cards with Kaito and Kaito lost his shirt. Three. A man called Krell bought her debts, and if she can't pay by Ember Night he takes her bird. Four. We put on nice clothes to rob a casino. Five. The man at the door knew our faces.

A beat.

BRAM (V.O.): Six. Nobody told me the vest was a medium.

SFX: one vest button hitting marble, rolling, stopping

Reverse: HOLLIS LUSK, 61, owner of the Cinder Palace, small, powdered, rings on every finger, holds a fan of four grey Sever notices. Four faces, drawn well. Sela's is flattering. Kaito's isn't.

LUSK: Captain Vey. Miss Calloway. The large one. And this must be the boy who lost his shirt.
KAITO: It was a bet.
LUSK: It was a Tuesday, dear. Everyone saw.

Sela steps up. Midnight-blue gown, backless, high slit; she wears it like a coat she's owned for years. She takes the notice with her face on it and looks it over.

SELA: They've given me a kinder nose.
LUSK: The Sever are romantics. (he lowers the fan) Warden Krell has paid for the High Table tonight. All of it. He asked for an audience.
JUNO: And?
LUSK: And you're the audience, Miss Calloway. (he steps aside) The House doesn't care who loses. Only that somebody does.
SELA: And if the audience leaves early?
LUSK: Then the Warden has paid a great deal of money to sit by himself, and I've had a lovely evening either way.

Juno in sparkling red, gloves on, Ashka on her shoulder trailing sparks on the carpet. She looks at the open doors, then at the crew.

JUNO: Six to one we walk out of there.
KAITO: With the money?
JUNO: I said walk out, sweetheart. Don't get greedy.

She goes in. Kaito tugs at a waistcoat two sizes too small.

KAITO: (to Bram) Does mine look tight?
BRAM: Like a sausage that's proud of itself.

## PART A

## SCENE 2 — INT. CINDER PALACE — HIGH TABLE ROOM — NIGHT (≈ 2:30)

A long room under a ceiling of soot-black glass. One table, a ring of lamps, a gallery of rich strangers behind a brass rail. By the hearth, boots up on a low iron coffee table, sits WARDEN KRELL, 41, 195 cm, a heavy officer's coat open over a shield-plate, a drink in a fist like a mallet. The coffee table is Grist. Nobody has noticed. There is a card on him that says TABLE.

In the gallery, NIM HADDA, 30, one-armed, gentle-faced, chalks odds on a slate with his one hand and a lot of practice.

NIM: Lady in red to beat the House: eleven to one. Lady in red to last an hour: four to one. Boy in the waistcoat to survive the waistcoat: even money.
GALLERY PATRON (50s, sweating): And the big fella?
NIM: The big fella's a waiter. We don't bet on staff. (beat) Twenty to one on the vest.

Bram passes behind him with a tray, the waiter's vest straining at every seam. He doesn't look at Nim. The vest creaks.

At the table: the dealer, FENNICK, 36, narrow, neat, a trooper's haircut under a dealer's cap, shuffles a tall deck without looking at it. Juno sits. Sela takes the chair at her left. Kaito stands behind Juno with his hands behind his back like a man at a funeral.

FENNICK: High Table. The game is Cinders. The House plays the Warden's purse.
JUNO: Fennick, is it? (an elbow on the felt) Do you deal as slow as you look at me, or is that just for the table?

Fennick doesn't look at her. Doesn't blink. Deals.

Sela opens her logbook on the felt beside her chips and writes one line.

KAITO: (whisper) What's Cinders?
NIM: (from the rail, not whispering) Two cards down, one up. You want the biggest pile under thirty. Every round you can pay the House to pull one more card, or sit. Over thirty you're ash; you lose. At the end the biggest pile takes the pot. Ties go to the House.
KAITO: That's it?
NIM: That's it. Everything else is lying.
KAITO: And the dealer plays too?
NIM: The dealer is the House. The House is the Warden's purse tonight. So when she loses, who gets paid?
KAITO: ...Krell.
NIM: There's the boy. Two to one he learns another thing before midnight.

The cards: Juno's up card, the ash, is a 7. Fennick's is a 10. Juno lifts her two hidden cards a hair. Kaito, behind her, sees them. His whole face lights up like a lamp.

JUNO: (not turning) Kaito.
KAITO: What.
JUNO: Look at the ceiling.
KAITO: I'm not doing anyth—
JUNO: Ceiling.

He looks at the ceiling. The gallery murmurs. Nim rubs out a number and writes a worse one.

SELA: (writing) First hand. The pup has a good one, and so does the whole room.

Fennick stokes: pays the fee, draws one more card to himself, face down. Juno sits. Showdown. Juno turns 7, 9, 8. Twenty-four. Fennick turns 10, 8, 4, 3. Twenty-five.

FENNICK: House.

The chips go. Krell, at the hearth, doesn't smile. He lifts his glass an inch.

KRELL: Again, Calloway. I didn't pay for one hand.
JUNO: You didn't pay for me either, Warden. You bought a bit of paper with my name on it. Different thing.
KRELL: Paper is what the Sever runs on. Paper and a count of ten.
JUNO: (to Fennick) Deal, before he gets poetic.

Juno looks at Sela. Sela is watching Fennick's hands, not his face. She writes a second line.

## SCENE 3 — INT. HIGH TABLE ROOM — LATER (≈ 2:45)

Hands go by in quick cuts. Lamps burn lower.

Juno: twenty-two. Fennick: twenty-three. House.
Juno: twenty-seven. Fennick: twenty-eight. House.
Juno stokes, stokes again, over thirty. Ash. House.

Between hands, from the hearth:

KRELL: Is this the famous Calloway? I was told she cheated an officer blind on this rock.
JUNO: I did. He was prettier than you. (beat) That's not the bar you think it is.

Bram, passing behind Kaito with a tray, low.

BRAM: Your face is leaking.
KAITO: It's my FACE.
BRAM: Put a lid on it. Like a pot.

The crew's parts money, a pile at the start, is now three chips and a bad feeling.

JUNO: (bright, too bright) Nineteen to one he's lucky. Four to one he's good.
SELA: (quiet) And the rest?
JUNO: The rest I'm not saying out loud in a room with his boots in it.

A patron at the rail tries his luck with Sela, leaning over her shoulder.

PATRON: You're wasted on cards, Captain.
SELA: (not turning) I'm working, and you're losing. (she writes) Go and lose somewhere else.

He does. Juno snorts. Kaito, behind her, has turned the colour of her dress.

KAITO: (hiss) Can we go. Can we please just go, and I'll hit somebody.
JUNO: Hit who, sweetheart? The dealer? He's got a boss. The boss? He's got a wall of men with shields outside. (she flicks a chip) Sit down. You're standing.
KAITO: You told me to stand.
JUNO: And now I'm telling you to sit and you're still standing, so that's two things you can't do.

Bram arrives with a tray. Sets water in front of Juno, very gently.

BRAM: Drink. You're going pink round the edges.
JUNO: I'm in red, Bram.
BRAM: Not the dress.

SFX: a seam going

BRAM: (not looking down) Vest's fine.

Sela lays her pen down. Leans close to Juno. Low; the gallery can't hear.

SELA: He has never looked at you.
JUNO: Thanks. I noticed. Flirted at him for an hour and nothing. I'm starting to take it personally.
SELA: No. Listen. A dealer reads the player. Every dealer in every den on this rock would've looked at your mouth by now, at the pup's face, at my hands, at your bird. He looks at the gallery. He looks at Krell. He hasn't looked at you once, because he doesn't need to.
JUNO: (a beat; the brightness goes) He knows what I've got.
SELA: He knows what you've got before you do. Cold deck, stacked at the shuffle. He's dealt you a loser for two hours and dealt himself one better, and he's drawn the exact card every time he's stoked. That isn't luck, and it isn't good.

Kaito has heard enough.

KAITO: Then we flip the table.
JUNO: With what? Your other waistcoat?
SELA: Then we die under a shield line, in formalwear, and Bram's vest never gets its moment.
BRAM: (passing) It won't last the night anyway.

Juno looks at her three chips. Then at Ashka, on the back of her chair, whose tail plumes have started to glow: a tell she can't glove over. She lifts her arm. The raptor goes up into the black glass rafters, grumbling.

JUNO: (half to herself) You can't beat a man at cards who's already read them. (she turns her chips over, one, two, three) So you don't play cards.
SELA: What do you play?
JUNO: (looking up at the hearth) Him.

She drags Kaito down by the waistcoat until his ear is at her mouth.

JUNO: Next hand, you don't look at the ceiling. You look where I show you. And you've got to believe me, Kaito, really believe me, the way you believe you can fly.
KAITO: I can fly.
JUNO: See, that. Exactly that.

She lets go. Raises her voice to the room.

JUNO: Fennick. Deal. (to the hearth) Warden. New game.

[EYECATCH: Grist, labelled TABLE, with three drinks and an ashtray balanced on his shell, one eye open.]

## PART B

## SCENE 4 — INT. HIGH TABLE ROOM — CONTINUOUS (≈ 3:30)

Fennick deals. Juno's ash: a 2. Fennick's ash: an 11. The gallery groans for her.

She does not touch her hidden cards.

FENNICK: Stakes.
JUNO: All of it. (she pushes the three chips in) Every penny the Gullwake has, which is these, and the Gullwake's dignity, which is him.

She points a thumb back at Kaito. His waistcoat chooses this moment.

SFX: a button snapping, flying, a small splash

Krell looks down into his glass. The button sinks.

KRELL: Was that a bet?
JUNO: It's a deposit.

Fennick stokes himself; draws, calm. Juno holds up one finger.

JUNO: Turn the coal.

A murmur. Nim, at the rail, stops chalking.

NIM: (to the patron) Any player can make the House burn the top card before they draw. Nobody does. Costs you the fee twice.
PATRON: Why not?
NIM: Because it's a waste of money, unless you think the deck's got an opinion.

Fennick looks, for the first time all night, uncertain: at Krell. Krell nods, bored. Fennick burns the top card face down into the ash tray. Deals Juno one more, face down. She leaves it face down. She hasn't looked at any of them.

SELA: (writing) She hasn't looked at her hand. I don't think she's going to.
KAITO: (whisper) Is that good?
SELA: Pup, it isn't even a strategy.

FENNICK: Stakes.
JUNO: I'm out of money. (a breath, the first slow one all night) So. The bird.

The room goes very quiet. Up in the rafters, one sound from Ashka, short and hot. Juno doesn't look up.

JUNO: Not Ember Night. Tonight. I lose, the Warden's man walks out of here with Ashka on a chain, and I hold the chain for him.

Krell's boots come off the coffee table. He sits forward.

KRELL: Say it again.
JUNO: You heard it the first time. You've wanted it since you bought my paper. I'm putting it on the felt.
KRELL: The Sever accepts. Fennick, write it down.
JUNO: (beat) And I'm not done.
SELA: (not looking up) Juno.
JUNO: The ship.

Nobody breathes. Sela finishes the word she is writing. Puts the pen down. Looks at Juno for a long moment.

SELA: Done.
LUSK: (from the door) The House notes a ship.
KAITO: WHAT.
SELA: The ship's the captain's to stake, pup, and I've just staked it. Sit down.
KAITO: I'm STANDING, everyone keeps—
JUNO: And the wolf.

Kaito stops. All the way stops.

KAITO: (very quiet) Say that again.
JUNO: Your wolf. On the roof. Skarn.
KAITO: You don't— that's not yours to—
JUNO: No. It's yours. (she holds her gloved hand out to him, palm up) So you put it there.

A long beat. Kaito looks at the hand. At Sela, who gives him nothing. At Krell, who is smiling now, a wide ugly soldier's smile, because this is the bet he came for: two partners on the table.

Kaito puts his hand in hers. Hard.

KAITO: If you lose I'm going to be sick on you.
JUNO: Fair.

She pulls him down. Lifts the edge of her hidden cards an inch, for him alone. We don't see them. Her mouth at his ear.

JUNO: (whisper) We've got him. Smile.

Kaito's face. He believes her the way he believes he can fly. Slow, enormous, stupid joy: a boy who has just been told the storm is on his side. Ears red. Eyes wet. He straightens up and tries to hide it and cannot.

Across the room, Krell is watching that face. Krell has spent twenty years reading soldiers' faces for lies, and that one has never held a lie in its life.

Krell's eyes go to Fennick. Fennick's go to the ash tray. The burned card. The one card in the deck he hasn't counted.

FENNICK: (barely) Warden. She can't have more than—
KRELL: (low) You told me the deck was safe.
FENNICK: It was. Until she—
KRELL: Until.

Silence. Sela's pen doesn't move. Nim's chalk doesn't move. Bram, at the wall, has put the tray down.

KRELL: (through his teeth) Fold.
FENNICK: The House folds.
NIM: (faint, from the rail) Eleven to one. Somebody in this room is ruined, and I'm not sure yet it isn't me.

The chips come back across the felt. Ashka drops out of the rafters onto Juno's shoulder and bites her ear, hard. She doesn't flinch.

JUNO: Ow. Love you too.

Krell stands. The pot is hers; by the rules she owes nobody a look. The gallery is starting to breathe again, to laugh, to pay Nim, when Juno reaches out and turns her three hidden cards face up, one, two, three, slow, so the whole room can see.

A 3. A 1. The blind card: a 2.

JUNO: Six, sweetheart. (to Sela) Write it down.
SELA: (already writing) "Six."

Nim's chalk hits the floor. The patron sits down on nothing and misses it. Nobody laughs. The room has stopped.

KRELL: (standing) You had nothing.
JUNO: I had him. (gathering chips) Six and a face, Warden. It beat your eleven.

Kaito looks at the cards. At her. At the cards.

KAITO: You told me we'd got him.
JUNO: We had.
KAITO: You LIED to me.
JUNO: I lied to Krell. (she squeezes the hand she's still holding) You were the paper I wrote it on. He's a soldier, Kaito. Soldiers don't trust cards. They trust faces, and yours hasn't got a lie in it anywhere. It's your one good feature.
KAITO: I have LOADS of—
JUNO: Shh.

Under the table, where only Sela can see, Juno's other hand is shaking so hard the chips in it rattle. Sela puts her own hand over it. Holds. Writes nothing.

## SCENE 5 — INT. HIGH TABLE ROOM — CONTINUOUS (≈ 1:30)

Krell is very still. Then one word, flat, like a boot on a step.

KRELL: Cheat.

He takes the card table in both hands and turns it over. Chips, cards, lamps. Sela's logbook: she catches it one-handed without getting up.

LUSK: (from the door, sharp) The House witnessed a clean fold, Warden.
KRELL: The House is in the Empire, Mister Lusk. (he raises his voice) SHIELDS.

SFX: boots, many, and iron on iron

Both doors. Sever troopers in grey coats, visors down, shields locked edge to edge, filling each doorway two ranks deep. Bond-cutters out, glowing white. The gallery scatters to the walls.

KRELL: Her paper was the Sever's. The bird is the Sever's. Everything that touched this table is the Sever's, and I am done paying for the privilege of watching. First rank, hold the doors. Second rank: nobody leaves this room with a partner.

SELA: (low, to Kaito, still seated) Pup. When she lets go, you go left, and you keep going.
KAITO: I'm not leaving her.
SELA: I didn't say leave. I said left.

He kicks the iron coffee table out of his way. It doesn't move. He kicks it again; it still doesn't move, and the drinks on it slide off. Grist opens one eye.

KRELL: Who put an ARMOURED BEETLE in a GAMING LOUNGE.
LUSK: It was labelled a table.

Bram's seams give, all of them at once.

SFX: cloth tearing

BRAM: (gently) Vest's done.

Kaito is already moving to put himself in front of Juno, gauntlet up, the fork on the back of his hand starting to glow.

KAITO: Screw your table. Screw your paper. Come and get the bird.
JUNO: (standing) Kaito.
KAITO: I'm doing a thing—
JUNO: Kaito. Move.

He looks round. She is taking off her gloves.

Finger by finger, the left, then the right. She has never done this in front of them. Kaito sees her palms first, and we see his face see them before we see them at all: burned. Old burns under new ones, blistered, the skin shiny and wrong from the wrist to the fingertips. Years of it.

Sela's eyes close for one second. Bram's don't.

KAITO: Juno. Your hands—
JUNO: Don't.
KAITO: What happened to—
JUNO: (not unkind; final) Don't. Not tonight.

She drops the gloves on the spilled cards. Squares her shoulders. Looks at the shield line, and at Krell, and this time the odds she gives herself aren't said out loud; we see them cross her face, and they're bad, and she grins anyway.

JUNO: Gloves are for cards, sweetheart.

She lifts both bare hands. Ashka screams. The mark below her collarbone flares white-orange, and fire pours out of her open palms and across the floor at the shields like something let off a leash.

The whole room goes orange.

SMASH CUT TO BLACK.
