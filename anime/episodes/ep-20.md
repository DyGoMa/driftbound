## SCENE 1 — EXT. OPEN SEA, SOUTH OF LANTERNREACH — MORNING (≈ 1:20)

Black screen. A man's voice, silky, with a rasp like a match being struck.

SILAS (V.O.): Previously. As I understand it. A ship of strangers arrived in my port with its cargo badly minded, so I minded it for them. Elsewhere. There was a misunderstanding in a bathhouse, a larger one in a warehouse, and I lost my voice for nine days, which the crew describe as the happiest of their lives. Then a man in a white mask tried to kill the loud one and stopped halfway, because the captain called him a dog. (beat) Pup. She called him pup. The mask took it very hard. (the rasp catches) Also, I live on the boat now. Nobody voted.

Fade up. Hard blue morning. The Gullwake under tow, listing. The bow tow-post is split down its length and bound with chain; Bram (31, huge, shirtless under his dungarees, towel on one shoulder) kneels beside it with a mallet, listening to it like a chest.

SFX: a slow creak, wood under chain

BRAM: She's holding.
SELA: (O.S.) How long?
BRAM: Till she isn't.

Sela (28, cropped navigator's jacket, silver-blue ponytail, logbook open on her forearm) stands at the rail with a printed bill. Juno (23, bomber jacket, copper braid, Ashka on her shoulder) is at the mast with chalk.

JUNO: Four to one the post goes before we make land.
BRAM: Don't bet on my post.
JUNO: I'm betting on the sea, sweetheart. Your post's just where it lands.

Kaito (21, red jacket tied at his waist, right forearm bandaged wrist to elbow, chart board in his left hand) comes up the ladder and reads the bill over Sela's shoulder. He stops breathing.

INSERT: a woodcut bill, sun-faded. THE LONGWAKE REGATTA. SALTLIGHT ATOLL. THE SEVENTH YEAR. TO THE WINNER: THE PURSE, AND THE LONGWAKE CHART.

KAITO: The Longwake Chart.
SELA: Don't.
KAITO: The LONGWAKE CHART. Sela. That's the best map in the world.
SELA: It's the best map in the world for about a month. Then it's a very expensive drawing of where things used to be.
KAITO: A MONTH? I've never held a map that was right for a day!

Silas (24, shirt open to the stomach, red sash, dark-teal hair tied low; voice back, rasp and all) lies along the rail with his eyes shut.

SILAS: He's shouting at paper again.
JUNO: He loves paper. It's a thing. We don't talk about it.
KAITO: Screw the parts. We go for the Chart.
SELA: We go for the purse, pup. Two thousand crowns to the winner, and a bow-post that isn't held on by Bram's feelings.
BRAM: (tapping the post) Feelings and chain.
SELA: (writing) And chain.
KAITO: And the Chart.
SELA: And if you win the Chart, you may shout at it all you like.

She folds the bill into the logbook.

SELA: Saltlight. Neutral atoll, three days south. Every seven years every ship that can still float goes there to race, and nobody draws steel inside the reef. Not smugglers. Not the Red Tally. Not the Empire.
KAITO: The Empire's THERE?
SELA: Everyone's invited, pup. That's the point of it.
SILAS: (eyes still shut) I was invited last time. There was an incident.
JUNO: Evens it's the same incident.
SILAS: Darling, I never repeat myself. I have far too much material.

Kaito looks at his bandaged arm, then at the sea.

KAITO: The mask. At the docks. He stopped when you shouted.
SELA: I shout a lot.
KAITO: He stopped when you shouted PUP.
SELA: (not looking up, writing) Then perhaps he's a fan.

Her pen stops for one beat. Then goes on.

## SCENE 2 — EXT. SALTLIGHT LAGOON — DAY (≈ 1:40)

A ring of white sand and palms round a lagoon so clear the ships seem to hang in air. Hundreds of them: smugglers' sloops, fishing hulks in bunting, a racing yacht thin as a knife. Red buoys mark a sea-ring in the middle. Above it all, on a mast taller than the palms, a white flag with one blue wave.

And at anchor by the reef mouth, three grey Empire cutters, pennants furled, decks quiet.

Kaito and Skarn at the bow, staring at the grey ships the same way.

SELA: Truce flag. White, one wave. While it flies, nobody. Not them. Not us.
KAITO: And if they—
SELA: Then every ship in this lagoon takes them apart before lunch, and they know it. (hand on his shoulder) Look at the pretty boats, pup.

SFX: a laugh across the water, enormous

A red brig cuts across their bow close enough to count rivets, RED TALLY painted on her side in a hand that didn't care. On her rail: Captain Marisol Thane (35, woman, red coat over a corset-style vest, tattooed forearms, one gold eye-patch, laughing with her whole body). Below her a coral-pink hammerhead rolls alongside: Gnash.

MARISOL: Sela VEY. You're still afloat. I had money against it.
SELA: Marisol. You're still loud. Nobody had money against that.
MARISOL: Seven years! You look exactly the same. It's obscene. (she spots Kaito) Who's the pup?
KAITO: Why does EVERYONE—
SELA: He's crew. Hands off, Thane.
MARISOL: I've never once kept my hands off anything of yours, Vey, and you've never once minded.

Juno, at the mast, looks slowly from Marisol to the chalk board.

JUNO: I'm going to need a bigger mast.
SILAS: (sitting up on the rail) Darling. Who IS that.
JUNO: Get in line.
SILAS: I'm always in line. I simply don't queue.

Gnash bumps Vesh's flank. Vesh's lures flare teal once, a warning. Gnash rolls over, belly up, delighted.

MARISOL: Shameless. Like her captain.
SELA: Which one?
MARISOL: (grinning) Pick.

Sela writes. Marisol watches her do it.

MARISOL: You're writing me down.
SELA: Checking you against the last entry.
MARISOL: And?
SELA: You've got taller.
MARISOL: That's the boots. (a beat; quieter, across the water) Have you got me in there, Sel?
SELA: Two pages.

Marisol laughs again, a different laugh, and the Red Tally pulls ahead.

MARISOL: Then I'll see you on the sand. Bring the pup! Bring the one with no buttons!
SILAS: (to nobody, pleased) She noticed.
BRAM: (quiet, at the wheel) Everyone notices.

## SCENE 3 — INT. THE SPLICE, A BAR ON STILTS OVER THE LAGOON — LATE AFTERNOON (≈ 2:40)

Low ceiling, oars on the walls, a floor that moves with the tide. Every racer in Saltlight is here. At the bar end, alone with a bottle: Ottoline Ferro (52, woman, broad, sun-lined, short grey curls, sleeveless racing coat, flask on her hip, three gold Regatta medals hung off her chair-back on a chain, forgotten). She watches the door the way a cat watches a fish tank.

Bram comes in, shopping list in hand, ducking the lintel.

OTTI: Well now. That's a lot of man to fit through a small door.
BRAM: (polite) Ma'am.
OTTI: Ma'am! Say it again. Slower.
BRAM: ...Ma'am.
OTTI: (to the barman) Put his drink on mine. Put HIM on mine, if he's willing.
BRAM: I'm, uh. I'm here for a pump.
OTTI: I'll bet you are.

Bram goes misty, towel over his face, and backs into the barman (man, 44, extra). Silas slides into the gap like he was poured there.

SILAS: You've frightened the cook. He'll cry into the stew, and the stew will improve. Silas Moreau.
OTTI: Ottoline Ferro. Otti. You're pretty. Are you any good?
SILAS: Darling, I'm exceptional. It's the one thing nobody's ever argued, including under oath.
OTTI: (delighted) Too easy. You'd say yes before I'd finished asking.
SILAS: (turning, wounded, to Juno at the door) She called me easy.
JUNO: She's got a good eye.

Kaito shoulders in past Juno, bandaged arm held close.

OTTI: Hello. You. Red jacket.
KAITO: (looking round) Me?
OTTI: What's wrong with your arm?
KAITO: Nothing. Hound. It's fine.
OTTI: Give it here.

She takes his right wrist, not gently, turns it to the lamp and thumbs the bandage up. Below the fresh burn, old branching scars run to his elbow. The bar goes quieter.

OTTI: Hand to elbow. Twenty-one? Twenty-two?
KAITO: Twenty-one.
OTTI: Twenty-one, and you've burned yourself a decade. Who taught you to Surge?
KAITO: Nobody. You just do it.
OTTI: (laughs; drinks) "You just do it." Gods above. Sit down, you beautiful idiot, and buy me a drink.
KAITO: I'm not— I haven't— Bram's got the money—
OTTI: Then sit down for free. (pats the stool) I don't bite on the first drink.

Kaito backs away from the stool and into the oar rack. The whole rack comes down.

SFX: twenty oars, one after another, on a wooden floor

JUNO: THE OAR RACK! Who had the oar rack? (to Ashka) Nobody. Nobody had the oar rack.

Sela comes in on the noise, looks at Kaito under the oars, and sits beside Otti as if arranged.

OTTI: Yours?
SELA: Mine.
OTTI: He's hurting himself.
SELA: I know.
OTTI: Do you.
SELA: Every day. He doesn't listen.
OTTI: (drinks) They don't. Not till somebody makes them. (looks at Sela properly) Ottoline Ferro.
SELA: I know who you are. Three Longwakes.
OTTI: Three. (touching the medals without looking) I was drinking in this bar before you could swim, Captain Vey, and I'm still better at it.

The door bangs. A man strides in like the room owes him: Tobias Quill (27, man, long blond tail of hair, a gold tooth, a sash across his chest sewn thick with coloured race ribbons).

TOBIAS: OTTI! You old pirate. Tell me you're not racing.
OTTI: I'm drinking, Quill. Racing's for people who still want things.
TOBIAS: (spotting Kaito climbing out of the oars) Who's the stray?
KAITO: Who's the— what are you WEARING, a curtain?
TOBIAS: Ribbons. One per win. (counting with a thumb) You'd need a bigger chest.
KAITO: I've got a— I'd need a— (gives up) Screw you.

Skarn's head comes through the door, ears flat, teeth showing. Tobias whistles two notes, and outside the beach itself lifts: a sand kite-ray wide as a fishing boat heaves out of the shallows and shakes, pelting the doorway and Skarn with wet sand. Flint.

TOBIAS: Flint. Say hello.

Skarn, sanded, blinks once. Slowly turns his head to Kaito. The look is a letter of complaint.

JUNO: Nine to two Kaito hates him on sight.
SILAS: You said that about me.
JUNO: Paid out, didn't it.

[EYECATCH: A small sand-coloured Pal buried on the beach up to its eyes, perfectly still, as a crab walks across the top of its head. It lets the crab finish.]

## SCENE 4 — EXT. SALTLIGHT BEACH — SUNSET (≈ 3:00)

Bonfires down the whole curve of sand. Drums. Every crew has come down to the water for the Salting: wade in to the waist and the Regatta has begun for you. Everyone is in what they swim in, which on Saltlight is a matter of pride.

Juno has chalked a driftwood plank propped against a palm. WHO GETS LOOKED AT. BRAM 3-1. SILAS EVENS. SELA: NO BOOK (NOT FAIR). KAITO 40-1.

Bram comes down the sand in a striped racing suit that was small when it was new, towel on his shoulder, a bucket of something hot in each hand. Conversation falls over for ten yards in every direction.

JUNO: Bram. Sweetheart. Where's the rest of it?
BRAM: It's a racing suit. Tam made it. (a beat) In 'twenty-one.
SILAS: And has he grown?
BRAM: A bit.
JUNO: (chalking) Bram, three to one ON.
BRAM: Rule Thirty-Four. No remarks on the beach.
JUNO: This isn't your galley.
BRAM: (setting the buckets down) I brought the galley.

Silas arrives in very little: a short red wrap and the sash, hair down. He walks past the plank without reading it.

JUNO: Oi. You're evens.
SILAS: Darling, I've never been evens in my life.

Otti, in a deckchair with the flask, calls the parade like a race.

OTTI: Cook: yes. Pretty one: yes, and knows it, which is a shame. Red Tally: yes, yes, YES. Red jacket— (squints) Where's red jacket?

Kaito stands in the firelight in his cargo pants rolled to the knee and his sleeveless top, jacket still tied at his waist, bandaged arm folded in, refusing to be a parade. His nose, after a day of sun, is the colour of the jacket.

OTTI: Red jacket's wearing TROUSERS.
KAITO: I'm not RACING tonight!
OTTI: You're losing tonight, love. On points.

Juno comes over with a tin of white zinc paste and no sympathy.

JUNO: You're burning.
KAITO: I'm not.
JUNO: Your nose is a signal flare. Hold still.

She dabs it on his nose with one gloved finger. He goes very still. She's close. Ashka, on her shoulder, regards Kaito from a hand's width with a raptor's complete lack of mercy.

JUNO: (quiet, concentrating) It's medical, sweetheart. Breathe.
KAITO: I'm breathing.
JUNO: Not through your ears you're not.

She finishes. Steps back. Kaito, released, takes one step backward and goes straight through the side of a tent.

SFX: canvas, poles, a tray of cups

JUNO: (already chalking) THE TENT.
OTTI: (toasting the tent) There he is.

Further down, under the Red Tally's flag, Marisol stands at the tide line in a red bikini top and a white shirt hanging open, her coat thrown over Gnash's fin like a hat-stand. Sela joins her in her black bandeau and open blue jacket, sandals in one hand, logbook in the other. Shoulder to shoulder, looking at the sea: two women who know they're being looked at and have decided that's fine.

MARISOL: Same top as seven years ago, Vey.
SELA: Same shirt, Thane. The buttons gave up.
MARISOL: The buttons were weak. I'm strong.
SELA: Is that why it's open, or did you lose a bet?
MARISOL: I don't lose bets. I lose shirts.

A long admiring whistle from a passing crew. Both women turn, chin up, to accept it. It's aimed past them, at Bram, going back up the sand with the empty buckets.

MARISOL: ...The cook.
SELA: The cook.

They drink to it, unbothered.

Then Marisol's voice drops under the drums.

MARISOL: You remember the last one? Seven years. The lagoon, at night, you and me and that terrible bottle.
SELA: I've got two pages on it.
MARISOL: Pages.
SELA: And a drawing. It's a good drawing.
MARISOL: (not laughing now) Sel. Do you remember it, or did you read it?
SELA: (a beat) Does it matter?
MARISOL: Not to me. (she clinks Sela's cup) To you?
SELA: Ask me after I've read it.

Marisol looks at her a long moment, then hauls her in by the shoulders, loud again on purpose.

MARISOL: Then I'll make you a new one. One you can't put down.
SELA: That's what Juno said.
MARISOL: Who the hell's Juno?
JUNO: (from the plank) Hi.
MARISOL: (approving) Oh, I'd have remembered you.

Kaito, out of the tent with a pole across his shoulders, sees Sela and Marisol wading in together, Juno laughing at them, Otti's flask going up to him in salute, and he stops at the fire with the whole beach moving round him, zinc on his nose, and for one quiet second looks like a man who has never been invited anywhere before.

Skarn comes and leans on him. It nearly puts him in the fire.

KAITO: (shoving back) Yeah. Yeah, me too.

## SCENE 5 — EXT. SALTLIGHT, THE LONGWAKE BOARD — NIGHT (≈ 2:15)

A white driftwood table on a raised deck above the beach, a hundred lanterns, a ledger the size of a door. Behind it, the Regatta Master: Adaire Fenn (64, woman, salt-white coat, blue-wave pin at the collar, a voice like a bell hit exactly once). Every crew crowds the sand below. The grey cutters' crews stand apart at the back, hands clasped.

FENN: The Longwake Regatta. Seventh year. The rules are old and I'll say them once.

The drums stop.

FENN: Three legs. The First Leg: open water, round the atoll, any ship, any partner, any way you like. The Second Leg: the duels, in the ring, partner and human together, to a touch and no further. The Third: the Longwake Run, beyond the reef, over the course the Chart itself was drawn on. Finish all three and the lagoon knows your name for seven years.

A roar. Fenn waits for it to die, as if it were weather.

FENN: Under the wave there is no Empire, no free port, no debt and no feud. No steel in the lagoon. No cutters. No Surge turned on another entrant outside the ring. Break the truce, and every ship inside this reef is yours to answer to.

A pause. Nobody looks at the grey coats. Everybody is looking at the grey coats.

FENN: To the winner: the purse. And the Longwake Chart, drawn fresh over seven years by my own people, correct on the day it's handed over and not one day after.

Kaito, at the front, mouth open. Juno nudges him. He closes it.

FENN: Entrants. Sign.

They go up by turns. Marisol with a flourish. Tobias writing QUILL so big it takes two lines. Sela, neat: GULLWAKE. S. VEY. Kaito, right-handed, bandage and all, in letters like a fence after a storm: K. BRASK & SKARN. Silas signs something long; Fenn raises an eyebrow and lets it stand. Juno chalks odds in the margin until Fenn takes the chalk. Bram signs small.

Otti walks past the board without stopping, flask up.

OTTI: Not me, Adaire. Three's a nice number.
FENN: (not looking up) Nicer than four, Ottoline?
OTTI: Nicer than dead.

She goes down the steps. The next in line hasn't moved. The crowd has gone quiet in a ring, the way water stills before something surfaces.

SFX: a chain, link by link, over boards

Hekka Dorn (33, woman, 193 cm, heavy grey plates, bare midriff with the dull ring scar on her left side, long black hair swept back, a bond-cutter lance taller than she is) comes up the steps. Behind her on an iron chain, filling the deck: the bear. Ash-grey. Plates on brow and spine. Blank white eyes looking at nothing. A white core set in its chest, lit.

Every partner on the beach turns its head at once. Skarn's fin lifts and crackles. Ashka's feathers rise. Out in the lagoon Vesh's lures all come on, and Gnash's fin cuts a hard turn. The bear looks at none of them.

FENN: Steel stays sheathed on this deck, Captain.
HEKKA: It's a lance. It doesn't sheathe.
FENN: Then it stays down.

Hekka plants the lance point-first through the boards. It stands by itself. She takes the pen.

HEKKA: Dorn. One R.

She writes it. Turns. Finds Sela in the crowd first time, no searching.

HEKKA: Vey.
SELA: Dorn. You owe me a drink.
HEKKA: After.
SELA: After what?
HEKKA: (a nod at the board) That.

She goes down the steps, the bear after her, and the crowd opens without being asked. Kaito's left hand has closed over his bandaged arm. Sela hasn't moved. Juno, beside her, very quiet for Juno.

JUNO: I've got no book on this.
SILAS: Darling, I think that's rather the point of her.
BRAM: (quieter still) She fed it. Coming up the beach. Out of her hand.

Sela opens the logbook and writes one line without looking down.

INSERT: Dorn signed. Under the wave. The bear didn't know any of us. Neither did she, nearly.

FENN: (the bell, once) The Longwake Regatta is open.

Fireworks go up over the lagoon, red and gold, and every face tips up but two: Kaito, looking at the lance still standing in the boards, and Hekka, at the water's edge, feeding the bear from her bare hand while the sky comes apart.
