## SCENE 1 — EXT. PINHOLD, MARKET SQUARE — DAWN (≈ 1:00)

Black. BRAM's voice over clips from Episodes 1 to 3.

BRAM (V.O.): Last time. The lad stole some medicine and got paid in eggs. Fair trade. He met the captain and walked into a post. Also fair. A man in a mask set a dog on him, so he came aboard for stew. Then the grey coats read out their rule and did a terrible thing to an old man's ox, and the lad lost his temper. (beat) I'd have lost mine. Slower.

SMASH CUT:

KAITO BRASK (21) comes off a trooper's shield like a thrown cat and is up before he's finished landing. Skarn, a horse-sized storm wolf, stands over him, lip up at twelve grey coats.

Against the far wall, the queue from the stripping, forty townsfolk with beasts on ropes. Behind the troopers, the barricaded Pin Road.

TROOPER SERGEANT (44): Brask. On the ground. You'll be dealt with after the animals.

KAITO: After the... (to Skarn) Did you hear that? We're after the animals.

Skarn growls.

KAITO: Right. Screw that.

He charges. He is good for exactly one trooper. The sergeant raises the bond-cutter, hooked and white.

SFX: a slow CRUNCH behind the barricade

GRIST, an iron beetle the size of a cart, walks through the barricade like a cart through a hedge. On its back, BRAM TORVALD (31): huge, bearded, towel on one shoulder, a dungaree strap down, crowbar in hand.

BRAM: (gently) Morning.

The troopers scatter.

BRAM: (to Kaito) Pier's open for ten minutes. Captain says anyone with a beast gets on the boat.

KAITO: Ten minutes.

BRAM: Then they shut the road again. Grist and me can hold the pier, lad. We can't hold the road.

KAITO: How many on the pier?

BRAM: Forty, by the time you get there.

KAITO: And you're fine with that.

BRAM: I've fed forty. Forty's nothing.

Kaito looks at the Pin Road: barricades, cables, searchlights. Above it, the pylon. Humming.

KAITO: Then nobody's taking the road.

He looks up at the rooftops.

KAITO: (to the queue) Anyone with a partner! On me! We're going over!

MAUD PELLOW (63), small and wide, egg-stained apron, a goose-sized lizard under one arm: Over WHAT?

KAITO: Everything!

MUSIC: brass, hard

## PART A

## SCENE 2 — INT. FINCH'S CHART SHOP — DAWN (≈ 1:10)

A narrow shop, every wall a chart. ABEL FINCH (58), thin, spectacles on a cord, takes a chart down from the window. The shop is packed: Maud; HARL DUNNING (38), a well-digger, with his pig-sized burrowing beast, SPADE, asleep on his boots; a dozen more with birds, lizards, a woolly thing that may be a sheep.

KAITO: Right. Anyone who can climb comes with me. Anyone who can't waits here for Grist. Who can climb?

Nobody moves.

KAITO: Nobody can climb. Great. Finch, I need the sack.

FINCH: The what?

KAITO: My courier sack. Behind the counter. It's always behind the counter.

FINCH: (fetching it) Kaito. You're about to carry a bag of animals over the rooftops while a garrison shoots at you.

KAITO: Yeah.

FINCH: I sold you your first chart. I'd like it noted that I'm against this.

KAITO: Noted. You're against everything. You were against the eggs.

FINCH: Eggs are not a currency.

KAITO: Eggs are the best currency. You can't eat a coin. (slinging the sack on) Maud. Lizard. In.

MAUD: Her name is Yolk and she doesn't go in bags.

KAITO: She goes in bags today.

Yolk climbs in with enormous dignity and puts her head out of the top like a periscope.

MAUD: ...Traitor.

KAITO: Harl. Can yours climb?

HARL: Spade? No. He digs.

KAITO: How fast?

HARL: Fast enough he's under your floor already.

Everyone looks down. Spade is gone. There is a hole.

MAUD: Harl Dunning, that's a shop floor.

HARL: He's never been fussy, Maud.

KAITO: ...Fine. Then dig. Harbour's downhill; keep going till you hit water. Everyone else: when I go, you go behind me. Step where I step. If I jump, you don't jump. You wait for the big dog.

Skarn, filling the doorway, exhales through his nose.

KAITO: He's thrilled. (to Finch) You coming?

FINCH: (quiet) I haven't got a partner, Kaito. They don't want me. I'm allowed to stay here and be correct.

He hangs the chart back and smooths it. Pinhold, with a pin drawn through.

KAITO: That chart's wrong, you know. Always was.

FINCH: Not any more. (beat) Go on. Before I'm right about you too.

## SCENE 3 — EXT. PINHOLD ROOFTOPS — DAWN (≈ 1:50)

Kaito comes out of a skylight at a run, Yolk's head out of the sack. Skarn runs the ridge above.

SFX: tiles cracking under boots

KAITO: First map of the Drift that stays right. Mine. Say it back.

Skarn grumbles.

KAITO: You never say it back.

An alley gap. The gauntlet flares; he's over in a crack of light. The glow runs up under his sleeve and stays a half-second too long.

Skarn ferries the townsfolk across on his back, Maud first, apron over her face.

MAUD: Don't look at me! I'm not dignified!

KAITO: Nobody's looking, Maud, we're being shot at!

MAUD: That's no excuse!

Yolk hisses at the alley.

KAITO: Nobody asked you. (to Maud) Does she always talk this much?

MAUD: Only to people she doesn't like.

Below, a trooper raises a long-gun. Another knocks it down.

TROOPER: No shooting the animals!

KAITO: THE ANIMALS SAY THANKS!

Searchlights swing up and catch gulls. Then three shapes that are not gulls. Plates. Cables. A white light in each chest where a heart would be.

Kaito's face changes. He knows what they were.

KAITO: (quieter) Everyone down. Flat. Now.

They go flat. The swoopers pass over, core-light sliding across the slates.

MAUD: (whisper) What are they?

KAITO: (whisper) They were somebody's. Like Yolk's yours.

MAUD: ...Oh.

KAITO: Yeah. Keep her head in. (to Skarn) Don't look at their eyes, Bram said. (beat) He didn't say what to do if they look at ours. He left that bit out.

The swoopers bank away. Kaito's up.

KAITO: Go, go, go, go.

A chimney explodes beside his head.

KAITO: HEY! NO SHOOTING THE ANIMALS, HE SAID! I'VE GOT AN ANIMAL!

A tile goes. He slides down the slate, sack held high, and off the edge.

Skarn's jaws close on his jacket. Kaito hangs upside down over a street full of troopers, holding a bag of lizard.

KAITO: (to the troopers) Morning.

TROOPER: (automatically) Morning.

KAITO: (to Skarn) Don't you dare drop me. (beat) You're thinking about it.

Skarn hauls him up.

## SCENE 4 — EXT. THE CHAIN YARD — DAWN (≈ 1:20)

The roofs end at a yard where the pylon's cables come to earth: a dozen arm-thick steel lines strung over to the harbour wall, each one humming. Below, a shield line.

KAITO: Okay. The cables go over the yard to the harbour wall. So we go over the yard. On the cables.

MAUD: On them.

KAITO: On them.

MAUD: Boy, I am sixty-three years old.

KAITO: Then you ride the dog. (to Skarn) You're taking the heavy ones.

Skarn looks at Maud. Maud looks at Skarn.

MAUD: Charming.

Kaito steps onto a cable. It hums up through his boots, his knees, his teeth.

KAITO: (quiet) ...You feel that?

Skarn's fin is up, bristling.

KAITO: It's been going since the night it started. It's in the cables. It's in the... never mind.

MAUD: My hens stopped laying that night. Forty eggs, that's all I've got left in the world.

KAITO: That's forty more than Finch. Go.

He runs the wire, arms out. A searchlight finds him: a boy on a wire against the sky.

TROOPER: There! On the line!

KAITO: OBVIOUSLY ON THE LINE!

Skarn glides beside him, Maud on his back, eyes shut.

MAUD: If I die on a wolf I will never hear the end of it from my sister!

The yard heaves. Four troopers go up like a tablecloth, and out of the dirt come Spade, chewing, and Harl, grinning.

HARL: Harbour's that way?

KAITO: THAT WAY!

HARL: Ta. Mind the hens, Maud!

He drops back down. The hole moves off.

Halfway across, Kaito looks down at the harbour: the Gullwake against the pier, Bram a dot beside Grist, forty shields coming down the road. And across the harbour mouth, a chain boom thick as a tree.

KAITO: (stopping dead) ...They've shut the harbour.

MAUD: (eyes shut) Is that bad?

KAITO: The ship's in a bottle, Maud. We're carrying everyone into a bottle.

MAUD: Does the captain know?

KAITO: She's looking right at it.

Far below, a tiny figure in blue walks to the bow, writes in a book, and puts it away.

MAUD: And?

KAITO: And she's writing. Why is she WRITING?

The tiny figure rests her hands on the rail.

## PART B

## SCENE 5 — EXT. THE PIER AND HARBOUR — DAWN (≈ 1:30)

Grist stands across the pier like a dropped anvil, Bram beside him. Forty shields come down the road.

BRAM: (to Grist, low) Steady. Nobody's hurt yet.

The line hits. Bram's forearm goes grey, dull as a pan; a bond-cutter rings off it like a bell. He pushes a shield flat-handed and six men go backward.

BRAM: (patient) I did say.

Grist lowers his horn. The troopers reconsider.

From the ship: SELA VEY (28), cropped jacket over a black bandeau, logbook at her back.

SELA: (not raising her voice) Bram. Mind your feet.

BRAM: Aye, Captain.

SELA: Everyone on the pier, hold on to something nailed down. (to the troopers) That includes you.

She steps up onto the rail. The spiral mark below her collarbone goes from a glow to a light.

The harbour goes flat, then down, as if the basin took a breath in. Boots stop.

SFX: a long sound from under the water, like a cathedral door

She steps off the rail onto the water, and the water takes her weight and rises. VESH comes up under her: a brow of dark-blue scale, then a back longer than the pier that keeps coming, lantern-lures lighting one by one. Water pours off a frilled jaw as wide as a barn door. Sela stands on the head, one hand on a horn, until they are both looking down at the pier, and the pier is full of very small men.

High on the cable, Kaito's mouth is open.

KAITO: (small) ...That's been under us the whole time?

BRAM: (not looking up) She likes the deep.

SELA: (almost gently) The chain, love.

Vesh lays her jaw across the boom and bites. The chain comes apart with a crack that rattles every window in Pinhold. She lowers her head to the Gullwake's rail; Sela steps off onto the deck without looking. Vesh sinks, lures dimming.

The shield line has taken three steps back, as one body.

BRAM: (to the nearest trooper, kind) You can go, if you like. I'd go.

TROOPER SERGEANT: (O.S.) ...Fall back to the road.

BRAM: Sensible.

SELA: (up to the sky) Courier. You're late.

KAITO: I'M THE DELIVERY!

SELA: "I'm the delivery." Bram, write that down for me. My hands are full.

BRAM: Haven't got a pencil, Captain.

SELA: Then remember it. It's a good one. He won't have another for weeks.

At the wire's end, above the pylon's base, Kaito hooks the sack into Skarn's jaws as the wolf glides past with Maud.

KAITO: Take them down. I'll run the last bit.

Skarn gives him a look.

KAITO: It's forty feet, you big baby. Go.

On the pier, Bram sets Maud down like a parcel. She looks up at him. Up.

MAUD: Oh. Hello.

BRAM: Ma'am.

MAUD: (taking the sack) Well. Yolk, we're saved. Nice boat.

BRAM: She leaks.

MAUD: So do I, dear. I'm sixty-three. (climbing aboard, to Sela) You're the one who buys old maps.

SELA: Survey sheets. And yes.

MAUD: Whatever for? They're all wrong.

SELA: That's what's useful about them.

MAUD: That's not an answer.

SELA: No. Sit down, Mrs Pellow.

Spade erupts through the pier planks beside Grist, who does not react.

HARL: (climbing out) Told you. Fast.

BRAM: ...I'll fix that.

## SCENE 6 — EXT. THE PYLON BASE — DAWN (≈ 2:20)

A concrete anchor block the size of a house. Above, the pylon's flank, white seams pulsing slowly. Here the hum is a sound.

Kaito drops off the cable. And stops.

The HOUND: bull-sized, grey, cables standing out of it like veins, a white core shuttered in its chest. Blank eyes. On its back, ankles crossed as if waiting for a ferry, SILT (29): wiry, ragged black coat, white mask with one eye slit.

SILT: (flat) You run loud.

KAITO: You.

SILT: Me. (beat) You got them all off. That was well done. That's the last of them on the boat.

KAITO: Then get out of my way.

SILT: In a minute. Why do you run?

KAITO: What?

SILT: You run. Every island, you people run. (a hand at the sea) It's the same sea. Pinned or not. Where do you think you're going that isn't this?

Kaito's gauntlet glows. He is shaking, a little, and hating it.

KAITO: Somewhere that moves.

SILT: Hm. (beat) That's not an answer. That's a direction.

KAITO: It's the one I've got.

SILT: It won't last. Nothing out there does. (beat) I'm not being cruel. I'm telling you early.

KAITO: Tell me from further away.

The hound stands; Silt stands with it.

SILT: Run, then. It's better when you run.

MUSIC: out. Just the hum.

The hound lunges. Kaito dives flat; jaws close where his head was. It turns like a skater, and for half a second the chest plates part and the white shows.

KAITO: (to himself) Plates open when it turns. Hit the white. How many times? (beat) Till it stops, idiot.

It comes again. Kaito jumps, and as it passes under him he puts the gauntlet down and lets everything in his arm go.

SFX: a lightning crack like a snapped mast

The bolt hits the open core. The hound screams, not a dog noise. Kaito lands, knees go, he's up. Under his sleeve the scar forks past the elbow.

KAITO: (through his teeth) One.

SILT: (interested, barely) Good. Again.

The hound circles. Silt, no weapon drawn, is watching the arm.

SILT: You're burning that arm for one dog.

KAITO: It's MY dog.

SILT: Hm. I admire the waste.

Lunge. Kaito fires from a crouch; the hound slews into the cables, and they sing.

KAITO: (gasping) Two.

The scar climbs to his shoulder. The hand shakes so hard the gauntlet rattles.

SILT: (flat) That's your arm for a week. You know that.

KAITO: (straightening) Then I'd better finish inside a week.

SILT: You're not funny.

KAITO: I'm a bit funny.

A third time. Kaito doesn't jump, because he can't. He plants his feet, takes it full in the chest, and as the jaws come down he puts the gauntlet inside the plates, against the white, and Surges with everything that's left.

The flash takes the whole screen.

SFX: a sound like a plate breaking under a hand

Kaito on his back ten feet away, sleeve smoking. The hound stands over nothing, chest open, a dark crack across the core. The light inside stutters, stutters, holds. Dim.

It doesn't attack. It looks confused, if a thing with blank eyes can.

Silt looks at the crack. For a moment, completely still.

SILT: (quiet) Three.

KAITO: (on his back) ...Told you. Bit funny.

He walks over and crouches. Kaito gets to one elbow; the right arm lies on the concrete, slack. The mask looks at the arm. Then at the face.

SILT: Does it hurt?

KAITO: What do you think?

SILT: I think you'll do it again tomorrow. (beat) That's the part I don't understand. (standing) Hm. You've improved.

KAITO: (hoarse) Get off my island.

SILT: It isn't yours in about four minutes. Listen.

The hum is climbing. The seams pulse faster.

SILT: Keep that arm warm for me, Brask.

He swings onto the hound and walks it away along the harbour wall, unhurried, into the pylon's shadow.

Kaito lies there, breathing. The hum.

Then, across the harbour, from the Gullwake, Sela's voice. Raised, for the first time since we met her.

SELA: PUP!

KAITO: ...Pup?

SELA: (O.S.) MOVE!

He moves.

## SCENE 7 — EXT. GULLWAKE, DECK AND HARBOUR — DAWN INTO DAY (≈ 1:20)

Kaito comes off the harbour wall at a dead run, dead arm swinging. Skarn sweeps under him, clears the widening water, and drops him on the deck on his face.

SELA: Hm. Stuck the landing.

KAITO: (face in the deck) I meant to do that.

SELA: Of course you did.

KAITO: Who's "pup"?

SELA: You are.

KAITO: Why?

SELA: (writing in the logbook) You've got the ears for it. Look, there they go.

They're going red. He claps a hand over one.

KAITO: They are NOT. That's sunburn.

SELA: It's dawn.

KAITO: Early sunburn.

Bram lifts him by the jacket, sits him on a crate like a pot coming off the heat, and looks at the arm.

BRAM: (quiet) All the way up, lad.

KAITO: It'll come back.

BRAM: In a bit. (beat) Three hits?

KAITO: Three. Cracked it. (grinning) You should've seen his face. Not his face, he's got a... you know what I mean.

BRAM: I know what you mean.

SELA: "Cracked it." Did it stop?

KAITO: ...No. It walked off. He walked off. Like he'd finished his shift.

SELA: (writes that too) Then he'll be back for the rest of the arm. Good to know.

KAITO: You're very calm about my arm.

SELA: Somebody has to be. You're bleeding on my deck.

He looks. He is.

KAITO: He asked me why I run.

SELA: And?

KAITO: I didn't have a good answer.

SELA: You will. Give it a few islands.

Behind them, Maud, Harl and a dozen Pinholders, looking back at the town.

SELA: (closing the logbook) Everyone look at the harbour.

They look. Pinhold, behind the ship: roofs, square, yard, pylon.

The hum climbs to a note everyone can hear. Skarn's ears go flat.

The pylon's seams go solid, all at once.

SFX: one sound. Deep. Final. A door the size of the island, shutting.

And the hum stops. Not fades. Stops.

Silence. The sea goes on moving under them. The other islands on the horizon drift, slow, the way they always have. Pinhold sits where it was pinned, like a thing on a wall.

HARL: (quiet) It's not moving.

MAUD: It never moved, Harl. Not that you'd see.

HARL: You'd feel it. In the well water. (beat) I can't feel it.

KAITO: (quiet) ...It was there. In the cables. The whole time. And now it's just—

SELA: I know.

KAITO: How do you—

SELA: (hand flat over her mark, the way you'd check a watch) Because I can't feel it move any more. (beat) Write that down. (nothing to write with) ...Later.

Bram has gone misty. He wipes his face with the towel and puts it back on his shoulder.

BRAM: (to Grist) Lie down, old man. We're clear. (to Maud) I'll do eggs. Have you got—

MAUD: (lifting a basket) Forty.

BRAM: (a wet sniff) Bless you.

KAITO: Can I have one?

BRAM: You can have six. Vegetable first.

Finch alone at his wall. He takes the pin out of Pinhold on the big chart, looks out of the window at the islands, and puts the pin back exactly where it was.

Pan: every chart on the wall. Pinhold on every one. Every one right.

FINCH: Well. (beat) Every single one.

He straightens a frame that was already straight.

MONTAGE, silent: the tavern's chart, right. A chart chalked on a fisherman's door, right. And on the Gullwake's deck, under a mug, a blank sheet, Kaito's left hand holding a pencil over it. Not moving.
