## COLD OPEN

## SCENE 1 — RECAP MONTAGE / EXT. PINHOLD, WELL-HOUSE SQUARE — NIGHT (≈ 1:15)

Black. A ladle knocks a pot. Over clips from Episodes 1 and 2:

BRAM (V.O.): Last time. A boy fell on my deck like a dropped loaf. Kaito. Twenty-one, he says. Eats like he's nine. A hunter in a white mask ran him across half of Pinhold; he fought back, lost, and burned a new fork up his arm getting out. Captain offered him a bunk. He said no. Pinhold's his. Then he opened a door he shouldn't have, and that's between him, the Captain and the sail-screen. Then the Sever put paper on every wall in town. Every monster on the island, handed over by dawn. It's nearly dawn. I made bread.

SFX: the ladle, once more, softer

CUT TO:

Night, real. Pinhold's well-house square: grey soil, a dry well, one lamp. A Sever notice nailed to the well-house door, the symbol at its head, a straight line cutting a circle. Next to it, an old framed chart of the island, the pins in it exactly where the coastline is now.

KAITO BRASK (21; white streak in black hair, red jacket tied at his waist, right forearm in a fingerless metal gauntlet, fresh lightning-scar running past the elbow) stands reading the notice by the lamp.

KAITO: (reading) "All bonded beasts, working or kept, to be presented at the harbour square at first light, with their handlers. Mercy will be given." (he reads the last line again) Given.

He tears the notice off the nail. Looks at it. Puts it back on the nail, carefully, and smooths it flat.

KAITO: (quiet) Right.

He walks. The town is a dead town pretending to sleep. Up a stone stair to the roofless signal tower above the harbour. At the top: SKARN, a storm wolf the size of a horse, slate fur, sail-fin along the spine. One yellow eye opens.

Kaito sits down against the wolf's ribs. The fin crackles once, faint.

KAITO: They've put paper on the well-house. Every monster, by dawn. "Monster." That's you, apparently.

The yellow eye, unimpressed.

KAITO: I know. I said that. (beat) The captain says she's got a deck with room on it. Says it like she's doing me a favour. (beat) Pinhold's mine. Somebody's got to draw it.

Nothing from the wolf.

KAITO: Not giving you to anyone.

Skarn's eye closes. Far below, under the harbour, under everything, a sound you feel in the teeth before you hear it.

SFX: a hum, very low, three slow swells

Kaito's hand finds the fur. His eyes shut.

SMASH CUT TO:

White. All white. The sound of a sky splitting.

## PART A

## SCENE 2 — FLASHBACK. EXT. HEADLAND CLIFF, PINHOLD — FIFTEEN YEARS AGO — LATE AFTERNOON (≈ 1:50)

Greys and one green. The island still moves here: beyond the cliff, far out, another island slides slowly past.

A brass survey tripod. A chart table of stones. OREN BRASK (39 here; 54 now), a tall surveyor in an oilskin coat, lean, same jaw as his son, sights through the instrument into the wind. Pinned under stones: survey sheets in a neat hand, a corner initialled O.B.

THE BOY, Kaito at six, no white in his hair yet, lies on his belly holding down the edge of the top sheet with both hands.

OREN: Hold that corner, Kai.
THE BOY: I'm holding it.
OREN: You're holding the sheet. Hold the corner. The wind takes corners first.

The boy moves his hands. Oren reads the instrument.

OREN: Write that down for me. Three, one, two. Can you make a two?
THE BOY: I can make a two.

The boy writes it, tongue between his teeth. Oren looks up.

THE BOY: Why do you measure if it moves?
OREN: Because it moves.
THE BOY: That's not an answer.
OREN: (a small, tired almost-smile) No. (he writes) People say the islands move, so maps are useless. People are lazy. Measure a thing long enough, it tells you where it's going.
THE BOY: Where's it going?

Oren does not answer that. The sky over the sea has gone the colour of slate. The far island vanishes behind rain.

THE BOY: Da. It's coming.
OREN: One more bearing.
THE BOY: Da.
OREN: One more. Then home.

The wind takes a corner. The boy flattens himself over the sheets to keep them. Oren bends to the instrument. The first drops hit the brass.

A sound like the sky tearing in half.

White.

SFX: silence; then rain, very far away

Grey again. Morning. The boy in a cot under a window, the light flat. He sits up. His hair falls across his eyes and one lock of it is white. He touches it. Looks at his hand.

The one room. A stove gone cold. The chart table, bare. A stool pushed in. A coat hook with no coat.

THE BOY: Da?

The door is open. Beyond it, the path to the headland.

The cliff. The tripod still standing, beaded with rain. One survey sheet left under one stone, the ink run to grey clouds, the corner still reading O.B. The boy pulls it out and holds it in both hands, by the corner.

Below, the sea, going about its business.

## SCENE 3 — EXT. SIGNAL TOWER, PINHOLD — DAWN (≈ 1:35)

Colour back, thin and cold. Kaito wakes against Skarn with his hand still in the fur.

SELA VEY (28; silver-blue ponytail, cropped navigator's jacket over a black bandeau, wrap skirt, spyglass on her hip) sits on the parapet with her back to the sea, a worn logbook open on her knee, writing.

KAITO: How long have you been there.
SELA: Since the tide turned.
KAITO: You could've woken me.
SELA: You were talking. I didn't want to interrupt.

He waits for the rest of it. There isn't any. She keeps writing.

KAITO: What are you writing.
SELA: Where we are. What day it is. Who's here.
KAITO: Why?
SELA: So I'll know.

She closes the book on her thumb.

KAITO: They're really going to do it. Every beast in town.
SELA: Yes.
KAITO: Bram said they cut it. The bond. Like a rope.
SELA: Not like a rope. A rope you can splice. (beat) I've seen it done twice. I wrote both down, so I'd never need to see a third to remember.
KAITO: Does that work?
SELA: Ask me after.
KAITO: That's not an answer.
SELA: It's the one I've got.

The drum hasn't started yet. The hum has never stopped.

SELA: The Gullwake leaves on the next slack water. Bram's getting her ready. There's room on the deck for a wolf.
KAITO: I'm going to the square.
SELA: I know.
KAITO: To watch.
SELA: I know.

Below them, out of sight, a drum begins. Slow. One beat to a step.

KAITO: My da went out in a storm when I was six. Sea kept him. That's what everybody says.
SELA: And what do you say?
KAITO: (standing) Nothing. I was six.
SELA: Did he leave anything?
KAITO: A tripod. One sheet of his survey, rained to nothing. The corner says O.B. and the rest says cloud.
SELA: (looking at him properly now) You kept it.
KAITO: It's in the roof-beam at home. Where else is it going to go.
SELA: Bring it.
KAITO: Why?
SELA: Because I pay couriers for old survey sheets for a reason. A thing a man measured is worth more than a thing a man said. (she stands) And because the Sever are standing between you and your house. So you'll have to want it.

He slings the jacket's sleeves tighter at his waist and goes for the stair. Skarn rises to follow. Kaito turns and puts a hand flat on the wolf's nose.

KAITO: Stay. You come down there, they count you. I'll shout if I need you.

Skarn stays. The fin lifts, very slightly, and lies down again.

Sela watches him go. She opens the logbook, writes one more line, and follows.

## SCENE 4 — EXT. HARBOUR SQUARE, PINHOLD — DAWN (≈ 2:15)

Wide. The harbour square, the Great Pylon's grey tower standing over the roofs behind it, humming. A line of Sever troopers across the square, grey coats, white collars, the black bar and ring on every chest, visors down. Bond-cutters on their hips, hooked blades glowing faint white.

In front of them, the town, in a queue. HOB ANSER (68; a farmer, hands like roots) at the front, holding a rope. On the rope: TAMBLE, a moss-ox, low and broad, a brow plate like a shovel, a coat of green moss, a tail like a hawser. The ox leans on his leg. Behind Hob, MAREN TULL (44; the baker, flour still in her hair) with a kiln-newt on a leather lead, the newt glowing a dull stove-orange. A dozen more behind. Nobody speaks.

On the roofline across the harbour, for one shot: a smooth white mask with one eye slit, watching. Then gone.

Kaito arrives at the edge of the crowd. BRAM TORVALD (31; six and a half feet, shaved head, black beard, shirtless under leather dungarees with one strap down, a towel over his shoulder) is already there. He hands Kaito a heel of bread without looking at him.

BRAM: Eat.
KAITO: Not hungry.
BRAM: You fight worse hungry.
KAITO: I fight fine hungry.
BRAM: You fought like a dropped pie yesterday.

Kaito takes the bread. Doesn't eat it. Sela arrives at his other side. Her eyes go to Hob's forearm, where two parallel lines, a furrow, glow soft green through the old man's skin.

SELA: (low) Hob Anser. Sold Bram turnips yesterday and wouldn't take money for the small ones. That's a thirty-year bond. Look how the beast stands.
KAITO: It's leaning on him.
SELA: No. He's leaning on it.
KAITO: What happens to them. After.
SELA: You saw one. Yesterday, on the deck. Plates and cables and a white light where the beast used to keep itself.
KAITO: That was somebody's.
SELA: Everything is somebody's. That's the trouble with the Sever. They've noticed.
BRAM: Galley rule six. Nobody fights in my galley.
KAITO: This isn't your galley.
BRAM: I'm standing in it. Where I stand is the galley.

SERGEANT AUDRIC DASK (36; a Sever sergeant, visor up, a plain tired face) steps out of the line with a printed card. He reads it the way a man reads a timetable.

DASK: Pinhold. By order of the Director of the Sever, under the Meridian seal. I'll read it once. Sever Standing Order Twelve. A bond-cutter is to be held at the bond mark for a full count of ten. Subjects will scream. Partners will scream louder. Do not stop at the count of five; a half-cut bond does not make a Hollow, it makes a problem. Do not speak to the subject. Do not name the monster. Report any trooper who names the monster. By order of the Director, who reminds you that this is mercy.

He puts the card away.

DASK: First.

Hob doesn't move. The ox blinks slow at the troopers, uninterested.

HOB: He's a plough-beast. He pulls a plough. He's never hurt a soul; he can't be bothered.
DASK: (to a trooper beside him, not to Hob) Bring the subject forward.
HOB: I'm talking to you, son. You've a face. I've seen it in Maren's on a Thursday; you take the burnt loaves because they're cheaper.
DASK: (a flicker, then nothing) Do not speak to the subject.
HOB: He's forty years old. Tamble. I've had him since my father's hands went. He knows the field better than I do. You cut that, what pulls the plough?
DASK: The Empire issues ploughs.
HOB: The Empire issues a lot of things.

Two troopers take Hob by the elbows. Gently. That is the horrible part. Hob lets them, because he is a polite man, and the ox follows because the ox follows Hob.

MAREN: (behind, sharp) Hob, you don't have to—
HOB: (not turning) Course I do, Maren. Look at them.

Kaito takes a step. Bram's hand lands on his shoulder, not heavy, just there.

BRAM: Don't.
KAITO: Bram.
BRAM: Watch. You said you came to watch.

## PART B

## SCENE 5 — EXT. HARBOUR SQUARE, PINHOLD — DAWN, CONTINUOUS (≈ 2:10)

A trooper kneels, takes Hob's forearm, turns it mark-up, and sets the hook of the bond-cutter against the green furrow. The glow of the blade and the glow of the mark touch.

DASK: Begin.
TROOPER: One.

Hob's whole arm jumps. He clamps his jaw.

TROOPER: Two.

Tamble bellows. Low, then not low.

HOB: (to the ox) Steady, lad. Steady—
TROOPER: Three.

The furrow's green flickers like a lamp in wind. Hob is making a noise through his teeth now.

HOB: It's all right. It's all right, I'm here— The ox strains at the rope, towards him, not away.

TROOPER: Four.

Faces in the queue. Maren's hands over her own mouth. A fisherman turns his back and is turned round again by a trooper.

MAREN: Hob—
TROOPER: Five.
FISHERMAN: Stop. That's enough, you can see it's enough, he's—
TROOPER: Six.

Hob stops screaming. That is worse than the screaming. His mouth stays open. His eyes are on the ox.

TROOPER: Seven.

Close on Tamble's eye: brown, wet, going white from the edge inward, like frost on a window.

TROOPER: Eight.

Hob's face. Something leaving it. Not pain; pain is still there. What goes is the thing behind the pain that knew where to look. His eyes slide off the ox and find nothing to settle on.

BRAM: (very low) Kaito. Look at me. Not him.
KAITO: No.

TROOPER: Nine.

Tamble goes quiet. Stands square. Looks at nothing.

TROOPER: Ten.

The cutter lifts. Where the furrow was: two dull grey lines, a scar that looks forty years old. The trooper stands and holsters the blade.

SFX: the pylon's hum, and under it, nothing else at all

Hob looks at his own arm. Then at the ox. His hands are still holding the rope. The rope is slack.

HOB: Whose... whose is that?

A trooper takes the rope out of his hands. The ox walks where it's led, placid, and doesn't look back, because there's nothing to look back at. Hob's hands stay in the shape of holding a rope.

HOB: (to no one) He's called Tamble. He answers to it.
DASK: (flat, to his trooper) Note the subject named the monster. Not our concern. Lead it to the pen; plates and fitting at the pylon yard.
TROOPER: Sergeant.
DASK: Next.
KAITO: (barely) He doesn't know it.
SELA: No.
KAITO: It doesn't know him.
SELA: No.

Sela has the logbook open. She writes two words in it and underlines them. Over her shoulder, her hand: *Hob Anser. Tamble.*

SELA: (to herself) Not a trooper. Report that.

Maren is pushed forward with the kiln-newt. The newt's glow has gone very small. She is shaking so hard the lead rattles.

Kaito puts the bread in his pocket.

## SCENE 6 — EXT. HARBOUR SQUARE, PINHOLD — DAWN, CONTINUOUS (≈ 1:45)

Kaito walks out of the crowd and stands between Maren and the line.

KAITO: No.
DASK: Do not speak to the subject.
KAITO: I'm not the subject. I'm the idiot in front of her. (to Maren, without turning) Maren. Go home.
MAREN: Kaito, they'll—
KAITO: Yeah. Go home.
MAREN: I've known you since you were stealing crusts off my sill.
KAITO: Then you know I'm not moving. Go.
DASK: You read well, boy, for a thief. Did you read the part about mercy?
KAITO: You read that card like a ferry timetable. Thirty years of a man's life. Like a bloody timetable.
DASK: It's the same in every town. It reads the same. (beat) Stand aside, and the order says you keep your hands. That's the whole of the offer.
KAITO: Screw that.

Dask nods. The two troopers who took Hob's elbows come for Kaito's.

Kaito hits the first one in the visor with his forehead.

SFX: a crack like a dropped plate

The square goes still. Kaito, nose bleeding, grinning like it hurts.

KAITO: That's one. You lot like counting.
DASK: You've a beast somewhere. Nobody's hand lights up for nothing.
KAITO: My hand lights up because I'm pleased to see you.
DASK: (sighing) Shields.

The line locks: grey shields up, a black bar on every face of the wall. They advance.

Kaito's bond mark, a jagged three-point fork on the back of his right hand, lights electric yellow. He Surges: a short burst. He is in front of the wall and then he is behind two men, a crackle on the air, and they are down.

The shields turn. Too slow. He is already among them. Lightning goes down his arm and out through the gauntlet into a shield-face; the man behind it drops like a sack. Another shield catches him across the ribs and he goes sideways across the stones, rolls, comes up.

BRAM: (not moving, not loud) Kaito.
KAITO: Busy.
BRAM: There's twenty.
KAITO: Then I've got seventeen to go.
BRAM: Eighteen. One's getting up.
KAITO: Eighteen, then!

He goes back in. It is not a clever fight. He takes a shield-rim to the mouth. He gives a knee to a visor.

TROOPER: He's Surging—
SECOND TROOPER: Hook him! Get the hook on him!
KAITO: Twenty on one! Those are Empire odds, I thought you liked those!

Two troopers get his arms, and he Surges again, longer this time, and the scar on his forearm smokes. The fork on the back of his hand burns white. The two troopers are flung into the third.

On the edge of it, Sela has not moved. She is watching the scar, not the fight.

SELA: (quiet) Bram. The ship.
BRAM: Aye.
SELA: And wake the beetle.
BRAM: He's awake.
SELA: He's never awake.
BRAM: He's awake for this.

He goes, fast for a man that size, towel still on his shoulder.

A shield takes Kaito behind the knee. He goes down on one. A bond-cutter comes out, white, and hangs in the air a foot from his face.

DASK: Hold.
TROOPER: Sergeant, he's bleeding on the—
DASK: I can see. I said hold. (he steps through his line and looks down at Kaito) Boy. You've no beast on you. You're a sack of lightning with nothing to spend it on. Where's the wolf?
KAITO: (spitting blood) What wolf.
DASK: You think this is the cruel part. It isn't. The cruel part is thirty years of a thing that'll die on you, or you on it, and one of you left not knowing why the house is quiet. We're the kind part. The order says mercy. Go on like this and there's less of it for you.
KAITO: Say that to Hob.
DASK: I'm not permitted to speak to the subject.

Kaito looks up at him. Looks at the sky over the man's shoulder.

Dask follows the look. Too late.

## SCENE 7 — EXT. HARBOUR SQUARE, PINHOLD — DAWN, CONTINUOUS (≈ 0:35)

SFX: a sound like a sail filling

Skarn drops out of the dawn with the sail-fin full of light and lands square on the stones beside Kaito. The impact cracks the flags. The shield wall goes back three steps on its own.

Kaito gets up.

KAITO: Told you to stay.
SKARN: (a grumble)
KAITO: Yeah. Me neither.

He puts his bloody right hand on the wolf's shoulder. The fork on his hand and the fin on Skarn's spine light together.

Wide, from behind them: the two of them against the whole grey line and the pylon standing over the roofs.

KAITO: Screw your mercy.

The fin goes white.

SMASH CUT TO BLACK.
