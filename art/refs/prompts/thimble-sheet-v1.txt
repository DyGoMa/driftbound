Four full-body views of the same creature in one row, same scale, evenly spaced, not overlapping, all on the same baseline:

Creature: Thimble, an old, gentle farm plough-beast, original design. Huge and heavy: about as long as a cart-shed, low to the ground, with a broad barrel body. Long, shaggy, moss-green hair covers the body and hangs in a fringe over the upper legs; a few patches of real moss grow on her back. Four thick pillar legs like tree trunks, with wide flat hooves, dark grey. A broad, flat snout shaped like a spade, wide and blunt, dark grey-brown skin at the muzzle. Two short, worn stub horns on top of the head, smooth and pale. Small, patient brown eyes, half-lidded and kind. A short tail ending in a shaggy moss-green tuft, like a cow's tail. Around her neck, a worn leather strap holding one cracked brass bell under the chin.
- FRONT view: the bell hangs at the centre under the chin; the crack shows on the bell.
- LEFT SIDE and RIGHT SIDE views: the bell hangs under the chin at the front of the neck.
- BACK view: the strap passes over the back of the neck; the tail tuft points straight at us, so we see its round end; nothing hangs down between the back legs.
She is calm and old, a plain farm animal, not a monster: no armour, no plates, no cables, no glow.
