## 1. Hard rules (from docs/WORLD_BIBLE.md, never broken)
1. **Every character is an adult.** Stated ages 21+, adult faces and bodies. No school settings. No named child characters. Any new character gets an age of 21 or older, written in the art note on first appearance.
2. **Fan service line:** bold 2000s-anime outfits (crop tops, open jackets, short shorts, shirtless men), innuendo, flirty banter, and gag scenes that cut away. **No** nudity, no sexualized posing or camera framing, no explicit scenes. Romance fades to black. See section 6.
3. **Original only:** we borrow how great manga are *built* (structure, pacing, escalation). We copy no characters, names, symbols, techniques, plot beats-as-written or famous lines from any series. No Pokémon-like designs.
4. Mature tone is fine: violence, injury, death, swearing. No sexual violence or sexual threat, ever, including as a villain's menace.
5. Pals (cute collectibles) are never in flirting or innuendo.
6. Flashbacks to anyone's childhood are rare, plain and sad or warm. They contain zero flirting, innuendo or fan service, on that page or the facing one. Kaito as a boy is shown only in Issue 3 and Issue 37.
7. Gag faces are exaggerated *adult* faces. Never chibi or super-deformed.

## 2. What we borrow from the greats (structure only)
- **One Piece:** an island per arc; each crewmate is won by the hero standing up to the person ruining their life; a dream shouted on page one; running gags; a serial told on the title pages; a letters column; huge heart, huge laughs, then a gut punch.
- **Naruto:** a rival who is the hero's dark mirror; a neutral tournament arc that gets invaded; a teacher who pushes then dies; a battered hero who answers with stubbornness; officers ranked and hunted one by one.
- **Naruto Shippuden:** a hard two-year time skip after the crew is broken. Everyone returns changed: new looks, new powers, the rival further gone, the world worse.
- **Fullmetal Alchemist:** a magic system built on cost; a mentor-villain; a conspiracy hiding in plain sight on the map; horror chapters; every early detail pays off later; the villain is sincere.

## 3. The spine (the secrets, in full; the reader learns them slowly)
**The Count.** Sever Standing Order 12 says a bond-cutter is held on the bond mark for a full count of ten, and warns "Do not stop at the count of five; a half-cut bond does not make a Hollow, it makes a problem." Director Vasch is doing this to the whole world. The Empire says its giant anchor pylons pin islands for safety and trade. Really, ten **Great Pylons**, each planted on a chosen island on an **Anchor Day**, are one bond-cutter the size of the sea.

**The First Bond.** The Drift is not geology. The islands drift because they are carried by one sleeping world-sized being, **the Sleeper**, bonded long ago to the first islanders. Every human-monster bond is a thread of that First Bond. The moving islands follow its slow pulse, a rhythm felt as a very low hum: **the Undersong**. Pin ten points and cut, and the whole bond strips at once: no more partners anywhere, and one Hollow the size of the world under Empire control. Vasch sincerely calls this mercy: nobody ever pays a bond's price again.

**The pattern.** Plotted on a chart, the ten Great Pylon sites draw the Sever's own symbol: a straight line cutting a circle. It has been on their chests since Issue 1. Kaito finds this in Issue 35 because of his dream: he is the only idiot trying to chart the whole Drift.

**The map that stays right is a song.** The islands move to the Undersong. A map that stays right is not a drawing but a score. Kaito's dream is real and it is the cure.

**Kaito's father.** **Oren Brask** (54 now), an Empire surveyor, surveyed the Drift and found the pulse. He left when Kaito was small. Kaito thinks he died at sea. Vasch sited the pylons with Oren's survey, so the dream Kaito inherited built the weapon. Reveal: Issue 37. Oren is alive, working in Meridian under guard, and is seen in Issue 47.

**Who Silt is.** Silt's real name is **Corin Vale** (29). He was Sela's first mate and lover eight years ago, a son of the **Cantors**, drift-singers who steered by the Undersong. When Sela was 20 she went deeper than anyone ever had to save the old Gullwake, and that Surge drowned every memory of him. He stood on the deck and she asked him who he was. He tore the page with his name out of her logbook. She had written a warning to herself first: "Ask him about the song. He will say you made it up. You did not." He told her once that if she ever forgot, he would say it never happened, so it would not hurt her. Then he walked away. Vasch found him. His partner, the hound **Burr**, he stripped himself, full count of ten.
- "Nobody knows his real name" is literal: the only person who knew it paid it away.
- **"Pup."** Sela calls Kaito "pup" and doesn't know why she likes the word. It was her name for Corin. She got it from him; the habit survived and the man didn't. Silt hears it in Issue 19 and his composure breaks for the first time.
- Payoff: Issue 41, Sela asks the masked hunter about the song. He says: "You made it up." Then he hums it, without meaning to, on the last panel. She still doesn't remember him. She never gets the memories back. That is the rule of this story: **costs stay paid.**

**Ione's wrist is the proof.** Vasch stopped at five on Ione by accident. Her half-cut bond is unstable but it lives, and it can *regrow*. Her crystal growths aren't only damage, they're a bond rebuilding in the wrong shape. So a half-cut world might also regrow. In Part Two this is the only hope.

**The crew stops it at five.** At the end of Part One the crew wrecks the sixth Great Pylon at the cost of the ship and each other. The count stops at five. Exactly what the order warned of happens: **the Problem**. In the two-year gap, the half-cut Sleeper thrashes. Islands collide, currents reverse, wild monsters go mad, and people blame the Gullwake crew. They did save the world, and they also broke it. Vasch only needs five more.

## 5. Voices (keep them distinct; a reader should know the speaker with the name covered)
- **Kaito:** short, loud, sweary, bad at subtlety, dumb jokes under pressure. Says "Screw that." Declares things. Blushes and walks into things around flirting.
- **Sela:** dry, unhurried, warm, always one step ahead. Teases. Calls him "pup." Never raises her voice until it matters. Writes things down mid-conversation.
- **Juno:** quotes odds constantly ("Five to one you're about to do something stupid"). "Sweetheart." Filthy jokes, big laugh, deflects all pain with a bet.
- **Silas:** silky, "darling," innuendo as breathing, elaborate sentences, faint rasp. When he's serious the sentences get very short. When Surged out, he writes on a slate or says nothing.
- **Bram:** few words, gentle, food metaphors, misty-eyed. Quiet "Towel's down" means somebody is about to die.
- **Ione:** deadpan, clinical vocabulary used as innuendo ("I can be very thorough"), footnotes herself, unaware or pretending to be. Guilt under every line.
- **Skarn etc.:** partners don't talk. They act, grumble and judge. Their reactions are comedy gold.
- **Vasch:** gentle, patient, "mercy," speaks like a good doctor. Never gloats. **Hekka:** blunt soldier, few words, bleak humour. **Silt:** flat, sparse, tired, sometimes cruelly kind. **Krell:** drill-sergeant bluster. **Maro:** tallies people as numbers, polite.

## 6. Fan service (Dylan asked for it; deliver it inside the line)
Target: at least one real moment in most issues, a set-piece every arc, and a lighter touch in the grimmest issues (Issues 32, 41, 42 and the horror chapters have only a dry joke or none). Equal-opportunity: the men get it too. Comedy first, characters in charge of themselves. Fun tools:
- Outfits, formalwear and disguises: casino gowns and waiter vests, regatta swimwear, festival wraps, wet-from-the-sea shirts, Bram shirtless at the stove, Silas's shirt that's never done up.
- Innuendo: Juno's bets, Ione's "science", Silas's everything, Sela's teasing, Otti's shamelessness.
- Cutaway gags: bathhouse and hot-spring scenes in towels and heavy steam, shot from the shoulders up, cutting at the key moment to Skarn's disgusted face, a kettle boiling over, Grist asleep, or a black panel with only a caption or an offscreen line. The joke is what we *don't* see.
- Kaito walking into masts, Kaito's ears going red, the crew's running betting pool on who he'll embarrass himself in front of.
- Romance tease: Kaito and Juno slow burn with a betting pool on it. Silas flirts with everyone of every gender. Sela and Marisol have history. Ione and Silas have a dry, slowly real thing. One fade-to-black only in the outline: Kaito and Juno, Issue 40.

**How to write these in art notes:** normal eye-level or medium shots, comedy staging, faces and reactions. Never write a camera lingering on a body part, never a pose for the viewer, no undressing shown, no nudity, no see-through wet clothing, no looking up skirts, no peeping played as a reward. If someone walks in on someone, it's an accident, the person walked in on is amused and in control, and the walker pays for it comically. All flirting between adults who are enjoying it. Bond marks below Sela's and Juno's collarbones are plot details: frame them as such. No fan service aimed at a captive, injured or frightened person. Nothing of this kind in any scene with a Pal.

## 7. Craft rules for every issue (how the greats pace a chapter)
- **Length:** 22 to 26 story pages. Average 4 to 6 panels a page; action pages 2 to 4; one big splash or spread per issue at the emotional peak.
- **Hook:** something happens on page 1. Title splash by page 3 at the latest.
- **Page turns:** the book reads right to left, so odd-numbered pages are the ones you see *after* a page turn. Put reveals and splashes on odd pages. End even pages on a question.
- **End on a hook** every issue: the last panel makes you need the next one.
- **Fights tell a story:** a plan, a reversal, a cost paid on the page. Bond costs are shown every time someone Surges big, and they stay paid.
- **Silence:** let panels breathe. Use wordless panels, especially around grief.
- **Words:** no more than about 25 words in a panel, 3 balloons in a panel, 18 words in a balloon, except rare monologues. No narrating what the art shows.
- **Each issue has a shape:** a joke, a heart beat, a turn. Even a fight issue gets a laugh; even a gag issue moves the plot or a relationship.
- **Back matter:** every issue ends with a title-page serial and a letters column (format doc).

## 8. The outline
Part One: **THE MAP THAT LIES** (Issues 1 to 42). Part Two: **THE LONG WAKE** (Issues 43 on, two years later). Each issue's beats are mandatory; invent everything between them. Fan-service notes marked FS.

### Arc 1: PINHOLD (Issues 1 to 5). Kaito joins the crew.
1. **"The Islands Move."** Cold open: Kaito outruns Empire customs across a Pinhold rooftop with a stolen parcel, Skarn gliding above him, shouting his dream at no one: the first map of the Drift that stays right. Pinhold is being pinned: grey soil, dead wells. The whole town has a chart on the wall, and the charts are finally "correct" because the island no longer moves, which is the horror nobody says. Kaito delivers medicine and gets paid in eggs. The parcel's client: Sela Vey. She orders a very strange thing: old survey sheets. FS: she teases him and he walks into a post. Night: the Great Pylon starts up. First hum. Ending splash: a white-masked rider on a cable-veined hound looking down at Skarn's roost: "Found you."
2. **"Ears Go Red First."** Silt hunts them through the town. Kaito fights back like an idiot, loses, Surges hard to get away; a new fork burns up his arm (reader learns bond cost, sees scars at the elbow). He wakes on the Gullwake in Bram's galley: a giant, stew, a beetle-truck asleep on deck. FS: wandering the ship, he opens the wrong door on Sela in a tub on deck behind a sail-screen; cut to Skarn's face, offscreen laugh; she carries on negotiating his wages, Kaito in the mast. Sela offers a berth: he refuses, because Pinhold's his. Bram says Sever cut partners. Kaito sees a Hollow up close for the first time: it had been somebody's. End: Sever posts the order: all monsters on Pinhold to be surrendered by dawn.
3. **"Standing Order 12."** Kaito's backstory: a boy on a cliff with his father Oren the surveyor, a chart, a storm, a lightning strike, a white streak. Father gone by morning (plain sad flashback, no jokes). Present: dawn. The town queue with their pets and working beasts. Trooper reads Standing Order 12. We see a stripping, on an old farmer's plough-beast, the full count of ten, panel by panel, and the farmer's face emptying. Horror peak. Kaito snaps and attacks a whole line of troopers. Ending: splash of Skarn landing beside him. "Screw your mercy."
4. **"Run."** Full Pinhold run set-piece, echoing the game Storm Run: Kaito across rooftops, barricades, cables, carrying the town's last partners out to the Gullwake with Bram and Grist holding the pier. Sela and Vesh rise out of the harbour, reader's first look at how huge Vesh is (spread). Silt catches Kaito at the pylon base. He asks "Why do you run? It's the same sea." Kaito lands three hits on the hound's core using lightning, and it cracks. Silt, unbothered, leaves. Sela calls "Pup!" from the ship for the first time. End: the pylon finishes. The island stops. Every chart on the wall is right.
5. **"First Count."** The town's aftermath: the island is dead still; birds gone; people weep or celebrate. A worldquake rolls through the sea, felt by every partner at once: Skarn howls, Vesh screams, and on a far island a bear in chains looks up. Vasch, first seen, marks a line on a chart in a white room: "One." Kaito tries to go home and his neighbours bar the door: he brought the Sever. He signs on, signs his name on the ship's roster under "deckhand", sets up a blank chart. FS/gag: Bram measures him for a uniform, the crew bets on it, there isn't a uniform. Sela writes it in the logbook. We see the first line: "Ask him about the song." She doesn't know whose hand wrote it. Ends on her thumb on the old torn edge.

### Arc 2: ASHMARKET (Issues 6 to 10). Juno joins. Stakes: gambling, debt, and fire.
6. **"Ten to One."** New island. Ashmarket: a gamblers' city on a volcanic crag, lanterns and soot. The crew needs parts, has no money. Juno Calloway introduced cheating a Sever officer at cards and winning, then bets Kaito (he bets her first) that he can't last ten seconds against her. He loses his boots, then his jacket, then his shirt, comedy strip game with the whole bar betting. FS: Kaito shirtless and furious, she keeps his goggles as a "deposit." Heart beat: she never takes off those gloves. End: Warden Krell's shield line marches in. Juno's debts are bought. Her marker now reads the Sever.
7. **"The House Always Wins."** Juno's owed to Krell: she has until the Ember Night to pay or he takes Ashka. Nim Hadda explains the city's rigged. Crew plan: the High Table at the Cinder Palace. They need disguises. FS set-piece: formalwear makeover. Sela in a backless midnight gown with a slit, Juno in sparkling red, Bram bursting out of a waiter's vest, Kaito in a too-tight waistcoat, plus a lot of ribbing. Grist as a coffee table. End: at the door they're greeted by the owner; he has already seen their pictures.
8. **"High Table."** The game itself, tense card duel, simple rules made clear to the reader. Juno against Krell's bought dealer with Sela reading tells and Kaito failing to keep a poker face. Juno wins on a bluff so reckless it stops the room. Then Krell turns the table over: "Cheat" and the stakes shift to the shield line. Reveal: why the gloves. She pulls them off to fight: burned, blistered palms. Splash: fire from bare hands.
9. **"Hot Hand."** Sever raid on Ashmarket; street battle, echoing the game Hot Hand. Juno's heat rising. Numbers on-panel as she counts her own odds, fever climbing. Kaito and Juno team up badly. Ashka spotting. Krell's shields can't be burned: overload the heat vents below the market. To do it she must hold more fire than she can. Her cost shown: she's burning from the inside; Bram carries her into the harbour. Krell retreats, scorched, alive, sworn to come back.
10. **"Marker Paid."** Quiet after. Juno in fever, Bram cooling her with wet towels, a sincere talk with Kaito: at nineteen her father staked her labour on a card game and lost, and she worked the Ashmarket furnaces for years to pay it off, which is where the burn tolerance began (spoken only, not shown; no flirting on that page). Why she bets: because you can't lose what you've already wagered. Nim forgives her debt. She "wins" a place on the crew by cheating Kaito at cards, gets his goggles back to him, and a gag: she moves in and takes the best bunk. FS: the crew's first full bath-night on deck, with a roster and a lot of steam, cut to Grist, Kaito's ears. Starts betting pool: "Kaito and who?" End: Sela's chart shows a new Great Pylon being raised at Gaptooth. Second quake. Vasch: "Two."

### Arc 3: GAPTOOTH REACH (Issues 11 to 14). Sela's cost. Hekka.
11. **"Gaptooth."** Gaptooth Reach: two island chains grinding past each other, teeth of rock, the Gullwake's only way east. Vesh in full. Sela at her best: how she feels the Drift. She teaches Kaito to read currents; he starts drawing the real chart. FS: Sela's teasing lesson with a very bad metaphor, Juno commentary; he falls overboard. Heart beat: he catches her rereading her own logbook to check her own favourite food. End: harpoons out of the mist. Hekka Dorn's cutter, with the chained bear on deck.
12. **"Rival."** Hekka and Sela's first duel. Their brutal respect; Hekka flirts like a soldier and Sela flirts back mid-swordfight (adult banter, full armour, no framing). The story of the bear is told by Hekka herself, plainly, while fighting. Ring scar on her midriff. The Gullwake is cut loose; Kaito's arm numbs; Bram holds the deck. To save the ship from the closing Gap, Sela must Dive deep. Kaito: "What's it cost?" Sela: "I'll let you know. If I remember."
13. **"The Bill."** The Dive, nearly silent, gorgeous: Vesh and Sela sinking past drowned islands. Far below they hear the Undersong; she almost understands it. Spread: a shape too big to be a shape. Up they burst through the cutter's hull. Hekka thrown off, bear hauling her out by the chain, she isn't even angry. Sela on deck, soaked, laughing. She looks at Bram. "Who taught me to make that stew?" Nobody knows. Then: she has lost the memory of the entire year she learned navigation, and she doesn't know it yet, but the reader sees a filled-in logbook page now reading as a stranger's.
14. **"Reading It Back."** Quiet character issue. The crew finds out how this really works. Kaito is furious with her, she's calm. She shows him all the logbooks, a whole trunk, in her hand, about a person she takes on trust. A funny sad story of her reading the same joke fresh twice. Vesh curls around the ship at night. FS: lightly: Juno tries to give her "a memory worth keeping" with a very bad dance and a very good bottle, Silas isn't here, Bram sings, a sweetly silly drunk night; Sela wakes up, writes it down. End: Kaito finds the torn-page edge and the note, she shrugs: "Old tear." In Lanternreach, a smuggler gets a letter, looks up, faint rasp: "Well. That's inconvenient, darling."

### Arc 4: LANTERNREACH (Issues 15 to 19). Silas. Silence. Silt returns.
15. **"Port of Lamps."** Night port; no one here uses names; everyone owes Ledger Maro. Silas Moreau introduced talking his way out of being stabbed by three people, including one he slept with. He flirts with Kaito on sight and Bram on purpose. Kaito hates him; he has stolen the Gullwake's cargo. Gag chase. FS: Silas's unbuttoned everything; his bedroom escape rooftop chase with a sheet of love letters. End: Kaito catches him at the docks, in time to see Silas let eleven caged monsters out of a Sever pen for no one.
16. **"The Steam Ledger."** Heist: Maro's real ledger is kept in the Lantern Baths, where deals get made because nobody can hide a wire in a towel. FS set-piece: the whole crew in the bathhouse in towels and steam, with a cheerful disaster: mix-ups, Juno betting on whether Silas can hold his breath, Bram's beard, Ione not here yet, Sela effortlessly running the room. Every near-reveal cuts to a kettle, Skarn's face, a steam-whistle panel. They get the ledger. End: it holds the name of every smuggler who sold to the Sever, and Silas's own name on page one.
17. **"Ledger."** Silas's past: he sold partners for Maro, until the night of the eleven. Maro, polite, shows up with the bill: hand over the crew. Silas agrees, smiling, and sells them out on the page. Reader believes it. The Gullwake crew is trapped in the warehouse. Lucan-free, this is Maro's show. End: Silas walks away; Kaito's voice, furious: "I KNEW it." Silas, alone, hand to his throat, starts to breathe out a long slow Surge.
18. **"…"** The silent issue. Almost no dialogue, Silas's Surge steals his voice as he stalks through Maro's lamp-lit warehouse, cutting every lamp and guard with chain and wind, echoing the game Silent Cut. All balloons from him are empty or "...". Others shout at him and we see him unable to answer. He frees the crew, gets Maro at last. Maro, in a calm balloon: "Who will you tell?" A last-page cut. Afterward, he writes on a slate: "Darling, I was never going to" and rubs it out. Kaito gives him the slate back: "Your handwriting's shit. Get on the boat."
19. **"Pup."** Silas joins, silent for days, driving everyone mad with slate innuendo. FS: gag: slate-flirting with Bram at the stove while Bram stirs, shirtless; he gets his voice back to say one filthy thing and loses it again. Silt attacks at the docks: Kaito vs Silt, round two. Kaito's improved; he's still losing. Sela shouts "Pup, left!" from the mast. Silt stops dead mid-strike. First crack: for one panel the flat voice is gone: "What did she call you?" He leaves without the kill. Kaito gets away with a burned arm and a bad feeling. Last page: Silt, alone, sits on the hound, takes off one glove, looks at an old page torn from a logbook. Face unseen.

20. **"Everyone's Invited."** Ships are short of parts and Kaito needs the Regatta's prize: the **Longwake Chart**, the best map in the world. The Regatta is run every seven years on the neutral atoll of Saltlight. Empire, smugglers, crews, all bound by truce. Arrive at festival. Meet Otti Ferro, in a bar, drunk, trying to pick up Bram, then Silas, then Kaito. Meet Tobias Quill and Flint, Red Tally crew, Marisol and Sela's history. FS: Regatta opening beach party. Swimwear parade; the crew bet; Bram in a tiny striped suit, Silas in very little, Sela and Marisol's bikini-tops-and-shirts rivalry, Kaito burned on the nose. End: rules of the Regatta are read, and Hekka Dorn signs up.
21. **"First Leg."** The race starts, a mass of ships and partners. Kaito on Skarn against everyone, in a huge fun chaos of creatures. Juno betting against her own crew. Tobias and Kaito's rivalry starts. Wren and Dove Aster and Pell. Kaito cheats by accident, wins by being an idiot. Quiet bit: Otti watches him fly: "That boy's hurting himself on purpose." End: Otti finds him that night, slaps his numb arm: "Do you want it back? Then come here, you beautiful moron." Kaito goes the colour of his jacket.
22. **"Otti."** Training. Otti teaches the Short Surge: small bursts on the beat, not one long burn (the first hint that there's a rhythm). Comic training hell, plus plenty of her flirting. FS: training-on-the-beach montage, wet sand, Otti's commentary on everyone's bodies, the crew's commentary back. Heart beat: the empty harness, her partner **Merrow** whom the Sever cut. Doctor Pike shows his work. The Ostlers stitch a Hollow. Lucan Ash bows. The Ten Count revealed in one black spread with Vasch's voice giving the order: "Begin the count."
26. **"Invasion."** Saltlight burns. All crews fight the Sever together: Red Tally, Tobias, Asters. Hollows made from racers' partners that morning, recognized by their owners on the page. Worst moment: Tobias's friend. Doctor Pike fights Juno, and Lucan Ash vs Silas (Silas outclassed, loses his voice again). Otti, no partner, walks through it with a boathook, terrifyingly. Bram puts the towel down.
27. **"Third Count."** The neutral atoll was the Great Pylon's site all along, drilled under the reef. It rises through the burning festival: spread. The Longwake Chart burns, but Kaito grabs a piece. Quake. Vasch, watching from a boat, meets Kaito's eye and tips a gloved hand. Survivors at sea at dawn. Tobias joins the fight in his own ship. Otti gets on the Gullwake uninvited: "You're out of booze. I'm fixing that." Hooded woman hands Sela a note: "Keelbreak. They're coming for your ship next. Dr. I. Raske."

### Arc 6: KEELBREAK (Issues 28 to 32). Bram. The first real death.
28. **"Dry Dock."** The Gullwake limps to Keelbreak: shipyard island, Bram's home. Old Tam Reed curses him lovingly for the state of his ship. FS: everyone stripped to work clothes on the docks, sweat and tar, Bram and Silas shirtless and swapping jokes, Juno and Otti rating them with scorecards; Kaito's score is low. Bram's iron has spread: Tam sees it right away, says nothing, moves him to lighter work; he doesn't go.
29. **"Galley Rules."** Comedy issue with a heart: Bram tries to feed all of Keelbreak. Cook-off vs Tam; every character's kitchen-related shame. How he became crew: lost his family's ship and his wife to the Empire's first pinning, drinking in Tam's yard, he was 22; Tam handed him a hammer. Then his bond: Grist was a broken-legged stray he refused to put down. Sela, Kaito, a sober talk: Bram's knee has locked. He hides it with a stool. End: Sever scouts arrive and the yard's workers sell them out, or so it looks.
30. **"Hold the Deck."** The siege starts. Echoes the game Hold the Deck: three lanes to the galley, wave after wave, Bram's iron against everything, and each use he takes a step slower. Grist charges. A joke between waves: he stops the battle to feed people. Keelbreak's yard folk come back and hold up the other side: they didn't sell anyone out.
31. **"The Bear."** Hekka's bear breaks through the deck in phases. It doesn't know her; she goes on feeding it. Fight with Bram: the bear nearly tears his iron off. To win, Bram goes all-iron: half his torso is grey and won't come back. Old Tam, a hammer in hand, comes down into the hold and gets between the bear and him.
32. **"Towel."** Old Tam dies, plain, ugly, no last speech, she gets one rude word out. Bram puts the towel down. Silent splash of Bram stone-faced, walking at the Sever. Hekka looks at what has been done and calls the retreat herself, against orders; she can't watch it. Grief and funeral at sea. Bram can't cook for three days, and the crew cooks; it's terrible and they eat it. No fan service. End: Ione arrives on the deck, hands up: "I wrote the procedure. You may shoot me afterward. First, I need you to look at a map."

### Arc 7: THE GLASS WARD (Issues 33 to 38). Ione. Horror. The truth.
33. **"Dr. Raske."** Crew nearly kills Ione. She offers her wrist in evidence. Deadpan one-liners under guard. FS: Silas interrogates her in the galley, sparks fly: two people trying to out-innuendo each other, she wins with a fact about tissue. Her offer: the Glass Ward, a dead lab where the Director's first experiments are. It has the survey. Juno bets she betrays them; Ione takes the bet herself.
34. **"Mother Kell."** On the way: Cantor Isle, ghost-village, one old blind woman, Mother Kell. She hums and Skarn lies down. Sela hears it and cries and doesn't know why. Kell touches her face. "You. You poor girl, you paid it all." She'll say no more. The Undersong explained as a story, a Cantors' legend of the Sleeper. FS: comic lightly, a village bath with an old lady who can't see a thing and comments anyway. End: The Glass Ward: a whole island of greenhouse-lab grown over with crystal.
35. **"Line Through a Circle."** Horror chapter. The Glass Ward is full of half-finished things, half-stripped people who survived, walking crystal, Prism's old nest. Dr. Pike's old notes. Kaito, safe in a closet with his chart and the survey, plots all pylons as he has for months, adds the survey's points, draws lines. Page-turn splash: the Sever's symbol, across the whole Drift. "The whole goddamn sea is a bond mark."
36. **"Mercy."** Vasch waiting in the lab. Sits and talks. He isn't lying about any of it. Ione's backstory: she loved him as a father, volunteered at 23, he stopped at five. Vasch offers each crew member a free strip, no pain: their costs gone. Shows Bram's chart, Sela's memory, Kaito's arm. Temptation issue, FMA-level dialogue. He offers Ione her life. Each answer. Ends: Bram's silence, Vasch's small smile: "I can wait." Then the Hollows wake up.
37. **"Oren."** Fights through the Ward, echoing the game Split Light: light puzzle with Prism and Ione's growing shards. Vasch's last gift: the survey's signature, shown to Kaito in a small panel: **Oren Brask**. Flashback: the cliff, the boy, the chart, what the father was actually running from. Kaito's world falls in. He Surges past any safe limit, tries to kill Vasch and fails: the mechanical arm catches his fist: "He said you'd have his temper."
38. **"Half."** Ione breaks from the Director at last, using a crystal shield she can't hold, and shards grow up her neck. They get out. Dead arm: Kaito's right arm fully numb, won't come back for days. Ione's signed on by Bram with a bowl of soup, which is the whole trial. Kaito can't hold a pen; Juno holds it for him, her hand on his to draw the line of the map: first real moment. Crew now knows the Count: five sites done or nearly done. Next: the sixth, Anvil Rock, in nine weeks.

### Arc 8: COUNT OF FIVE (Issues 39 to 42). End of Part One.
39. **"All Hands."** Everyone they've met gets gathered: Red Tally, Tobias, Asters, Nim. Big war-council comedy and heart. Everyone's flags. FS: last party on the eve of sailing, in Otti's words "drink, swim, regret." Beach-night fun, loose coats, all the running gags pay off, the betting pool nearly closed. Yevka Sorn's fleet appears on the horizon. Fourth quake, then the fifth, faster than expected. Vasch: "Five."
40. **"Night Before."** Quiet, gorgeous issue of one-on-ones. Bram and Tobias's cookout; Silas and Ione's dry mutual confession as a technical argument; Sela writes letters to herself; Otti's harness; Juno and Kaito at last. Sparks, jokes, a bet she lets him win, door closes, fade to black, cut to Skarn and Ashka on the roof, unimpressed. Juno collects the pool: she bet on herself. Morning. Yevka Sorn at Anvil Rock sees a Hollow made of a pylon worker and questions it.
41. **"The Sixth Pylon."** Biggest battle. Double-page spreads. Every thread: Juno vs Krell's rematch, Krell dies burning. Silas vs Lucan Ash. Sela goes to Silt and asks him about the song. "You made it up." On the last panel, he hums it. Otti holds the gap with a boathook and a stolen flag so the Gullwake can get under, and dies at the end of it, flask raised, panel goes white. No jokes after this.
42. **"Do Not Stop At Five."** Kaito and Skarn race the pylon on a dead arm, and Skarn is hit by a bond-cutter, the count starts on him. Kaito goes up anyway, runs lightning through his dead arm, all the way to the shoulder, and blows the pylon's core. The count stops at five, world and Skarn too. Silence. Then the sea breaks. Gigantic spread: islands crash. The Gullwake is torn apart. Each crew member flung in separate panels, in a separate direction. Silt lifts a half-cut Skarn away on a chain. Kaito wakes on a shingle, right arm dead to the shoulder, mark dark. Last page: black. "TWO YEARS LATER."

### Arc 9: DEADWATER (Issues 43 to 48). Part Two starts. Everyone is 2 years older.
New looks (mandatory): **Kaito (23):** longer hair tied back, red coat worn properly, right arm in a brass-and-leather rig he works with the left; can Surge only in short beats, scars past the shoulder onto his chest. **Sela (30):** silver hair cut to the jaw, officer's long coat worn open over her bandeau, a trunk of logbooks she no longer fully trusts. **Juno (25):** long open duster, crop top, hair cropped, pit-boss now of a floating gambling hulk. **Silas (26):** voice almost gone, soft rasp, wears a scarf and still no buttons. **Bram (33):** half-iron, left side grey to the jaw, walks with a stick, runs a soup kitchen. **Ione (28):** crystal up her neck to the jaw, hair longer, still in a coat. Her mark is growing back.
43. **"The Long Wake."** Opening cold: two years later the world's chaos. Islands collide, sea-quakes, monsters mad. Posters of "the Gullwake crew who broke the world". Kaito sailing solo with a one-armed rig, chart in a tube, drawing a new chart nobody has ever needed more. A town tries to lynch him; he stops a wild monster for them and walks. FS: lighter, a bar woman or two recognize the jacket, he's still bad at it. End: a voice: "Odds you're still an idiot: even." Juno, a bit older, with a shotgun smile and a hulk full of refugees.
44. **"Bad Hand."** Reunion comedy, and a real fight, over two years of silence: she thought he was dead; he didn't write because he couldn't hold a pen. Juno's floating casino hulk, the Second Chance, its charity grift. FS: casino set-piece again, older, bolder, with reversals: Kaito waits tables shirtless to pay his tab, she runs the room in her duster. They're not together anymore, they don't know what they are. End: a job: Bram's kitchen on Dunmarrow has gone quiet.
45. **"Soup."** Bram, half iron, feeding a whole collapsed island on nothing, refusing to fight, and Grist, slower now. Sever "mercy clinics" offer free strips to starving people, and they're accepting. Worse: it's working. Yevka Sorn, defected, comes to warn them. Deacon Sorrel comes in person; last rites; Bram sets a bowl down in front of her. Quietest villain scene in the series. Then Bram stands up, finally, and everyone hears the joints.
46. **"Whoever You Trust."** Rescue of Silas from Lanternreach, now flooded; Silas sold a fake identity to everyone, runs a silent-theatre of smugglers. FS: gag, Silas's slate-flirt reunion with Bram, Ione's dry letters revealed as romance. Infiltration, then a shock: Sela has come back, smiling, calling Kaito pup, and she's not Sela. Ten: Sable Oriel. A horrible good fake who has read the logbooks; Kaito knows because the fake remembers something real Sela no longer does.
47. **"The Cartographer."** The real Sela found, leading a ragged convoy using charts by hand; she's lost more; has to read that she knows Kaito. Warm, funny, devastating. Ione's report: her mark's regrowing, so the world might. Infiltration of a Meridian records barge: first sight of **Oren Brask**, alive, old, chained to a chart table, drawing the next site. He looks up at a noise. Kaito's face on the far side of the glass. End on the two men looking at one another.
48. **"Half-Cut."** Sever raid, and the rider who comes for them is Silt on a new mount: Skarn. Half-cut. One eye blank, one eye yellow. Skarn doesn't attack; doesn't stop either. Kaito, dead arm, one beat at a time, walks up and takes the bite. Mark flickers, just once, and the hum comes up through the hull. Silt, voice flat, mask cracked: "Six is at Meridian. Bring the song." He leaves on Skarn. Crew together on a stolen hull, at last. They need a ship. Kaito opens the chart, a thing that's half a map and half a stave of music. "The islands move. Fine. Let's go learn the tune." To be continued, final panel is the first line of the logbook, in Sela's hand, new ink.

If time remains beyond Issue 48, a later batch will extend the outline; writers never invent past their own issue's beats.
