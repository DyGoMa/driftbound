## 1. Ione at a glance (locked by the cast file)
26, woman, 168 cm, slim. Pale skin, sharp grey eyes, thin round glasses, messy black bob with one crystal-white lock on the left. Open lab coat, rolled sleeves, cropped black low-cut V-neck, high-waisted shorts with a vial belt, dark stockings, buckled boots. Mark: a cracked hexagon on the back of her left wrist, crystal growing from the cracks and up the left side of her neck. Light beams and crystal shields in short bursts; every Surge grows painful crystal. Part Two (28): crystal to the jaw, hair longer, mark growing back.

## 2. Birthplace and family
Born on **Hallam Stair**, a small Empire-held island of terraced glass kilns three days' sail from Meridian. It smells of hot sand; everyone there has burn scars on their forearms and good eyes for colour.

- **Teodor Raske** (father, 58): a lens-grinder. Quiet, exact, counts under his breath while he works. Ground her first glasses when she was nine and every pair since; her thin round frames are his pattern. Writes one letter a season about the weather and the price of sand, never about the Empire.
- **Maren Raske** (mother, 56): harbour lamp-keeper, bonded to **Wick**, a grey lamp-heron whose feathers glow. Maren Surged every foggy night for twenty years to bring the kiln-boats in. Wick's cost is sight. By the time Ione was eleven her mother was blind. She never once considered giving Wick up. She keeps the lamp by feel, and Wick, old now, sleeps on the rail outside her door.
- No siblings.

Not a sad family; a careful one. Ione's deadpan is her father's, her refusal to let anyone else pay her bills her mother's.

## 3. The years before we meet her (ages at each event)
- **Age 6.** Notices her mother reaching for the kettle a hand's width left of it. Says nothing; starts moving things to where her mother expects them. First footnote, on the shelf: "*kettle. moved. sorry.*"
- **Age 9.** First glasses, ground by her father. The world has edges; she decides edges are the only honest thing.
- **Age 11.** Her mother asks her to describe Wick's light, because she can't see it anymore. Ione does it badly, then spends a year learning the words for colour so she can do it properly. (Memory one.)
- **Age 14.** Reads all four books on bonding in the island's lending-chest and writes to Meridian asking why marks glow. Nobody answers.
- **Age 17 to 19.** Apprentice lens-grinder under her father. Builds a prism rig in the kiln loft and measures the colour of her mother's mark when Maren Surges. Writes it up, footnoted to death, as "On the Light of a Bond Mark," and posts it to Meridian. Someone answers.
- **Age 19.** **Director Aurel Vasch** (then 39) sails to Hallam Stair himself and tells Maren her daughter has the steadiest hands he has seen. He asks Maren, gently, whether she would like her eyes back. Maren: "No. Thank you, Director." He nods and never asks again. Ione misreads it as kindness.
- **Age 20.** Joins the Empire bond labs in Meridian under Vasch. He calls her "Doctor" two years before she earns it. He teaches her to hold a bond-cutter as he taught her to hold a pen: "Steady is kind. Hesitation is cruelty."
- **Age 21.** Posted to the **Glass Ward**, the greenhouse-lab island of Vasch's first experiments, as keeper of its records. She reads every half-strip file: people who survived a count that stopped early, and what grew on them. Finds a glass mantis-moth nymph in the vents under the greenhouse floor.
- **Age 22.** Doctorate by thesis. Bonds Prism by accident, asleep. Writes the field text of **Sever Standing Order 12** from the Ward's records, including the warning she has read a hundred times in those files: "a half-cut bond does not make a Hollow, it makes a problem." Captain **Hekka Dorn** (then 29) walks into her lab with her bear at heel, puts the cutter in Ione's hand, takes it back, and does it herself, standing, by Ione's procedure. Ione stops sleeping properly.
- **Age 23.** Volunteers as the first subject of Vasch's painless "clean strip." He stops at five (section 6). Her left forelock goes white within the week.
- **Age 23 to 25.** Stays. Vasch studies her, calls her his best work, means it. The crystal starts at the wrist; she tells herself it is damage. At 24 she drafts the consent form for the future "mercy clinics," the wording on the posters in Part Two.
- **Age 25.** Sees the Great Pylon drawings and recognises the drill head: her own hold-geometry from Order 12, scaled to the size of a harbour. She does not know what the pylons cut; she knows the shape. She walks out of the Meridian labs that night with Prism in a crate stencilled GLASSWARE, FRAGILE. Nobody stops her; nobody has ever stopped Dr. Raske carrying glassware.
- **Age 25 to 26.** A year on the run, hooded, watching bonded people's marks whenever the sea quakes, trying to prove the marks and the pylons answer each other. She needs someone who charts. Regatta (Issue 23), the note (Issue 27), Keelbreak (Issue 32).

## 4. The bond, and what it first cost
Prism was a nymph the length of her thumb when she found it, clear as water. She fed it light. It sat on the lamp over her desk and split the beam across the ceiling, and she worked under rainbows and pretended not to notice. One night at 22 she fell asleep at the desk with Prism on her wrist and woke with a whole ice-blue hexagon on the back of her left hand and Prism's cold forehead pressed to it. No ceremony, no choice. She footnoted it: "*Subject (self) did not consent. Subject (self) is not complaining.*"

The first cost, before the cut, was cold: every Surge turned her left hand white and numb to the elbow for hours and left no mark. The cost she has now was made by Vasch's hand, not Prism's: a half-cut bond growing back through a cracked mark, in crystal, through skin, every time she asks for light. It hurts the way a broken bone hurts when you lean on it. She leans on it anyway.

## 5. Three defining memories
1. **The colour of the light (age 11).** The kitchen on Hallam Stair, fog, Wick glowing on the rail. Her mother: "What colour is it tonight?" Ione: "Grey. Sort of grey." Her mother, smiling at nothing: "That's not it." Ione lay awake trying to find the word. There wasn't one yet. (Plain, warm, sad; no jokes on the page.)
2. **The hexagon (age 22).** Waking at her desk, Prism's forehead cold against her wrist, the mark glowing whole, and her first feeling not fear but relief: something had chosen her without asking what she was for.
3. **Five (age 23).** The white room. Pike's hands on her shoulders. Vasch counting, gentle as a lullaby. At five, the case behind her exploding into rainbows. His hand sliding off the mark. His face, for one panel, not kind. Then kind again, which was worse.

## 6. Vasch, her mentor, and the day he stopped at five
What he was to her: the first adult who took her seriously, which at 19 is indistinguishable from love. He never raised his voice. He footnoted his own papers; she caught the habit. He fed Prism from his bare metal fingers. After the Hekka night he sat with her on the Ward's roof and said, "You wrote the truth down. That is all a procedure is. The cruelty was mine for needing it." She believed every word, and most of it was true.

The day, told plainly. Age 23. Vasch had promised the Empire a strip with no screaming: a numbed mark, a subject who walks out whole, bondless, grateful. He needed a volunteer who understood the risk; she was the only person alive who had read every half-strip file. She volunteered because she thought she was proving him right, and because part of her wanted to know what her mother had refused.

The room was white. Doctor Pike held her shoulders. Prism, cold-sedated, was in a glass case behind her, because Vasch would not cut a bond with the partner loose. He put the cutter to the hexagon and counted softly, the way you count a child to sleep. At five, Prism opened its wings inside the case, threw every lamp in the room into a thousand colours, and the case came apart. Vasch's hand slid off the mark. He reached to re-seat the cutter and the mark was black, nothing to hold. By the time it came back, dim and cracked, the Ward's own files said what resuming would do: a half-cut mark re-cut kills the subject. He said "Ione," not "Doctor," for the only time. He said, "I'm sorry." Then he said, "Well. We'll learn something," and she stayed two more years because that sentence sounded like hope and she was 23.

He sincerely believes he owes her a finished count; his letter in `shared/stories.js` says so. In Issue 36 he offers to complete it, painlessly. She says no, and learns as she says it that she is her mother's daughter.

## 7. Wants, fears, and the inside of her head
**Wants:** to prove a half-cut bond can grow back whole, because then nothing she wrote is final. To be forgiven without having to ask. To describe one colour correctly to her mother. To make Silas lose an argument.

**Fears:** that Vasch is right and the kind thing is a knife. Sleep, because she counts in her dreams and wakes at five. That Prism stays out of pity. Being thanked.

All of it hides under footnotes. The guilt isn't a mood; it's the floor she stands on.

## 8. Prism
- **Species:** glass mantis-moth, a rare wild species from the crystal caves of the Ward island, called Tern Hatch before the Empire put a greenhouse over it. Look per the cast file and locked sheet: large-dog size, ice-blue glass body, four walking legs, blade forearms, feathery antennae, clear rainbow-edged wings folded flat, glowing ice-blue faceted eyes. Prism is the last of the lab's colony.
- **Where found:** the warm vents under the Ward greenhouse floor, when Ione was 21. Its old nest, a cup of fused glass, is still there in Issue 35.
- **Temperament:** patient, still, superior. It watches a room the way Ione does and reaches the same conclusions a beat sooner. Likes Bram's galley lamp, Sela's spyglass, and nobody's jokes.
- **Three habits:** (1) it sits in any light it can reach and scatters rainbows across the ceiling when content, which is how the crew learns to read Ione's mood; (2) every night, unasked, it files the crystal on her wrist and neck smooth with the flat of its blades, the only thing that eases the pain; (3) when a stranger comes aboard it folds flat against the nearest glass or white surface and is hard to see until it wants to be.
- **How it fights:** it does not charge. It takes a position with light behind it and cuts once, fast, when something comes near. For a Surge it opens its wings and Ione's light goes through them: a beam she bends off any crystal, including her own. Shields grow from her shards; Prism only holds the angle. Short bursts, by nature and by cost.
- **How it shows affection:** forehead to her hexagon, cool and still, as on the night the mark appeared. A low chime through its wings that makes her crystals ring, which she calls "diagnostically interesting" and which puts her to sleep. It has not sat on Vasch's arm since the count.

## 9. How she talks (ten lines in voice)
Deadpan. Clinical words as innuendo; she will not tell you whether she knows. Starred footnote captions. Guilt under every joke.
1. "I can be very thorough.* (*Peer-reviewed.)"
2. "A bond is a sustained mutual exchange between two bodies, frequently louder than expected. No, Silas, I will not read it again slowly."
3. "You may shoot me afterward. First, I need you to look at a map."
4. "That is not a scar. A scar is the body finishing a sentence. This is the body refusing to."
5. "Three to one she betrays us? I'll take that. I know the subject."
6. "Hold still. I'm going to touch you somewhere you won't enjoy.* (*Your ulnar nerve.)"
7. "I wrote the words 'do not stop at five.' I had very good handwriting."
8. "Prism likes you. It's in your lamp. It never sat in Vasch's. Draw your own conclusions; I've drawn mine."
9. "I don't sleep because I count. Everybody I've ever met counts to ten. I get to five."
10. "He was kind. Write that down. It's the thing nobody ever believes, and it's the whole problem."

- **Sela:** respect bordering on reverence: Sela pays a worse cost and writes it down without a single footnote.
- **Juno:** Juno bet against her and Ione bet with her; neither has mentioned it; both count it as the start of a friendship.

- **Otti:** flirts with her out of fairness; Ione answers with anatomy; Otti laughs hardest, until Issue 41.

- **Doctor Pike:** he held her shoulders; her footnotes call him "the surgeon."

## 13. Fighting style
She does not fight hand to hand. She fights the room: where the light is, what will bounce it, where her own shards are. Prism holds the beam; she bends it off crystal, glass, water, Bram's iron, Vasch's arm, in short bursts, counting each under her breath and never past four. Shields grow from her own shards and she cannot hold one long (Issue 38). Two belt vials hold ground glass to throw into the air and catch a beam on. Tell: glasses off before a big Surge, because the light fogs them. Every Surge is a step toward the jaw, taken deliberately, like a surgeon choosing where to cut.

## 14. Never
- Never counts to five aloud, in a fight, a card game or a joke.
- Never lies about data. She will withhold a number; she will not change one.
- Never says Pike's name.
- Never calls Vasch a monster, and corrects anyone who does: "He is a doctor. That's the problem."
- Never asks to be forgiven.
- Never flirts near anyone hurt, frightened or captive, or near Prism's nest.
- Never takes the free strip. Not in Issue 36, not in Part Two, not ever.
- Never says "I'm sorry" to the crew; she says "noted" and fixes it.
- Never leaves a footnote unfinished. If a line trails off (Issue 45), something is very wrong.
