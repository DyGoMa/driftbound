# Backstory dossier: Bram Torvald, Grist, the Patience, and Old Tam Reed

For every medium. Looks and ages come from `C:\Users\Dylan\Documents\driftbound-villains\docs\CAST.md` and win over anything here; beats come from `docs/manga/SERIES_BIBLE.md` and this file never moves one. **[new]** marks invention. Rules for the past: every drawn flashback of Bram shows a grown man (22+); his childhood is spoken about, never drawn. Flashback pages are plain and sad or warm, with zero flirting, innuendo or fan service on them or the facing page. Grist is never in a flirting scene.

## 1. Bram Torvald

**Now:** 31, man, 198 cm, huge and broad. Light-brown skin, kind brown eyes, thick black beard, shaved head. Flowing bands-and-points tattoos across both shoulders, the left partly swallowed by iron. Shirtless under heavy work-dungarees, left strap down, tool belt, thick boots, cook's towel over the right shoulder. Bond mark: a square anvil over his heart. Iron: left shoulder and left forearm (base state). Colour: iron grey and rust orange.

### Birthplace and family
- Born aboard the **Patience** **[new]**, his family's wide-bellied iron-hauling barge, off the foundry island of **Hobnail** **[new]**, where the Torvalds bought scrap and sold timber. He has no island; "home" has always meant a galley.
- **Anders Torvald** **[new]**, father, hauler-captain, a quiet mountain who said one sentence a day and meant it. Bram's voice is his.
- **Mette Torvald** **[new]**, mother, the Patience's cook. Taught him everything at the stove, starting with "low heat." Died of lung fever when Bram was 19 **[new]**. Spoken of only, never drawn.
- The shoulder tattoos are **hauler marks** **[new]**: a band for every year aboard, a point for every crewmate. The left shoulder was his family's side. The iron is covering them one point at a time, and he knows it.

### The years before we meet him (ages at each event)
- **0 to 19:** the Patience's galley and timber hold. Cooking from Mette, timber and iron from Anders. Dialogue only; never a drawn panel of Bram under 22.
- **20:** meets **Hanne Aske** **[new]** (21), a rigger who laughs with her whole body and steals from the pot while it's cooking. She signs on.
- **21:** marries Hanne (22) on deck. Wedding supper: pan bread with onions, his mother's recipe **[new]**. It becomes the dish he can't make.
- **22:** the **Lowmoor pinning** **[new]**. The Empire drives its first-ever anchor pylon at the harbour island of Lowmoor in a running current and gets it wrong: the island stops, the sea doesn't, and a wall of water comes over the harbour. The Patience is driven onto the harbour wall and broken with Anders (56) and Hanne (23) aboard. Bram is ashore buying salt and onions. The Empire calls it a storm. Pylons have been driven at slack water ever since, which is why nobody else remembers a pinning killing anyone **[new]**. (Ordinary anchor pylons are the Empire's cover story in the bible; the ten Great Pylons come later.)
- **22:** a breaker's tug tows the Patience's bones to **Keelbreak** for stripping. Bram follows his ship's wreck to the yard and drinks in it **[new]**. Old Tam Reed (58) hands him a hammer.
- **23:** finds a half-grown iron rhino-beetle with a crushed left hind leg in the yard's slag heap **[new]**. Yard rule: wild beasts are put down. He refuses, splints the leg with a ship's rib and iron bands, feeds it scrap and cabbage for a season. Tam names it: "it eats my scrap like grist."
- **24:** the bond. A crane chain parts; Bram gets under the falling hull section and for the first time his skin goes iron and holds. The anvil appears over his heart. His left forearm stays grey **[new]**.
- **26:** Sela Vey (23) brings the battered Gullwake in for a refit. Bram rebuilds her galley and lays the Patience's keel plank as the galley table **[new]**. When the ship is ready he is already aboard. Tam: "Go. You're eating my profit." Five years as Sela's crew **[new]**; he never met Corin Vale and knows no more about the torn page than anyone.
- **29:** holds the Gullwake's sprung mast step closed through a two-day storm; the iron climbs to his left shoulder and stays **[new]**. That is the sheets' base state.
- **31:** Issue 1.

### How the bond happened and what it first cost
Not a choice, a reflex: a beetle he'd nursed for a year was about to be crushed, and his body did what it does for everyone he feeds. Price: his left forearm never came back to skin, and he has known from day one that the iron only goes one way. He has never once Surged for himself.

### Three defining memories
1. **The harbour wall at Lowmoor (22).** He walks up the quay with a sack of onions and a towel on his shoulder and the harbour is gone. He sets the towel on the wall, very neatly, and goes into the water. He does not find them. That is where "towel's down" began: the towel is the cook; when it comes off, the cook is set aside **[new]**. (Drawable; silent; no jokes on the spread.)
2. **The hammer (22).** Dawn in Tam's yard, Bram asleep against the Patience's broken keel, a bottle beside him. Tam kicks the bottle into the sea and puts a caulking mallet in his hand. He kept working so he'd be too tired to drink.
3. **Grist under the chain (24).** The falling hull, the beetle's one good roar, the grey climbing his arm, the moment he realises he isn't being crushed. He looked at his iron hand and laughed, then cried, which is the order he does everything in.

### What he wants and what he fears
- **Wants:** nobody hungry, nobody going over the rail hungry, the whole crew at one table at once, which almost never happens. To see them old.
- **Fears:** not dying. His hands going first; he flexes every finger each morning before anyone's up **[new]**. The kitchen going quiet (Issue 44 makes it literal). And the one he tells nobody until Part Two: that the iron reaches his face and he stops being able to cry **[new]**.

### How he talks
Few words. Food and ship metaphors, completely sincere. Misty-eyed at kindness, flat-calm in danger. "Captain" means three different things depending on the day. Ten lines in voice:
1. "Sit. Eat. Then shout."
2. "Hull's like bread. The crack's never where it started."
3. "Grist. Off the hatch." (beat) "...Everyone use the other hatch."
4. "Juno. Cards go in your pocket or in my stove."
5. "Silas. It's stew. It isn't doing anything to you."
6. "Captain, you wrote down you liked it. You wrote it twice."
7. "Doctor, you can look at the arm after the bowl's empty."
8. "Low heat. Everything worth it is low heat."
9. "Tam'd have hated this. That's how you know it's right."
10. "Towel's down."

### Relationships (one line each)
- **Kaito:** the stray he's decided to fatten up; makes him eat greens and lets him think the galley rules are about him.
- **Sela:** he keeps her logbook dry and her stew on, and has served her eel pie enough times to know exactly what she's lost.
- **Juno:** she calls him sweetheart and prices his shirtlessness; he confiscates her cards; he cooled her fever with wet towels.
- **Silas:** flirts at Bram on purpose because Bram blushes into the beard and answers with food; a friendship neither names.
- **Ione:** signed on with a bowl of soup (Issue 38); the only one he lets measure the iron, because she never says sorry while doing it.
- **Grist:** see section 2. The only one he calls "lad."

### Secrets the reader learns later (which issue)
- **Issue 29:** the Lowmoor story, spoken over a stove; Tam's hammer; Grist's leg; that the galley table is the Patience's keel **[new]** (so "nobody fights in my galley" has been about his family's ship since Issue 2); that he marks the iron's climb on the galley doorframe like a child's height chart **[new]**; that his knee has locked.
- **Issue 32:** the towel goes down for the second time in his life. From here he carries Tam's caulking mallet on his belt **[new]**.
- **Issue 36:** Vasch's chart is a projection: at the current rate the iron reaches the anvil over his heart at about 36 **[new, provisional]**. Bram says nothing; Vasch reads it as a maybe. It isn't.
- **Issue 40:** he cooks pan bread with onions at Tobias's cookout and says Hanne's name aloud for the first time on the page **[new]**.
- **Issue 45:** the soup kitchen's board reads TAM'S, though Dunmarrow calls it Bram's kitchen **[new]**. The towel is the only piece of the Gullwake he has left.

### Wardrobe variations (inside the fan service line)
- **Base:** as the sheet. Towel on the right shoulder; left bib corner folded so the anvil shows.
- **Cinder Palace (Issue 7):** a waiter's vest two sizes too small, bow tie, no shirt because none fit; the buttons don't survive.
- **Regatta beach (Issue 20):** a tiny striped bathing suit and the towel. He is unbothered; everyone else is not.
- **Dry dock (Issue 28):** dungarees rolled to the knee, tar to the elbows, Tam's welding goggles pushed up.
- **Funeral at sea (Issue 32):** a plain shirt buttoned to the throat, too small. The one time he covers the mark. No towel.
- **Part Two (33):** half-iron, left side grey to the jaw; a heavy wool work coat with the left sleeve cut off because iron won't fit a sleeve **[new]**; the towel; a stick that is the shaft of Tam's caulking mallet **[new]**.

### Fighting style
He holds; he doesn't strike first. Three moves for the artists **[new names]**: **the Brace** (his body in the gap: a torn deck, a doorway, a collapsing hull), **the Serve** (he hands a man a roll, a pot, a bucket, and shoves him overboard while his hands are full), and **the Towel**: towel off, no blocking, hits land on iron, he takes hold of whatever threatened the crew and does not let go. Weapons: the big pot, a beam, and from Issue 32 Tam's mallet. Grist does the charging. After Issue 31 he fights slower each time; in Part Two he refuses to fight until Issue 45.

### The never list
- Never eats before the crew has. Never lets a plate go back full.
- Never hits someone already down, or anyone holding food.
- Never says "it's fine."
- Never flirts first. He is flirted *at*, enjoys it, goes red into the beard, and answers with a second helping.
- Never calls Grist "it." Never swears at a Pal.
- Never says Hanne's name before Issue 40.
- Never puts the towel down for a threat to himself alone.
- Never walks away from a lit stove, even in Issue 30.

## 2. Grist

- **Species:** iron rhino-beetle beast, element Iron. Truck-sized, domed shell of dark iron plates with rivet bumps and rust-orange scuffs, one huge anvil-shaped horn, glowing rust-orange eyes, six thick legs, no tail.
- **Where found:** Keelbreak's slag heap, half-grown, left hind leg crushed under a dropped hull section he'd crawled under to eat the rust off **[new]**. Bram was 23. Wild iron beetles come to yards for scrap and are usually killed **[new]**.
- **Temperament:** slow, stubborn, immovable, fond of exactly one person and tolerant of the rest. Not grumpy like Skarn; Grist simply does not see why he should move.
- **Three habits:** (1) sleeps wherever he is least wanted (hatches, rails, the galley door), snore bubble at the horn; (2) eats scrap iron and old nails, which Bram calls "supper" and serves in a bucket, but abandons anything for a cabbage; (3) the healed left hind leg has a faint hitch when he walks **[new]**, motion only, never visible standing on the sheet.
- **How he fights:** charges when he finally decides to, and the charge ends arguments; otherwise a wall, a prop (horn under a torn deck), and a thing troopers mistake for furniture. Never once fast.
- **How he shows affection:** leans his full weight on you, slowly, until you have to move; sleeps against the warm stove wall; puts his horn under Bram's hand when the stove goes quiet. Lets Hush alone sleep on his shell; Skarn's fin sparks off the iron and Grist never notices **[new]**.

## 3. The lost: the Patience, Hanne, Anders

Dead before the story; flashback-only, plain and sad; never on a page or facing page with fan service.

- **The Patience** **[new]:** a broad, slow iron-hauling barge with a galley bigger than its wheelhouse. Her keel plank is the Gullwake's galley table until Issue 42.
- **Anders Torvald (died at 56)** **[new]:** a bigger, quieter Bram. His one drawable line, Issue 29: "Cook's the one who decides when we're done."
- **Hanne Aske-Torvald (died at 23)** **[new]:** rigger, loud laugh, stole from the pot. Her two lines in Bram's telling: "You're rushing it." and "Save me the crust." Bram says the first to Kaito in Issue 11 without knowing where it came from; the reader learns in Issue 29.

## 4. Tamsin "Old Tam" Reed

**Now:** 67, woman. Small, leather-skinned, foul-mouthed, master shipwright of Keelbreak. Dies in Issue 32.

### Birthplace and family
- Born on Keelbreak **[new]**, the shipyard island, to a yard family; has never lived anywhere a hammer wasn't being swung.
- Widowed at 30 **[new]**: her husband **Joss Reed** **[new]**, a caulker, fell from a scaffold. No children. The yard is her family, exactly: it takes in the broke. Bram is the one who stayed.
- Never bonded **[new]**. "I've got a yard. I don't need a dog." Her face when she sees a stripped partner says what she doesn't.

### The years before we meet her (ages at each event)
- **17 to 30:** her father's yard; learns to build twice rather than pretty. Spoken of, not drawn.
- **30:** Joss dies. She takes the yard over the same month.
- **37 (thirty years ago):** builds the racing hull that wins **Otti Ferro** (then 22) her first Regatta **[new]**. Drinking rivals ever since; Otti boarding the Gullwake in Issue 27 puts them in the same yard in Issue 28.
- **58:** a huge drunk man asleep against a wreck in her yard. She gives him a hammer. Bram is 22.
- **59 to 62:** teaches him. Puts too much black pepper in everything **[new]**, which is where eel pie "the way Bram does it" comes from (Issue 11).
- **62:** tells Bram to get on Sela's ship and go.
- **64 to 67:** runs the yard alone; refuses Empire refit contracts, which is why the yard is broke and the Sever come knocking in Issue 29 **[new]**.

### How the bond with Bram happened and what it first cost
Not kindness: she needed a big back and had a rule about drunks in the yard. It cost her the bottle (she drank too, and stopped so he'd have no company in it **[new]**), and later her best worker, because she sent him away on purpose.

### Three defining memories
1. **The scaffold (30).** Joss on the stones below. She finished his seam before she went down to him. "Build twice" started there.
2. **The hammer (58).** Kicking Bram's bottle into the sea at dawn and handing him a mallet. She tells it as a joke; it wasn't.
3. **The galley table (62).** Watching Bram lay his dead family's keel plank into a stranger's ship and knowing he'd be all right. She went inside so he wouldn't see her face.

### What she wants and what she fears
- **Wants:** the yard to outlive her; Bram off the ladders; one more good argument.
- **Fears:** her hands going before her mouth. And the iron: she sees it first (Issue 28), says nothing, moves him to light work. He doesn't go.

### How she talks
Short, filthy, exact. Everyone gets a nickname: Kaito is "the shouting one," Silas "that shirt," Sela "Captain" with a question mark in it. Ten lines in voice:
1. "You can drink in my yard if you work in my yard. Hammer's there. Bottle's mine now."
2. "Who did this to your keel? You? Then who taught you? Shit. That's on me."
3. "Nine years I've been fixing what you break, you great wet sack."
4. "Don't 'Tam' me in that voice. I'm sixty-seven, not dead. Keep up."
5. (to Silas) "Button that or I'll rivet it."
6. "Pass me the shouting one. He's about the right length for a brace."
7. (to Sela) "You've had my boy five years and he's heavier than you took him. What are you feeding him?" / "He feeds us." / "Figures."
8. (to Grist, Issue 28) "You. Keep him off the ladders."
9. "Soup's not a plan, Bram. Soup's a very good soup."
10. "I don't build pretty. I build twice."

### Relationships (one line each)
- **Bram:** the son she won't call one; curses the state of his ship because the alternative is saying it.
- **Sela:** two captains of a kind; Tam trusts her with Bram and says so sideways.
- **Kaito:** "the shouting one"; rated by how long he can hold a brace, and he improves to spite her.
- **Juno:** Tam wins one hand off her and never plays again, so she's ahead for life.
- **Silas:** he flirts; she threatens his wardrobe with yard tools; he adores her by Issue 29.
- **Otti:** thirty years of out-drinking each other; the two women who die holding gaps.
- **Grist:** named him; the only beast she ever let stay in the yard.
- **Hekka:** Tam gets between the bear and Bram (Issue 31); Hekka sees what follows and calls the retreat herself (Issue 32).

### Secrets the reader learns later (which issue)
- **Issue 28:** she sees the iron first and says nothing; the reader sees her see it.
- **Issue 29:** the pepper; the hammer; that she sent him away on purpose; that refusing Empire contracts is why the yard is poor and the Sever come **[new]**.
- **Issue 32:** one rude word. Suggested, for the Issue 32 writer to take or leave: "Bastards." **[new, suggestion only]**

### Wardrobe variations
- **Base [new]:** sleeveless leather yard apron over a grey undershirt, canvas trousers rolled over bare ankles, wooden clogs, leather cap with welding goggles up, mallet on a hip loop, pencil behind the ear; forearms all scar.
- **Cook-off (Issue 29):** the same with a stolen cook's towel worn wrong, to needle Bram.
- **Issue 31:** apron off, undershirt, mallet in both hands.
- Never in a fan service moment; she comments on other people's.

### Fighting style
None. A 67-year-old with a two-handed caulking mallet and the yard's knowledge of where a thing is weak. In Issue 31 she goes for the bear's shoulder plate, the one Bram couldn't reach, and lands one hit.

### The never list
- Never says "I love you," "I'm proud of you," or "son." Says "eat," "sit," and "off the ladder."
- Never flirts, with Silas or anyone; she abuses.
- Never asks the Empire for anything.
- Never puts down a tool she hasn't cleaned.
- Never gives a last speech; Issue 32 keeps this.
