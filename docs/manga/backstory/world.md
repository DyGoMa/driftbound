# The world of Driftbound: backstory dossier (v1, 2026-11-06)

For every medium: website, manga, anime, games. Sources: `docs/WORLD_BIBLE.md`, `docs/manga/SERIES_BIBLE.md`, the cast file in `driftbound-villains`, `shared/stories.js`, `docs/FRIDAY_BATCH.md` and the sibling dossiers in this folder. Where they are silent this file invents; every invention is marked **[new]**. The bible and the cast win on any clash. Hard rules apply in full: everyone 21 or older; no fan service near a Pal, a childhood memory or anyone hurt or frightened; no sexual threat ever; original only. Under everything: **costs stay paid.**

## 1. The Drift

A sea with no fixed land. Islands ride the water on long rock roots, like teeth in a jaw **[new]**: some low as sandbars (Ninefathom), some high-shouldered cliffs the water barely wets underneath (Pinhold). They move a few ship-lengths a day in a quiet month, a harbour's width a night in a bad one, never where the chart said; a crew buys "last month" every time (`stories.js`). Islands whose roots let go sink and hang in the deep as **drowned islands** **[new name]** (Issue 13). Where two roots cross, the water turns over in a **down-draw** **[new]**, the whirlpool that nearly took the old Gullwake eight years ago.

Three kinds of people live on it: **free islanders**, who keep ledgers of where their island has been; **crews**, who sail by feel and by whoever is bonded aboard; and the **Meridian Empire**, thirty-odd pinned or garrisoned islands in two rings round the capital, selling the one thing the Drift cannot give, a map that stays right. Nobody says aloud what a pinned island costs.

**Drawing it:** slate sea at dusk, islands lit on top and dark on their faces (the Pinhold picture), chains wherever the Empire has been, three wavy lines wherever the Undersong is heard.

## 2. The Sleeper, the Undersong and the Cantors

**The truth (bible, section 3; learned slowly).** The Drift is not geology. One sleeping being the size of the world, **the Sleeper**, lies under the sea and carries the islands. The first islanders bonded to it: the **First Bond**. Every human-monster bond since is a thread of it, which is why every cost is paid to the same account. Its slow pulse moves the islands and is heard as a very low hum, **the Undersong**: always three small wavy lines, never words.

**Who hears it.** Partners, always; they go still, or howl, when it rises (Issue 5). Bonded humans, faintly, in the mark **[new]**. Deep water carries it best (Issue 13). It is loudest for one night wherever a Great Pylon bites **[new]**: a cutter on a mark makes a person scream; a pylon on the Sleeper's mark makes its song audible. That is Issue 1's "first hum." The **worldquake** after each count is the Sleeper flinching **[new wording]**.

**The Cantors.** The drift-singers of Cantor Isle steered by the Undersong before there were charts, singing an island's course from the **bell-house** (villains dossier): the keel-voice holding the low line, the youngest voice, the **pup**, learning the tune without yet holding it (Sela dossier). They give no names outside the song: "names are what we pay" (Kell). Their legend, as Kell tells it in Issue 34, a story not a lecture **[new text]**: *the first people drifted on a dead sea with no islands; something rose under them and carried them; the first singer sang so it would not dive again, and it has slept to that song since; her price was her name, and no Cantor has kept one.* The isle emptied over a generation as charts replaced singers (Sela dossier); the Sever took its last partners six years ago (allies dossier). Mother Kell (71) is the last elder. Corin Vale, now Silt, is the last Cantor who hears the song clearly, and still does, which is the cruelty of Issue 41.

**The map that is a song.** Kaito's "map that stays right" exists only as a score: the islands keep time, and a chart marking each current's beat (the small tick on Oren's and Kaito's charts) can be read forward. By Issue 48 his chart is half map, half stave. Oren has tried for fifteen years and cannot hear it (allies dossier).

## 3. Bonding as a science

What the labs know. What the sea knows is in section 2; nobody has put the two in one book, which is the series.

- **The bond takes; it is not made.** Every bond on record began with a person sitting down beside a hurt or trapped creature and staying (Kaito in the rain, Sela in the weed, Juno at the firebox, Silas at the bars, Bram in the slag, Ione at a desk, Hekka in the sleet). No ritual, no choice **[new rule, drawn from every dossier]**.
- **The mark.** A simple shape in the partner's colour, brighter when power is borrowed (CAST). All marks flicker at once at a worldquake **[new]**; Ione's year on the run proved it (Ione dossier).
- **The Surge.** Borrowing the partner's element. Small Surges are safe; big or long ones cost one kind of thing per person: scars, memory, heat, breath, iron, crystal. The labs count six elements (Storm, Tide, Fire, Wind, Iron, Crystal); the sea has more (Brine, Frost, Echo in the villains dossier; Sand, Salt, Light among the allies) **[new reconciliation]**.
- **The Short Surge.** Otti's discovery (Issue 22): small bursts on the beat cost almost nothing. The labs never asked a racer. The beat is the Undersong; Otti never knows it, and the reader learns it in Issue 35.
- **Why the cost.** Vasch calls a bond "a wound with a name" (`stories.js`). The Cantors say it kinder: everything carried is paid for, and the Sleeper carries everyone **[new framing]**.
- **Costs stay paid.** No cost has ever reversed. A scar fades a little; a memory never returns; iron never softens; a stripped mark goes to a dull ring (Hekka). The one exception is the wrong-shaped regrowth on Ione's wrist, and it hurts worse than the damage (Issue 47).

## 4. Hollows, the count, the half-cut

**Standing Order 12** is canon in full (`stories.js`); Ione wrote its field text at 22 from the Glass Ward's records (Ione dossier).

**A strip.** At ten the thread breaks. The human's mark goes dark and scars to a dull ring (CAST, Hekka). The monster's eyes go blank and a white **core** is left where the bond sat, which the Sever's Doctors plate and cable into a **Hollow** (CAST). Hollows answer the Director's voice (villains dossier) and the Ostlers' chains. A core still rings faintly toward living bonds **[new]**: that is how Silt's hound tracks the bonded (Issue 4; Ione's footnote, Issue 33) and why Hollows go for partners first.

**The half-cut.** Stop at five and the thread frays. The mark is cracked and dark for an hour, then comes back wrong (Ione dossier). Re-cutting kills the subject. Left alone it regrows in the wrong material: crystal on Ione; on Skarn, one blank eye and one yellow (Issue 48). The Glass Ward's half-strip files are the people this happened to by accident; they walk its corridors in Issue 35. "It makes a problem" is Part Two in five words.

**Who gets stripped.** By law, every pair on a pinned island (Issue 3). By purchase, anyone whose debt a Ledger buys (Issue 6). By hunt, anyone Silt finds. By choice, the Sever's officers, to prove loyalty. In Part Two, by consent form at the mercy clinics (Issue 45).

## 5. Pylons and Anchor Days

**Ordinary pylons.** Steel anchors driven through an island's root into the seabed, four or more to an island, chained to its collar (the Pinhold picture; Storm Run's "Pylon Four"). First used nine years ago (bible, Issue 29); since then driven only at slack water. Meridian was never pinned: the Empire says "anchored for forty years"; old Admiralty charts show it was simply the slowest island in the Drift **[new reconciliation]**. A pinned island stops. In a week its charts are right, which the town celebrates. In a season the birds go, because the updrafts die **[new]**. In a decade the wells are dead, because the water table no longer breathes with the pulse (Kaito dossier), and the soil goes grey.

**Great Pylons.** Ten, one per chosen island, each planted inside a ring of ordinary pylons so it looks like a bigger anchor **[new]**. Together, one bond-cutter the size of the sea. Plotted, the sites draw a line through a circle (Issue 35); Meridian is the circle's centre, hence "Six is at Meridian" (Issue 48) **[new placement]**. Sites: One, Pinhold (Issue 5). Two, Gaptooth Reach (Issue 10). Three, Saltlight (Issue 27). Four and Five, Issue 39, unnamed in the bible; proposed, PROVISIONAL: **Harrow Bank** and **the Tithe Stair** **[new]**. Six was Anvil Rock (wrecked, Issue 42), now Meridian. Seven to Ten: a later call.

**Anchor Day.** Began twenty years ago as Meridian's day of mourning for the four hundred Admiral Hale lost when two islands ground together in fog (villains dossier). Vasch borrowed the name **[new]**: each Great Pylon's planting is proclaimed "an Anchor Day" on that island, a holiday with bunting on the chains, free bread, a Deacon's blessing read from the collar, and the core driven at noon **[new]**. The hum comes that night, the worldquake within days, and Vasch says the number. Islanders who have lived through one call the bunting "ribbons on the knife" **[new]**.

## 6. The islands named so far

**Pinhold** (Issues 1 to 5; Storm Run). A tilted cliff slab on the Empire's trade edge, no beach, a harbour cut into the low south side, the Strikes on the north point where storm wolves roost (Kaito dossier). Charts on every wall, a Chart-house, a customs post, Tull Lane, six generations of well-keepers. Storm Run's stages are its geography: the Pin Road, Chain Yard, Searchlight Row, Pylon Four, the Long Drop. Great Pylon One.

**Ashmarket** (Issues 6 to 10; Hot Hand). A gamblers' city on a volcanic crag: lantern halls above, the Cinder Palace and its High Table on top, **Sootside** and the furnaces of **Bellows Row** below, piping heat up through the market floors (Juno dossier). **Ember Night** relights every lantern from one flame. Nim keeps the one honest book. Garrisoned, not pinned: the Empire likes its taxes moving **[new]**.

**Gaptooth Reach** (Issues 11 to 14; Tide Gap). Two island chains grinding past each other, the only way east, rock teeth opening and closing to the pulse. More drowned islands lie under it than anywhere, and the Undersong carries up through them (Issue 13). Great Pylon Two went into the flat-topped tooth pilots call **the Molar** **[new]**; the Gap has closed faster since.

**Lanternreach** (Issues 15 to 19; Silent Cut). A night port where nobody uses names (Maro's rule) and everyone owes Maro. Lamps on every post; the **Lantern Baths**, where deals are made because nobody can hide a wire in a towel; **Tallow Dock**, where the Sever's cutter-ship loads its lots (Silas dossier). South lie the **Quiet Shoals** and **Brittlemere**. Flooded in Part Two; Silas's silent theatre is in the upper storeys (Issue 46).

**Saltlight** (Issues 20 to 27). A neutral coral atoll: the Regatta every seven years, the buoy-riggers (the Asters), and the **Longwake Chart**, kept in the atoll's truce-house **[new]** until it burns in Issue 27. Great Pylon Three was drilled under the reef during seven years of truce.

**Keelbreak** (Issues 28 to 32; Hold the Deck). The shipyard island: dry docks, the breaker's slag heap where iron beetles come for scrap, the memorial post (allies dossier), Old Tam's yard, broke because she refuses Empire contracts (Bram dossier). Not a pylon site; the Sever come for the ship.

**Cantor Isle** (Issue 34). The singers' ghost village: the bell-house, empty song-steps, one hut with a lamp. Wild monsters lie down when Kell hums; so does Skarn.

**The Glass Ward** (Issues 33 to 38; Split Light). Once charted as **Tern Hatch** (Ione dossier), roofed by the Empire with a greenhouse-lab, now grown through with crystal: warm vents under the floor, the crystal caves, Prism's nest, Pike's notes, the half-strip files, Oren's survey. The Director's first failures, walking.

**Anvil Rock** (Issues 39 to 42). An iron-black sea-stack where the Empire forges pylon segments, a worker town bolted to its ledges, a harbour of barges **[new]**. Yevka Sorn sees a Hollow made from one of its workers (Issue 40). The battle wrecks pylon, ship and crew, and the stopped count breaks the sea.

**Dunmarrow** (Issue 45). A farming island that struck its neighbour in the Problem's first winter and lost its western half **[new]**; the east is terraces of refugees. Bram's kitchen, TAM'S on the board. The first mercy clinic opened on its quay **[new]**.

**Meridian** (Issues 46 to 48 on). The capital, slowest island in the Drift. The empty Anchor Seat, Regent Hale, the Admiralty, the **Still Room** where Vasch counts (villains dossier), the records barge where Oren is chained to a chart table (Issue 47). The centre of the circle.

## 7. The Gullwake, deck by deck

A short, broad salvage barque, named by Captain Hesper Lund for the gulls that follow a salvage ship's wake **[new]**. Three masts; the aftermost, cut to a stump after a storm, is **the Post**: the betting pool is chalked on it and Bram's Galley Rules are nailed to it a board at a time **[new]**. Below decks is as Bram rebuilt it five years ago (Bram dossier).

- **Aloft.** The main crosstrees are Skarn's; his fin crackles an hour before weather. Hush takes the fore-yard at dusk. Ashka argues with Skarn from the Post nightly (the Issue 40 cutaway).
- **Bow.** The **towing bitt** for Vesh's jaw-harness line, grooved by eight years of her pull **[new]**. She surfaces here to be scratched.
- **Foredeck.** The sail-screen and deck tub (Issue 2), rigged from a spare staysail, the bath-night roster pinned to its frame **[new]**. The fore hatch, which Grist sleeps on, so everyone uses the other hatch.
- **Main deck.** The main hatch, the stern salvage crane that is the ship's living, the cutlass rack by the wheel, and under the quarterdeck awning Lund's old chart table, now Kaito's: blank in Issue 5, the real chart by Issue 35 **[new placement]**.
- **The galley** (midships, below). Hold the Deck's three lanes are its three approaches: the main hatch ladder, the fore companionway through the berths, the stern passage from the captain's cabin **[new]**. The table is the Patience's keel plank; the doorframe carries Bram's iron marks; the stove hood is Ashka's bed; Prism sits in the lamp; Vesh waits under the porthole.
- **Berths** (forward). Six bunks; the best, against the warm stove wall, is Juno's from Issue 10. Under Silas's is the button pile. Ione sleeps in the sail locker beyond, made a lab with one lamp **[new]**.
- **Captain's cabin** (aft). The trunk of logbooks, Marit's letters, fourteen shed scales, Lund's pea-coat, the oilskin for the Dive (Sela dossier).
- **The hold.** Salvage, rope, and Hush at dawn watching every door.
- **The end.** Torn apart at Anvil Rock (Issue 42); the keel-plank table goes down with her. The towel and the trunk come out of the sea. Part Two: a stolen hull (Issue 48) and Juno's **Second Chance**.

## 8. The twelve Pals

Small, comic, original collectibles (FRIDAY_BATCH), two a game, in the Monster Book. Never in a flirting scene, bath gag or innuendo; no fan service shares their panel. No bonds, marks or costs **[new]**; they attach to a ship the way cats do. Looks are FRIDAY_BATCH's; the rest is **[new]**. Format: kind and where found; temperament; three habits; what it does in a fight; affection.

- **Fizzlet** (Storm Run). Fluffy storm cloud on stubby legs; the Strikes. Anxious, comes trotting to anyone hurt. Rains on itself when embarrassed; sparks at Skarn's growl; sleeps in the crosstrees' lee. Fogs a searchlight. Affection: a damp hug round the ankle.
- **Boltnip** (Storm Run). Tiny gecko with a sparking kite-tail; the Pinhold updrafts. Vain and fast. Rides storm-wolf fins; licks brass; hides in Kaito's goggles. A static nip. Affection: a tail-tickle to the ear.
- **Bobbin** (Tide Gap). Jellyfish shaped like a striped buoy; Gaptooth slack water. Serene. Bobs in the empty tub; glows at the Undersong; follows Vesh's lures. Floats where a marker is needed. Affection: a cool bump to the shin.
- **Mopclaw** (Tide Gap). Shy crab in a mop-head shell; Gaptooth wrack. Timid, tidy. Scrubs the deck at night; hides in the real mop bucket; sidles from Grist. One pinch, then a sprint. Affection: a spotless floor.
- **Cinderbun** (Hot Hand). Warm ember dumpling with tiny feet; Bellows Row ash heaps. Content, sleepy. Sits in the ash drawer; glows when Juno laughs; rolls off anything slanted. A hand-warmer. Affection: a nudge into a cold palm.
- **Dicemite** (Hot Hand). Beetle-mite with a pip-marked shell; Cinder Palace floorboards. Lucky, smug. Lands on the winning die; hides in Nim's pencil cup; clicks at a bluff. Fouls a dealer's shuffle. Affection: a pip-up perch on the shoulder.
- **Tuftle** (Silent Cut). Puff of shadow fur with two big eyes; Lanternreach lamp-shadows. Watchful, kind. Fills the darkest corner; blinks with the lamps; rides Hush unnoticed. Darkens a doorway. Affection: a soft weight on the foot.
- **Lamplick** (Silent Cut). Moth that licks lantern glass; the Lantern Baths. Greedy, cheerful. Polishes every lamp; naps in hot glass; flutters at Silas's slate. A dazzle of wing-dust. Affection: licks a nose.
- **Rivetling** (Hold the Deck). Iron hedgehog studded with rivets; the Keelbreak slag heap. Prickly, loyal. Rolls into tool-chests; sharpens rivets on Grist's shell; sits under Bram's stool. A rolled-up trip hazard. Affection: spines flat for one hand only.
- **Spoonling** (Hold the Deck). Begging stowaway with a ladle tail; the Gullwake's own galley. Shameless, hungry. Taps the pot at mealtimes; stands in the serving line; sleeps in the bread basket. Steals a trooper's ration. Affection: a ladle-tail round the wrist.
- **Glimmick** (Split Light). Snail with a faceted crystal shell; the Ward's crystal caves. Slow, serene. Parks in Prism's beam; leaves a glittering trail Ione footnotes; sleeps in the vial rack. Bends a beam one notch. Affection: a cool shell against the cheek.
- **Shardle** (Split Light). Round glass chick that chimes; the Ward's greenhouse floor. Nervous, brave at the wrong moments. Chimes at crystal growth before Ione feels it; nests in her coat pocket; follows Glimmick's trail. One note that cracks a thin pane. Affection: a chime at the pitch that puts Ione to sleep.

## 9. Across the media, and what to watch

- **Website:** island cards may use each entry's first sentence; the Monster Book may use section 8 whole. The site never reveals the Sleeper, the Count or the symbol before the manga does.
- **Games:** Hold the Deck's lanes are the galley's approaches; Storm Run's stages are Pinhold; the hum may sound in Tide Gap's deep water and Split Light's boss, never as words.
- **Manga and anime:** three wavy lines, every time. Anchor Day bunting on the chains in Issues 1, 10, 27 and 42. Charts on a pinned island's walls are always correct, and that is always the horror.
- **Noticed, not touched (sibling dossiers disagree; not resolved here):** Tam's spouse (Joss / Hesper) and last word; Burr's kind (knell-hound / song-hound); Kell's kinship to Corin (none / great-aunt); the first pinned island (Tallis Bank / Lowmoor); Vasch's age at Oren's survey (36 / 31); Ione's lab start (five / six years ago). This file names none of the disputed values.
