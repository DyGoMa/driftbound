## Director Aurel Vasch (46)

**Birthplace and family.** Lowmark, a hospital island in the inner ring where the Admiralty sent sailors to die quietly. Father a bonesetter, mother a records clerk; only child. He counted pulses before he could read.

**Before we meet him.** At 19, a medical student in Meridian, he bonded with **Vesper** in the hospital's wash-steps tide pool. From 20 to 31 he ran the Cost Ward, the Empire's one ward for people whose bonds were eating them, and borrowed Vesper's numbing touch to take his patients' pain. At 24 his right fingertips went dead; at 31 the arm was dead to the shoulder. At 33 he built the first bond-cutter and used it on himself, counting aloud, alone. Vesper became the first Hollow. At 34 the arm came off and the brass-and-glass one went on, Vesper's core in the forearm. At 35 Regent Hale read his paper and funded the Sever. At 36 Oren Brask's survey reached him from the Admiralty archive and he saw the pulse in it. At 40 he found Corin Vale. At 43 he stopped at five on Ione.

**The bond and its first cost.** Vesper's power was mercy in the literal sense: a touch that numbed. Its first cost was the feeling in the hand he healed with; he noticed when a dying woman squeezed his fingers and he felt nothing.

**Three defining memories.** (1) Age 26: a bonded fisherman on the Cost Ward begging him to cut the thing out of his chest, and Vasch having only his hand to offer. (2) Age 33: the count; his voice stopped at seven and he finished it in his head, and has never said it was hard. (3) Age 43: Ione's wrist under the cutter, five, and his hand lifting because she said his first name.

**Wants and fears.** Wants a world where nobody pays again, and means it. Fears being merely cruel: a ledger of pain that never balances. Does not fear Kaito. Fears Ione's regrowing mark, because it means he was wrong.

**How he talks.** Gentle, patient, a good doctor at a bedside. Never gloats, never swears, uses first names, asks permission he does not need.
1. "May I see the arm? Thank you. Yes. That's further than last month."
2. "I don't ask you to agree with me. I ask you to count what it has cost you."
3. "Nobody chooses a bond. A child falls in love with a monster and spends thirty years paying for it."
4. "One." (Issue 5, and nothing else on the page.)
5. "Call it cruelty if that helps you. I've been called worse by people I've healed."
6. "He said you'd have his temper." (Issue 37)
7. "Ione, you were never an experiment. You were the proof."
8. "I can wait." (Issue 36)
9. "Hekka, you'll want to sit for this part. No? Then stand. I'll be quick."
10. "There's no mask on me, Kaito. I show you my face because I'm not ashamed of it."

**With the crew, one line each.** Kaito: the son of the man whose survey built the weapon; Vasch is gentle with him, which is the worst thing about him. Sela: a case study; he knows what she lost and who. Juno: he offered to buy her marker before Krell did, and she spat. Silas: beneath notice until Lanternreach's eleven. Bram: the only refusal that shakes him (Issue 36). Ione: his best work and his one failure; he writes, she doesn't answer.

**Secrets, and where they land.** The core in his arm is his own partner (Issue 36, said plainly during the offer). He stripped himself first (Issue 36). He knows Silt was Sela's first mate (a half line in Issue 36 when he offers her memory back; proposed). He has never told the Regent about the Sleeper (Issue 46, through Sable).

**Wardrobe (artists).** Canon white belted coat, sleeves rolled, glass forearm bare. Variations: a surgical apron over it in the Glass Ward; a black court sash for Meridian audiences; coat closed and sleeves down in Part Two's mercy clinics (he hides the arm from the starving). Never casual, never undressed, no fan service near him.

**Fighting style.** He does not fight; he stands still and lets the room come to him. The arm's grip stops a Surging fist dead and, for one breath, quiets the bond under it (short-range cutter, PROVISIONAL). Hollows answer his voice. Game form: Split Light's moving-core puzzle.

**Never.** Never raises his voice. Never calls a Hollow "it" (he says "the patient"). Never lies about what a thing costs. Never touches anyone unasked. Never says "monster." Never laughs at pain, his own included.

### Vesper (the core in the arm)
A salt bell-jelly: lantern-pale, a bell the size of a bucket, trailing threads; element Brine, outside the six. Found in the Meridian hospital's tide pool. Temperament: slow, curious, drawn to fever heat. Habits: settled on sleeping patients' chests; dimmed when Vasch was angry; rang faintly in salt wind. Fought by stinging to numb, never to kill. Affection: it rested on his dead hand, which he could not feel. The core still brightens near a bonded pair. He has not said its name in thirteen years.

## Captain Hekka Dorn (33)

**Birthplace and family.** Kestle, a fortress island on the Empire's cold outer ring. Mother a navy quartermaster who raised her in barracks; father a sailor she never met; two younger half-brothers, both navy. Enlisted at 18 because everyone did.

**Before we meet her.** At 21, a corporal, she found a wounded ash-grey ridgeback bear on Kestle's ice ledge and sat with it through a night of sleet rather than shoot it. By morning the mark was on her left side. She named it Mallow, the softest word she knew. From 22 to 29 she rose to lieutenant in the Admiralty marines, Mallow at her heel, the most feared boarding party in the outer ring. At 28 she could no longer feel cold, then warmth. At 30 Vasch told her the cost would reach her heart in two years and, the same week, offered her a Sever captaincy, which asks loyalty. She walked into Ione's lab with the bear and did it standing (stories.js, "What Hekka did"). At 31 she took the Seventh seat.

**The bond and its first cost.** Mallow's element was Frost: her hands went white-cold and hard, her hits numbed what they struck. The first cost: she stopped feeling the heat of a cup. The last: she stopped feeling her own pulse.

**Three defining memories.** (1) Age 21: a bear's breath on her neck all night and deciding not to be afraid. (2) Age 30: Mallow's eyes going white at six, and finishing anyway because stopping makes a problem. (3) The next morning: the bear taking fish from her hand politely, like a stranger's animal.

**Wants and fears.** Wants an order that makes sense and a fight that is fair. Fears nothing physical. Fears learning the strip was for nothing, and feeding Mallow forever knowing it.

**How she talks.** Blunt soldier, few words, bleak humour, no cruelty. Flirts like she fights: direct, enjoying it, armour on.
1. "Harpoons. Then we talk."
2. "He doesn't know me. I still feed him. That's not a riddle, Vey, it's a job."
3. "You've got good legs for a navigator. Shame about the ship."
4. "Full count. I stood up for it. Sit down for yours if you like."
5. "Orders say strip it. Orders don't say watch."
6. "Cold? I wouldn't know."
7. "Pull back. All of you. That's mine to answer for." (Issue 32)
8. "You stopped my own bear for me. Don't make it a thing."
9. "I don't hate your crew, Vey. I just outrank my opinion."
10. "Again, then. You're the only one who makes it worth the lance."

**With the crew, one line each.** Kaito: a loud kid she oddly wants to see grow up. Sela: the rival; they like each other too much to stop (Issues 12, 25). Juno: swaps dirty jokes across a battlefield, none of it a threat. Silas: distrusts the smile, enjoys the duel. Bram: she nearly kills him, then calls the retreat herself; she owes him Tam and knows it (Issue 32). Ione: wrote the procedure and handed her the cutter; respect with a chill.

**Secrets, and where they land.** She stripped Mallow herself, unordered (Issue 12, told by her, fighting). She carries the bear's old collar tag, name scratched off (Issue 31, falls from her belt when the deck breaks; proposed). Vasch's two-year prediction was a guess (Part Two, unplaced). She has begun reading the Standing Orders for what they do not say (Issue 40, one-panel plant; proposed).

**Wardrobe (artists).** Canon grey plates, bare midriff with ring scar, dark cloth, loose trousers, lance. Variations: Regatta duel kit (plates off, dark sleeveless vest, wrapped forearms; the scar shows); off-duty navy wool coat open over the same vest; Part Two not yet called. Flirting with Sela happens in full armour. Never framed for the viewer; the scar is a plot detail.

**Fighting style.** Strength and reach, no Surge: the lance two-handed like a boarding pike, hooks, sweeps, a shoulder-charge that uses her height. Mallow is loosed on a chain word. She feels no cold and fights waist-deep in the sea. Game forms: Tide Gap's cutter, Hold the Deck's bear phases.

**Never.** Never strikes someone who is down. Never lies. Never lets anyone else feed the bear. Never says Mallow's name aloud (Order 12). Never flirts with anyone hurt or held. Never asks for mercy, hers or anyone's.

### Mallow (the chained Hollow bear)
A ridgeback bear, ash-grey, 2.2 m at the shoulder, now plated and cored (CAST). Found on Kestle's ice ledge with a harpoon in its flank. Temperament before: patient, greedy, protective. Habits now: sits when the chain goes slack; turns toward Hekka's voice without knowing why; licks frost off its own plates. Fights by weight and wrecking, no mind in it. Affection: before, it leaned; now it takes food from her hand without biting the hand, which Ione says is not affection and Hekka says is.

## Silt (Corin Vale, 29)

**Birthplace and family.** Cantor Isle, the drift-singers' village, now a ghost town (Issue 34). Mother Maren Vale, a keel-voice who sang the island's course; father Anselm, a net-mender. One elder sister, Tessaly, who left for Meridian and does not know he is alive. Taught the half-dark, singing with one eye covered to hear the song better, by Mother Kell, no kin.

**Before we meet him.** At 16 he bonded with **Burr**, a knell-hound pup found under the Cantor bell-house. At 20 he signed onto the Gullwake as a hand under its 19-year-old captain, Sela Vey; within the year he was first mate and they were lovers; he taught her the song by humming it and called her nothing, and she called him pup. At 21 she went too deep for the old Gullwake and came up not knowing him (bible, section 3). He tore his page out, kept his word that it never happened, and walked. For a year he drifted, drinking, sleepless. At 22 Vasch found him on a Lanternreach pier and said the only true thing anyone had: "You'll never get her back. I can make sure nobody else loses what you lost." He stripped Burr himself, full count. At 23 he took the mask; Nine of the Ten Count by 27.

**The bond and its first cost.** Burr's element was Echo: its howl carried through rock and water, and through it Corin heard the Undersong louder than any living Cantor. Every big Surge cost him sleep he never got back. By 21 he slept an hour a night. After the strip he could sleep again and found he did not want to.

**Three defining memories.** (1) Age 20: Sela on the mast humming his song back wrong, and him not correcting her. (2) Age 21: her face asking who he was, and his hand already moving to the logbook. (3) Age 22: Burr's eyes at ten, and the silence where the song had been.

**Wants and fears.** Wants the song to stop so it will stop hurting. Fears Sela remembering, because she would lose him twice. Fears, in Part Two, that he was wrong about the first thing and so about all of it.

**How he talks.** Flat, sparse, tired, sometimes cruelly kind. Short sentences, no swearing, says "Brask" and never "Kaito" until Issue 48.
1. "Found you." (Issue 1)
2. "Why do you run? It's the same sea." (Issue 4)
3. "Run. It's better when you run."
4. "You're the only one who keeps getting up. I do love that about you."
5. "Burning that arm for one dog. I admire the waste."
6. "What did she call you?" (Issue 19)
7. "You made it up." (Issue 41)
8. "Hm. Keep that arm warm for me, Brask."
9. "I don't hate you. I'm tired. You'll learn the difference."
10. "Six is at Meridian. Bring the song." (Issue 48)

**With the crew, one line each.** Kaito: his dark mirror, the boy still running; he wants him to keep getting up and does not know why. Sela: the only person who knew his name, and she paid it away; he hunts her ship because Vasch asked and because it is the only way to see her. Juno: a gambler; he has no odds to give her. Silas: moves as quietly as he does; mutual professional distaste. Bram: joined the Gullwake after Corin left and never knew him, which Silt is grateful for. Ione: wrote the procedure he used on Burr; he thanked her once and she has never forgiven it.

**Secrets, and where they land.** The torn page and the break at "pup" (Issue 19). The song (Issue 41, he hums it). His name: Mother Kell names "a Cantor boy, Corin, who sailed with a silver-haired girl" (Issue 34, proposed; the reader joins it to the mask in Issue 41). The hound tracks bonds because Burr's core still rings toward them (Ione's footnote, Issue 33; proposed). His face: one eye through the cracked mask in Issue 48; a full reveal is a Part Two call not yet made.

**Wardrobe (artists).** Canon mask, ragged black coat, hooked blade, gloves. Variations: coat off, grey Sever under-tunic with white collar; mask off, face unseen (back of the head, hand over the face); as Corin in flashback (Issues 19, 41, proposed): age 20 to 21, sea-grey Cantor wrap, bare feet, black hair in a sailor's braid, a small ring mark on the left palm. Childhood never shown; no fan service near him or any Corin flashback.

**Fighting style.** Patient: the hound runs you down and he arrives after. Hooked cables from the saddle, a bond-cutter blade close in, feints that read your rhythm, because he hears your Surge coming as a change in the hum. He stops when he loses interest. Game form: Storm Run's boss.

**Never.** Never says Sela's name. Never hums on purpose. Never kills someone who is down (he waits for them to get up). Never uses "mercy." Never takes the mask off where he can be seen. Never answers "what's your name" with anything but silence.

### Burr (the Hollow hound)
A knell-hound: lean, long-jawed, grey-brindled, a chest that boomed when it howled; element Echo. Found as a pup under the Cantor bell-house. Temperament before: solemn, clingy, hated being sung at. Habits now: circles once before lying down; turns to any humming; the core flickers near a bond mark. Fights by lunge and cable-drag, core exposed when it rears (Storm Run). Affection: it slept across Corin's feet so he could not leave without waking it; the Hollow still lies across his feet, and he steps over it.

## The Sever (the institution)

**Founded** eleven years ago by Regent's warrant on Vasch's paper, "On the Severance of the Bond as Medicine." Officially a medical corps; in practice the Empire's secret weapons works. **Symbol:** a line cutting a circle: a cut bond, and (Issue 35) a map. **Uniform:** grey coat, white collar, black bar; white for the Director only. **Hollows:** stripped monsters, plated, cabled, cored, sold to the Admiralty, which asks nothing.

**Structure.** The Director; the Ten Count (bible, section 4); under them Captains (field), Wardens (shield lines), Deacons (rites), Ledgers (buying partners and debts), Doctors (Hollow surgery), Hunters, Ostlers (Hollow handling); then troopers, paid double navy wages, recruited from people who lost someone to a cost. **Base:** the Still Room, a white chart hall in Meridian where Vasch counts. The Glass Ward, the first lab, is dead. **Standing Orders:** 12 is canon in full (stories.js). Also: Order 7, "No trooper serves on the island of their birth"; Order 9, "Mercy is spoken to the subject once, then not again." **Part Two:** mercy clinics (Issue 45) offer free strips to the starving, and they work.

## The Meridian Empire and Regent Emeric Hale (63)

**The Empire.** Thirty-odd pinned or garrisoned islands in two rings around Meridian, the capital, itself the first island anchored by ordinary pylons forty years ago. It sells safety: a pinned island's charts stay right and its wells die within a decade (Issue 1); nobody says both in one breath. Its navy is the Admiralty (Yevka Sorn's service). Its enemies: moving islands, smugglers' routes, anyone who sails by feel.

**The Anchor Seat.** Empty twenty years, since the last Empress died childless. Her chancellor, Emeric Hale, was named Regent "until the Seat is filled" and has not filled it.

**Hale.** Born in Meridian, 63, a chart-house family. An admiral at 43 who lost four hundred sailors when two drifting islands ground together in fog: the night he decided the sea must hold still. Widower, no children. Tired, decent in small ways, blind in the one that matters: he believes the Great Pylons are bigger anchors and that he is buying peace. He has never heard the word Sleeper. His envoy **Sable Oriel** (Ten) is his eyes, and she looks like whoever he trusts, which is almost no one. Ostler **Hale** is no relation; the name is common in Meridian, and writers should not imply kinship without a bible call.

**Hale's voice.** "Show me the chart, Director. I'm too old for the speech." / "Four hundred names. I read them every Anchor Day." / "If you've lied to me, Aurel, I'll find out when the sea tells me."

**Where he lands.** Issue 46 through Sable; Issue 47, his records barge, Oren Brask chained to a chart table; Issue 48, "Six is at Meridian." Whether he turns on learning the truth is a bible call not yet made.
