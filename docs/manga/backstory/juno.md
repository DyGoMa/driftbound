## JUNO CALLOWAY

### Birthplace and family
Born on **Ashmarket**, in **Sootside**: the lower tiers of the volcanic crag, under the gambling halls, where the smelting furnaces of **Bellows Row** melt down Drift salvage into the lantern-glass, chips and ironwork the city runs on. The furnace heat is piped up through vents under the market floors to warm the halls above; the rich gamble on a warm floor because the poor are sweating under it. (Those are the vents she overloads in Issue 9.)

- **Dace Calloway**, her father: a small-time floor gambler and "spotter" who found marks for bigger players. Charming, funny, always one hand from fixing everything. He taught her odds. He staked her and lost her. He left Ashmarket within the week and she has not seen him since; she does not know if he is alive.
- **Tess Calloway**, her mother: a furnace-hand on Bellows Row, dry-humoured, died of ash-lung when Juno was 16. Juno has her laugh.
- No siblings. **Nim Hadda** (30, one-armed bookmaker, bible) is the nearest thing: seven years older, her partner in crime from 17 to 19, the only person in Ashmarket who never took a bet on her.

### The years before Issue 6 (ages at each event)
- **0 to 16.** Sootside. Cards on the stoop with Dace; lantern smoke; the hum of the furnaces as a lullaby. Plain, warm, poor.
- **16.** Tess dies. Juno takes her mother's lantern-tending shift on Bellows Row. Learns she can stand nearer the furnace mouths than the grown hands, and that they pay more for it.
- **17 to 19.** Runs a two-hander with Nim in the back rooms: she is the loud face, he keeps the book. First taste of winning. First time anyone calls her "sweetheart" in a tone she decides to steal and own.
- **19.** The night of the stake (bible, Issue 10, spoken only). Dace, deep in to furnace-master **Orso Vell**, puts his daughter's labour on the table at a back game in Bellows Row and loses. The marker is a sum, four thousand marks, to be worked off at furnace wages. She stands behind him holding his drink. He does not turn around. Vell shakes his hand.
- **19 to 22.** The furnaces. She takes the flue-clearing nobody else will do because it pays double: that is where the burn tolerance begins, exactly as she says in Issue 10. She keeps the pair of furnace gauntlets they issue her.
- **20.** Ashka (below). Her palms. From here she is the best flue-hand on the row and Vell works her like it.
- **22.** Pays the last mark on the morning of **Ember Night**, Ashmarket's yearly festival when the furnaces are banked and every lantern in the city is relit from one carried flame. Walks out with Ashka on her shoulder and the gauntlets cut down to fingerless gloves. Vell files a claim that Ashka is stolen furnace property. That claim, plus a year of Cinder Palace markers, is the paper Warden Krell buys in Issue 6, which is why he can say he will "take Ashka" with a straight face and a deadline of Ember Night.
- **22 to 23.** A year at the tables on Nim's stake, up and down, mostly up, never far enough up. Issue 6 finds her cheating a Sever officer at cards and betting a stranger named Kaito he can't last ten seconds.

### The bond, and what it first cost
Ashka was never wild. Fire raptors nest in the crag's volcanic vents, and Bellows Row has trapped them for generations as living pilot-lights: a chained bird in the firebox so Furnace Six never goes cold overnight. At 20 Juno is sent to clear Six's flue and finds the bird on a short chain, half-starved, feathers burned to the quill. She has seen it every day for a year. That night she goes back, opens the firebox and pulls the chain pin out with her bare hands. The bird does not flee. It climbs her arm, sinks its talons into her shoulder, and the mark burns in below her right collarbone. The fire goes through her palms in the same instant: her first Surge, unasked for, both hands blistered to the wrist. They never heal flat. She laughs in the ash, because the heat is in her and she can hold it.

**First cost:** her hands, and the lie she tells about them. "Furnace" is true and it is not the whole truth. She let people believe the burns were given to her. She chose them.

### Three defining memories
1. **Age 7, the stoop.** Dace deals a deck by lantern light and makes her count it down. She beats him for the first time and he laughs so hard the neighbours bang on the wall. Warm, plain. Later, poisoned: he was teaching her to be worth something at a table.
2. **Age 19, the back room.** Holding his drink. The back of his head. Vell's hand. She says nothing, because she is already counting what the marker costs per hour and how many hours she has. She has never cried about this and she will tell you so, and that is the tell.
3. **Age 20, Furnace Six.** The pin, the chain, the bird on her arm, her hands coming apart, and her laughing. The one memory she would never sell.

### What she wants, what she fears
**Wants:** to be the one holding the book. Never to be on anybody's paper again. To pay out on every bet she ever lost, in full, so nobody can say she welshed. Under that: for somebody to stay at the table after the money is gone.

**Fears:** her hands locking at the wrong moment, with somebody behind her. Being staked again; Krell saying "I'll take Ashka" is her nightmare word for word. Pity. Being loved for real, because it is a bet she cannot hedge, which is exactly why she bets on herself in Issue 10 and collects in Issue 40.

### With the crew (one line each)
- **Kaito:** the only person she bets against to make him win; the slow burn runs Issues 6 to 40, and in Part Two neither of them knows what they are.
- **Sela:** the one player she cannot bluff; Sela reads her tells at the High Table and Juno, for once, likes being read.
- **Silas:** her double act; they out-filth each other for the crew's entertainment and she is the first to notice when the jokes are covering silence.
- **Bram:** carried her into the harbour in Issue 9 and cooled her through the fever in Issue 10; she can't repay that, so she loses to him at cards on purpose and he pretends not to know.
- **Ione:** Juno bets Ione betrays them and Ione takes the bet herself (Issue 33); Ione is the only one allowed to look at the palms, and the only one who never says sorry about them.
- **Ashka and Skarn:** mutual, fluent disgust; the two of them on the roof in Issue 40 are the series' best critics.

### Secrets, and the issue that reveals each
- **Issue 8:** the gloves come off to fight. The reader sees the palms. The gloves themselves are cut-down furnace gauntlets, visible in the art, named nowhere yet.
- **Issue 10:** her father staked her at nineteen; three years in the furnaces; "you can't lose what you've already wagered." Spoken only. On the same night she places the first bet in the "Kaito and who?" pool, in Nim's book, on herself, under no name. Nobody knows until Issue 40.
- **Issue 40:** how the palms really happened: not the furnace, the chain pin. She takes the gloves off for Kaito and tells him she chose it. Hands only on the page.
- **Issue 41:** Krell dies burning, and she does not take a single bet on that fight, before or after. Silas notices.
- **Issue 44:** a stack of unsent letters on the *Second Chance*, all addressed "Dace Calloway, wherever." Kaito finds them. She has written one a month since she was 22.

### Fighting style
She fights the way she plays: ante, raise, all in. Open with cheap flicks of fire to see who flinches ("ante"), count the room aloud in odds, then one big thrown ball when somebody commits ("raise"). Her heat climbs with every throw and so does her power, so she plays near the top on purpose. Ashka circles and screeches at a seam in the armour and Juno hits it; she does not look where Ashka isn't. Close in, she grabs a visor with a lit palm. No armour, no block, no retreat: her only defence is finishing first. **All in** is the overload (Issues 9 and 41): she holds more fire than she can carry, fever on-panel, numbers getting shorter, and afterward her hands lock and somebody has to carry her. Against shields she goes around them, through the floor, through the vents, through anything but the shield. Her gun, when it is decided, is for distance; the hands are the character.

### Never
- Never takes the gloves off for comfort, a dare or a joke; only to fight (Issue 8, 9, 41) and once for Kaito (Issue 40).
- Never bets on a Pal or anywhere near one. Never jokes about a child, a childhood or anyone frightened or hurt.
- Never says "I'm scared." She says she hasn't got a number.
- Never welshes. A lost bet is paid in full, that day, with a grin.
- Never calls Ashka "it," and never lets anyone else.
- Never uses the word "mercy" except to spit it.
- Never cheats a friend for anything but their own good, and never twice.
- Never pities anyone. She gives them odds instead, which is her version of hope.
- Never asks Nim about the arm.

## ASHKA

**Species.** A fire raptor: Ashmarket's crag-hawks, which nest in the volcanic vents and eat the heat as much as the meat. Large-eagle size, ember-red and black, sparks trailing off the feather edges, long tail plumes glowing like coals, hooked beak, yellow eyes, a spiky crest (locked sheet). She smells of struck matches. Her feathers are warm to the touch and never quite still.

**Where found.** Chained in the firebox of Furnace Six on Bellows Row as a living pilot-light, a year of Juno walking past her, then one night of a chain pin and two burned hands.

**Temperament.** Vain, imperious, a born judge. She believes she is the senior partner and Juno the loud one. She sulks, visibly, by turning her back; she forgives by preening. Hates water the way only a fire creature can: when Bram carries Juno into the harbour in Issue 9, Ashka screams on the pier and nobody on the crew ever mentions it to her.

**Three habits.**
1. Preens sparks into Juno's braid. The braid's cord is kept wet for exactly this reason; the singed ends are canon.
2. Steals shiny chips, coins and Kaito's goggles and hoards them in the galley stove, which is Bram's Galley Rule Six.
3. Sleeps on the stove hood, warm side down, one eye open on the room.

**How she fights.** Spotter, never brawler. She circles, picks the seam in the armour or the trooper reaching for the cutter, and screeches the spot; Juno throws at the sound. She dives to blind, drops embers on a shield line, and goes for the hand that holds a blade, never the body. When Juno's heat tops out, Ashka lands on her and will not leave, which has twice nearly killed them both. In the game Hot Hand she marks weak targets for bonus; this is that.

**How she shows affection.** Tucks her head down against Juno's neck (Issue 12, kept). Brings her one coin after a bad night and drops it in her glove. Warms the bunk before she gets in it. Toward the crew: she permits Bram, tolerates Sela, disdains Kaito on principle, and watches Silas like a card she knows is marked. Toward Skarn: open contempt, returned with interest. She is never in a flirting scene, never in a bath gag, never near a Pal in any way that plays as a joke on either of them.
