## Vitals (CAST.md, locked; not repeated here)
Silas Moreau, 24, 183 cm; violet eyes, beauty mark under the left eye, long dark-teal hair tied low; open white shirt, red sash, chain wound over the sash to a curved knife at the right hip; three tiny violet feathers down the left side of his neck. Cost: each Surge steals his breath. Partner: Hush, a leopard-sized silent wind owl-lynx. Part Two (26): voice almost gone, a scarf, no buttons.

## Birthplace and family [new]
Born on **Brittlemere**, a reed-and-stilt island of lamp-oil renderers south of Lanternreach, where the fish-oil vats burn all night. Only child of **Odile Moreau**, a vat-hand who sang at the rendering fires (every vat-crew sings; it keeps the stir even), and **Teodor Moreau**, a reed-boat ferryman who ran "a little of everything" across the fog to Lanternreach. Silas has his mother's voice and his father's habit of calling a smuggle a ferry. The violet eyes are hers; the island calls them "lamp eyes" and thinks them lucky.

## The years before we meet him (ages at each event)
- **0 to 9.** A plain childhood. He sings at the vats with his mother, and is good at it. [new]
- **9.** Empire customs impound Teodor's ferry over lamp-oil duty. Teodor borrows the price of a new boat from the Lanternreach counting-house of **Ledger Maro**, then 37. Maro brings a receipt and says the sentence Silas will hear for fifteen years: "Numbers are patient." [new]
- **12.** Teodor is lost in a night squall on a late crossing, running a payment to Maro before the quarter closed. The debt passes to Odile, with Maro's condolences. [new]
- **14.** Silas runs messages for the counting-house after his lessons stop. The clerks like him because he can repeat a whole conversation back word for word, in each voice. Maro notices. [new]
- **17.** Odile dies of vat-lung. Lanternreach hangs one funeral lamp for every person who owed the dead; Odile gets none, so Maro pays for one, and Silas never forgets which lamp it was. The debt is "forgiven" in exchange for Silas's employment. [new]
- **18.** He moves to Lanternreach for good: courier, clerk, then "the voice of the house", the man Maro sends to close a deal gently. He learns the chain from the doorkeeper, **Auntie Hobb** (then 57), who kept order with a weighted chain and said he had wrists for it. [new]
- **19.** First partner sale: a dock-hand's bonded river hound, drunk-sold to "a collector" for the price of a bar tab. Within a month Silas knows the collector is the Sever. He stays. [new]
- **21.** Hush (below). Nine days without a voice. [new]
- **23.** Marit Sund (memory three, below). [new]
- **24.** The night of the eleven, Issue 15. His years with Maro end on that dock.

By Issue 15 his column in the steam ledger runs to **thirty-one partners** sold to the Sever over five years. He let eleven out. The sum does not come out, and he knows it. [new]

## How the bond happened and what it first cost [new]
At 21 he is sent to Maro's holding pens at **Tallow Dock**, where lots wait for the Sever's cutter-ship, to quiet a new catch: a wind owl-lynx from the **Quiet Shoals**, hooded in leather after cutting two handlers to the bone with its wing-feathers. Nobody could get near enough to feed it, and a lot that starves does not sell.

Silas does the only thing he knows. He sits outside the bars and talks about nothing: the lamps, the clerks, the weather. The first night it faces the wall. The second night it faces him. On the third night it puts its hooded face against the bars, he realises he has been talking for an hour, and his throat closes as if a hand were on it: the bond takes, unasked, and the first Surge goes through him before he knows what a Surge is. Every lamp on Tallow Dock goes out in one breath of wind. He cannot speak for nine days. The voice comes back with the rasp, and it never sings again.

The first cost was not the voice. Maro had already sold the lot, and the Sever does not refund, so Silas buys his own partner out of the pen at the Sever's price, paid in his cut of the next twenty sales. He bought Hush with other people's partners. He has never said this aloud; it is why he has no depths in public. He named it Hush because that was the last word anyone said to him for nine days.

## The Maro years, in detail [new unless cited]
Ledger Maro (52, 172 cm, soft-spoken, lamp-lit, account books; bible and `FRIDAY_BATCH.md`) is not a shouting villain, and writers must never make him one. He is the gentlest man Silas ever worked for: never raised his voice, never hit him, paid for a lamp at his mother's funeral, and taught him that the price of anything is the thing the other person needs to hear, and that a kind man can do accounts.

Maro tallies people as numbers (bible section 5): a human in the ledger is a lot number, a partner a weight and an element. Silas's gift was turning the number back into a face for exactly as long as it took to sign. He was the house's duelist by 22, settling quay disputes with the chain so nobody had to be loud.

Lanternreach's rule that nobody uses names (bible, Issue 15) is Maro's: a name is a debt you can be found by. Silas kept his out of vanity, which Maro allowed because vanity is cheap. His name heads page one of the steam ledger (Issue 16) because he was the best broker the house had, and because Maro, politely, wanted him to know he was in it.

Maro's Sever contact at the pens, who inspected each lot before the cutter-ship sailed, was **Lucan Ash**, Six of the Ten Count, who never once looked at Silas while they spoke. Silas has met his "opposite" (bible) a dozen times before Issue 26; Lucan does not remember him. [new]

## The night of the eleven (Issue 15; `stories.js` "cargo")
The day the Gullwake reaches Lanternreach, Silas steals its cargo for the house, as a job, smiling (bible, Issue 15). That evening the Sever's cutter-ship ties up at Tallow Dock to collect a lot of eleven partners, four of them brokered by Silas. It brings its own pen guard down the gangway: a Hollow river hound, cables for veins, a white core where a bond was. He knows the gait before the face. It is his first sale, from when he was 19. [new]

He waits until the dock is empty. No buyer, no client, no one to see (he thinks). He cuts eleven locks with a chain and a breath of wind, and his voice is gone before the last cage opens. Kaito, chasing the cargo, sees it all from the tallow-house roof (bible). It is the night he stopped pretending he only did it for money (`stories.js`). The eleven come back in Issue 39 with new humans (bible, plants table).

## Three defining memories
1. **The morning after the squall (12).** The empty ferry slip, and his mother on the quay counting lamps instead of crying, because a Brittlemere woman cries at the vats where the smoke explains it. He learned there that you can hold a face. Spoken once, Issue 17, in two sentences; never drawn with a child in frame. [new]
2. **The third night at the bars (21).** The hooded face against the iron, the moment he heard himself and could not stop, and then could not speak. The only honest hour of his twenties, spent talking to a cat. [new]
3. **Marit Sund (23).** A reed-fisher from his own island, 34, whose bonded otter-hound he had brokered the month before, came to the counting-house and asked for it back. She did not shout; she had the money. Maro said, "Silas, show her out," and he did, charmingly, a hand at her elbow and a joke about the stairs, and she laughed. He learned later the hound was already a Hollow when she asked. The eleven were meant to pay for this. They don't. [new]

## What he wants and what he fears
**Wants:** to be loved without being read. To talk his way back to before the ledger, which cannot be done, so instead thirty-one things put right, one at a time, counted privately and never mentioned. Hush safe, above everything, and he would deny it under oath. A kitchen where he is allowed to be annoying at dawn. [new]

**Fears:** not silence, but being held to account with no words in his mouth: the slate, the empty balloon, the question he cannot answer with a joke. Being read, which is why Ione is the most dangerous person he has met. Maro's politeness, which he has never seen fail. And the sum: someone doing the arithmetic out loud, thirty-one against eleven, in front of the crew. [new]

## How he talks
Silky, "darling" to everyone of every gender, innuendo as breathing, long sentences that arrive late, a faint rasp. Serious: very short sentences. Surged out: a slate or nothing, the same voice with worse handwriting. Ten lines:

1. "Darling, I've been stabbed by better people than you, and two of them I still write to."
2. "Smuggling is only honesty with a time delay. The cargo gets where it's wanted; it merely declines to say so."
3. "Bram, that stew is doing something indecent to the whole deck, and I want you to know I noticed."
4. "Odds, Juno? Put me down for slapped by Thursday and grateful by Friday."
5. "Captain, you run a room the way I used to run a counting-house, and I mean that as the second-highest compliment I own."
6. "Doctor, if you're going to look at me like a specimen, at least label the jar."
7. (Serious.) "Thirty-one. Don't ask me again."
8. (Slate, Issue 19.) "DARLING. STIR SLOWER. I'M WATCHING."
9. (Slate, after rubbing something out.) "Never mind. Get on the boat."
10. "If the rasp bothers you, you should have heard me before Hush. I was unbearable. I was a tenor." [last sentence new]

## Fighting style
Silas fights the way he talks: he leads you into finishing his sentence. The chain unwinds from the sash as he moves, so an opponent never sees its length until it is round their wrist; the knife is for the last word only. A Short Surge is one breath out: a wind-cut along the chain that puts a lamp out at twenty paces. A long Surge is the Silent Cut: one held exhale that carries him through a lit room with no sound, and no voice at the end of it. He beats people who commit and loses to people who don't, which is why Lucan Ash, a straight-line fencer who never feints, takes his voice twice (Issues 26, 41). The quips come before and after a fight, never during: breath is ammunition. Hush takes the other flank, always from above. [Lucan's style new]

## The "never" list
He would never: raise his voice (he can't afford it); sing; do up a button; say "I love you" aloud; say "sorry" in words (he writes it and rubs it out); touch another person's partner without the partner's leave; name Maro's price for anything; flirt with anyone frightened, hurt or held, or near a Pal; blame Hush for the voice; lie to Hush; do the arithmetic out loud; call Maro a monster, because he knows Maro isn't one, and that is the problem.

## Hush
**Species:** silent wind owl-lynx. A big cat's body, leopard-sized; lynx ear tufts and bobtail; a flat round owl face with a pale disc ringed in darker feathers and a short hooked beak; smoky grey-violet fur and feathers, pale belly, charcoal-violet wing tips; owl wings folded along the body, flight feathers edged like knives; glowing violet eyes. Moves without a sound (CAST.md, locked sheet `hush-sheet-v2.png`).

**Where found [new]:** the Quiet Shoals south of Lanternreach, fog so thick it swallows sound, taken by a fog-trapper working for Maro's pens. Wind owl-lynxes hunt there by silence.

**Temperament [new]:** aloof, fastidious, judging. Hates noise; Juno's laugh flattens its disc. Loves the galley at dawn when only Bram is up. Tolerates Kaito like weather. Sits where it can see every door.

**Three habits [new]:**
1. Preens its wing-blades on the ship's rail, leaving a neat curl of wood shavings Bram sweeps up every morning without comment.
2. Eats buttons: every button on the Gullwake, swallowed whole and coughed up in a tidy pile under Silas's bunk. This is why his shirt is never done up, and why nobody else's is by Issue 20. Explained only by Issue 19's slate: "NOT ME. THE CAT."
3. When Silas has no voice, Hush does the talking: one flat hoot at whoever annoys him, and a long stare. The crew learns to read it faster than the slate.

**How they fight [new]:** never head-on. Hush goes up out of sight and comes down through a lamp. One wingbeat snuffs a row of lanterns (the Silent Cut's dark is Hush as much as Silas); its wing-edges open ropes, lantern cords and sleeves as it passes. It never throws a feather; it walks past you. It never roars or screeches. In the games it is what takes out the light before the player moves.

**How they show affection [new]:** Hush presses the flat of its face to the left side of his throat, over the three feathers, feeling for the voice when he cannot use it, and will not move until he talks or writes. It unties his hair band at night and drops it in the button pile. He talks to it for an hour about nothing every evening, as he did at the bars: the one conversation on the ship he never performs. When Hush brings him a button, he has been quiet too long.
