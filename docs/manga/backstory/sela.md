For every medium. Looks and ages: `C:\Users\Dylan\Documents\driftbound-villains\docs\CAST.md`; the spine: `docs/manga/SERIES_BIBLE.md` section 3. Where both are silent this file invents, and every invented fact is marked **(new)**. Hard rules apply in full: every named adult is 21 or older today; childhood flashbacks, and the eight-years-ago flashback in which Sela is 20, are plain and sad with zero flirting or fan service on that page or the facing one; costs stay paid.

## 1. Sela Vey (28, captain of the Gullwake)

### Birthplace and family **(new)**
Born on **Ninefathom**, a low, free island of salt pans and net-menders at the shallow western edge of the Drift, never pinned, too poor to be worth a pylon. Her mother **Rhoswen Vey** (56) keeps the island's tide ledger, forty years of high waters in the same neat hand Sela has now. Her father **Dov Vey** (59) dives for lantern-weed in the drowned trench off the north shore; he taught her to go under calmly and come up slowly. One younger sister, **Marit Vey** (24), who stayed, mends nets, and writes Sela a letter every month. Sela keeps them in the trunk with the logbooks: the only account of her life she didn't write herself, and she trusts it more than her own pages.

### The years before we meet her
- **Age 7 (new).** Rhoswen has her copy the tide numbers. If ever drawn: a child at a table, salt on the window, a mother's hand correcting a 7, and nothing else.
- **Age 14 (new).** Starts diving the trench with Dov. Learns what she still says: nothing on top of the water is in charge of it.
- **Age 17 (new). The bond.** A young serpent-dragon the length of a rowing boat is tangled in the lantern-weed on the trench floor, lures dark. Sela goes down with a weed-knife on one breath, cuts, and runs out of air with its frilled head against her chest. The bond takes in the water: the whirlpool mark burns in below her left collarbone and one Surge of pressure throws them both onto the salt flats. **First cost:** for a day and a night Sela does not know her own name. Rhoswen writes it on the back of her hand in ledger ink and hands her a blank book: "If the sea takes it, the page keeps it." The first logbook starts that evening. Its first line is her name.
- **Age 18 (new).** Vesh outgrows the shallows. Sela leaves Ninefathom with her, the first Vey to leave in three generations.
- **Age 18 to 19.** A year at **Sorrow Point** under **Mag Tolliver**, pilot, seventy, hands like oars, who throws her into the harbour forty-one times before she can read a current with her eyes shut (Issue 11). Mag also teaches her the pilot's stew **(new)**. Sela calls this the best year of her life and says she doesn't need the book for it. She will.
- **Age 19 (new).** Signs on as pilot aboard the salvage barque **Gullwake** under **Captain Hesper Lund** (a woman, then 61), the only captain willing to let a serpent tow her ship through gaps nobody else can run. The first mate is a quiet man of 20 who ships under no name but "the Cantor" and can tell you where an island will be tomorrow by listening to the water. **Corin Vale.**
- **Age 20.** Lund dies of a fever ashore at Sorrow Point and leaves the Gullwake to Sela **(new)**. Corin stays as her first mate; within the year they are more than that. Four months into her captaincy comes the Surge that saves the ship and ends him. See section 3.
- **Age 20 to 23 (new).** Lund's crew leave within a season; a captain who cannot remember the mate's name unsettles people. Sela runs the battered Gullwake nearly alone with Vesh, hauling salvage, keeping the book.
- **Age 23 (new, provisional until Bram's dossier settles it).** Brings the Gullwake to Keelbreak with a sprung keel and leaves with a shipwright who cooks: Bram Torvald, 26, out of Old Tam's yard.
- **Age 25 (new).** One short, loud Regatta-season affair with **Marisol Thane** of the Red Tally. It ends when Sela breaks a promise she wrote down and never read back. Marisol never forgave the book (Issue 20).
- **Age 27 to 28 (new).** Vesh screams at every new pylon. Sela starts plotting the pinned islands against old survey sheets, which is why a Pinhold courier is carrying survey sheets to her in Issue 1.

### How the bond works for her, and what it costs
Vesh feels the water pushing on everything in it; Sela gets what Vesh gets (Issue 11). Small Surges, a jet of deck water, a shove of current, cost nothing she notices. A Dive drowns one memory, whole, at random. She does not feel it go; she finds the hole later, when a page reads like a stranger's. The logbooks are not a quirk. They are a woman carrying her life in a trunk because her head is not a safe place to keep it.

### Three defining memories
1. **One she keeps (new).** Ledger ink drying on the back of her hand, age 17, her mother's voice: "If the sea takes it, the page keeps it." Artists: salt flats, a mother and a grown daughter, a serpent asleep in the shallows.
2. **One she will lose.** The forty-first time Mag Tolliver pulls her out of Sorrow Point harbour and says nothing, just hands her the wheel (Issue 11: "That one I don't need the book for"). In Issue 13 this year is gone. She can read it; she cannot feel it. She asks Bram who taught her the stew and nobody knows, because she never told them it was Mag.
3. **One she never had.** A deck, eight years ago, a man with a torn page in his hand. She has the tear and the warning above it. That is all she will ever have of it; the reader gets the rest in Issue 41, when he hums.

### What she wants, what she fears
She wants her crew east and her ship through, every time, and under that one thing she will not say aloud: to be a person who can be taken on trust from her own handwriting. She fears the day she reads the book and does not believe it, and she fears liking someone without knowing why (the pup problem). She does not fear the Dive. That is the trouble with her.

### How she talks
Dry, unhurried, warm, one step ahead, never loud until it matters, writes mid-conversation. Ten lines in voice:
1. "Write it down, pup. If it's worth shouting, it's worth a line."
2. "I don't get angry. I get specific."
3. "I'm not calm. I've read ahead."
4. "Yes, I've done it before. I've got it in writing, and the writing was very pleased with itself."
5. "Vesh. He isn't food. He only smells like it."
6. "I keep two lists: people I trust, and people the book says I trust. You're on the second one. It's the better list. I checked."
7. "If I come up and don't know you, feed me, lie to me gently, and hand me the pen."
8. "Bram, that isn't a vegetable. That's a threat."
9. "Hold that. No, the other end. There. Now you're an officer."
10. "Careful. I'm fond of you, and I haven't read why yet."

### Relationships, one line each
- **Kaito:** her pup; she teases him because the ears go red first and keeps him because he is the only thing on the sea more stubborn than the sea.
- **Juno:** the one bluff Sela cannot read, which she finds restful; they run the ship's two books, odds and log, side by side.
- **Silas:** two professionals of the unhurried sentence; she is the only person he has never got a rise out of, and he keeps trying on principle.
- **Bram:** keeper of the oilskin; he holds the logbook when she goes under, and he is the only one allowed to cry about it.
- **Ione:** Sela reads Ione's guilt like a current and never asks her to apologise, which Ione finds unbearable and then necessary.
- **Hekka Dorn:** a rival she likes too much to stop fighting; "owes me a drink" is written down, so it will be collected.
- **Marisol Thane:** old flame, loud ally; every argument is the same argument.

### Fighting style
A plain ship's cutlass off the rack by the wheel, used like a pilot's wheel: small moves, early. She turns off the hips, gets inside a longer reach and puts the point where the other person is going to be (Issue 12). Anything wet is a weapon, thrown as a hard jet, mark glowing teal. Vesh is the heavy hitter: coils, rams, a tow that drags a cutter sideways. The Dive is the last card; Bram holds out his hands for the book before she asks. Weakness: she must relearn tricks she has forgotten, so the current book has a page headed "Things I can do," reread before a fight **(new)**.

### Never
- Never raises her voice before it matters, and never for effect.
- Never says "I remember" about something she has only read.
- Never lies about the cost; she will decline to say it, never say it wrong.
- Never gets a memory back. Not one.
- Never tears a page, from her book or anyone's.
- Never lets the book get wet.
- Never calls Kaito "pup" to wound him.
- Never leaves the ship to save herself; she leaves it to save the ship.
- Never flirts with anyone frightened, hurt or captive, and never near a Pal.
- Never begs. She asks once, politely, and then she does it.

## 2. Vesh (deep-sea serpent-dragon, Tide)

**Species and look (CAST.md and the locked sheet):** a serpent-dragon as long as the Gullwake, dark blue scales, pale blue-grey belly, no legs, paired webbed side fins, two swept-back horns, fan-shaped jaw frills, glowing teal eyes, and one ridge of gold lantern lures on stalks from head to tail. The lures go out tail-to-head when she is hurt (Issue 12). Tows the ship by a jaw harness and towline.

**Where found (new):** the lantern-weed trench off Ninefathom, eleven years ago, the length of a rowing boat, starving. Her kind are born in drowned islands' roots and follow the Undersong up as they grow; she followed it too early.

**Temperament (new):** patient, vain about her lures, slow to anger and slower to forgive. Judges everyone by whether they hold still when told. Adores Bram. Tolerates Juno. Pretends Silas is not there. Kaito is a problem that will sort itself out.

**Three habits (new):**
1. Surfaces under the galley porthole at mealtimes and waits. Takes anything Bram offers except eel, as a point of principle.
2. Curls around the ship at night (Issue 14) and dims the lures one by one until only the one nearest Sela's cabin is lit.
3. When Sela has forgotten something, Vesh nudges the logbook pouch with her snout, which is how the crew learns it before Sela does.

**How she fights:** towing power first: she drags the Gullwake through a gap or a cutter off its heading. Water pressure blasts from the jaw stave in planks. Coils to crush, rams with the horns, a tail slap that empties a deck. In a Dive she takes Sela down on her neck and comes up through whatever is above them.

**How she shows affection:** lowers the frilled head to the rail so Sela can put her palm and then her forehead against the scales (Issue 12). Lights the lures head to tail in greeting **(new)**. Leaves one shed scale by the cabin door after each Dive; Sela keeps them in the trunk, fourteen so far **(new)**. Never in a flirting scene; she is in the cutaways as the disgusted witness, with Skarn.

## 3. Eight years ago: Corin Vale and Burr (exactly as the bible sets it)

**Corin Vale**, 29 now, 21 then; today he is **Silt**. A son of the **Cantors**, drift-singers of Cantor Isle who steered by the Undersong. The village had been emptying for a generation before he was born, because Empire charts made singers unnecessary; Mother Kell (71) is the last elder on it (Issue 34). **(new)** Corin left at 17 and shipped as a deckhand under no name; Cantors do not give their names outside the song, and Lund's crew called him "the Cantor" or "Mate." He was first mate when Sela came aboard at 19, and she is the only person he ever told his name. That is why "nobody knows his real name" is literal: the only person who knew it paid it away.

**(new)** How it happened: two navigators who found the Drift from opposite sides. She felt it through Vesh; he heard it. He taught her to hear the low hum under what Vesh gave her, the Undersong: the song in her warning. Cantors call the youngest voice in a song-crew "pup," the one who hears the tune but cannot yet hold it; he called her that while teaching her, and she stole the word and used it on him whenever he was solemn. Her name for him. She got it from him. The habit survived and the man didn't.

**Burr**, his partner: a Cantor song-hound, long-bodied, grey-brown, sail-like ears **(new look)**. The hound Silt rides is what is left of Burr **(new: the bible implies this, not states it)**.

**The Surge.** Sela was 20, captain for four months. **(new setting)** The Gullwake was caught in a down-draw where a drifting island's root passed over a drowned one, a whirlpool no ship had come out of. Sela and Vesh went under it, deeper than anyone ever had, and brought the sea back up under the hull, the same move she makes in Issue 12. The ship lived. The Surge drowned every memory of him.

**The deck.** He stood on the deck and she asked him who he was. He tore the page with his name out of her logbook. She had written a warning to herself first: "Ask him about the song. He will say you made it up. You did not." He had told her once that if she ever forgot, he would say it never happened, so it would not hurt her. Then he walked away. Vasch found him. His partner, the hound Burr, he stripped himself, full count of ten.

**Where it pays off.** Issue 5: her thumb on the torn edge. Issue 14: "Old tear." Issue 19: "Pup, left!" and Silt stops dead; that night he sits alone with a torn logbook page, face unseen. Issue 34: Kell: "You poor girl, you paid it all." Issue 41: "You made it up." Then he hums it, without meaning to, on the last panel. She still doesn't remember him. She never does.

**Art rule for this flashback.** Sela is 20 in it, so it is handled under rule 6 like a childhood flashback: plain, sad, rain on a deck, a torn page, no touch beyond a hand on a rail, no flirting or fan service on that page or the facing one. The romance is told, never shown.
